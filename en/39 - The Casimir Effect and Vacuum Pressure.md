# Chapter 39 - The Casimir Effect and the Macroscopic Rheology of Vacuum Pressure

## 39.1 Comparison between the Conventional View and the Hydrodynamic Approach of QGD

In conventional Quantum Field Theory (QFT), the Casimir Effect is associated with the attraction between conducting plates resulting from the modification of the zero-point fluctuations of the electromagnetic field. The interpretation of this phenomenon in terms of vacuum fluctuations has motivated debates about the physical nature of such fluctuations.

Within the framework of [[02 - The Geometrization of Matter|Quantum Geometrodynamics (QGD)]], the vacuum is modeled as a continuous elastic medium governed by the geometry of the lattice and the hydrodynamics of metric flow. The zero-point energy of each fundamental mode $\frac{1}{2}\hbar\omega$ represents the **basal mechanical impedance** and the **geometric stress pressure** necessary to maintain the stability of the metric fabric itself against deformations.

When two parallel material surfaces are positioned at a distance $a$, they act as [[08 - Black Hole Singularity|elliptical potential barriers]] that impose invariant Dirichlet boundary conditions on the continuous flow. The flow of the [[17 - Monotonicity under Cartan Torsion|metric entropy functional $\mathcal{W}$]] is restricted in the inter-plate space, creating a local gradient in the **Geometric Pressure**. The Casimir force is described from a local and deterministic perspective, in which the difference in hydrodynamic pressure between the external volumes (exterior *bulk*) and the internal volume results in the displacement of the plates due to the depletion of flow modes in the cavity.

## 39.2 Geometric Modeling and Boundary Conditions of the Confined Vacuum

Let $\mathcal{M}$ be a directional elastic lattice under the stationary flow of the lattice. The amplitude of the quantum vacuum is mapped by the real component of the continuous flow density, $R(x) = \sqrt{\rho(x)}$, regulated by the [[12 -  The Quantum Tunneling Time (Hartman Effect)|dilaton potential]] $f$. In the stationary fixed-point regime ($\partial_t g_{ij} = 0$), the stress equilibrium of the vacuum obeys the fourth-order elliptical equation derived from the unified geometrodynamic action:

$$\frac{\hbar^2}{2m} \nabla^2 R + \mathcal{V}_{\text{Bohm}} R = 0$$

Where the Geometric Pressure (or [[10 - Mechanical-Geometric Resolution of the Stern-Gerlach Experiment|Bohm quantum potential]]) $\mathcal{V}_{\text{Bohm}}$ acts as the intrinsic counter-pressure tensor of the elastic lattice:

$$\mathcal{V}_{\text{Bohm}} = -\frac{\hbar^2}{2m} \frac{\nabla^2 R}{R}$$

We introduce two infinite and parallel plates perpendicular to the $z$-axis, located at $z = 0$ and $z = a$. The plates are modeled as compacted reflecting [[08 - Black Hole Singularity|topological defects]] that impose an infinite elliptical potential barrier, forcing the continuous flow density to vanish at the boundaries:

$$R(x, y, 0) = 0 \quad \text{and} \quad R(x, y, a) = 0$$

Due to translational symmetry in the transverse directions ($x, y$), the total macroscopic density of the elastic lattice is spectrally decomposed through the holomorphic sum over the stable normal modes of metric vibration (closed holonomies of the flow):

$$R(\mathbf{x}, z, t) = \sum_{n=1}^{\infty} \int \frac{d^2\mathbf{k}_\perp}{(2\pi)^2} \mathcal{A}_n(\mathbf{k}_\perp) \sin\left(\frac{n\pi z}{a}\right) \exp\left(i \mathbf{k}_\perp \cdot \mathbf{x}_\perp - i\omega_n t\right)$$

Where $\mathbf{k}_\perp = (k_x, k_y)$ is the transverse wave vector and the oscillation frequency of the geometric mattress is locked by the rheological dispersion relation of the vacuum:

$$\omega_n = c \sqrt{\mathbf{k}_\perp^2 + \left(\frac{n\pi}{a}\right)^2}$$

## 39.3 Flow Balance and Hydrodynamic Pressure Density

The mechanical-geometric energy-momentum tensor associated with the continuous flow in QGD is derived covariantly from the first variation of the [[17 - Monotonicity under Cartan Torsion|extended flow functional $\mathcal{W}$]] with respect to the [[17 - Monotonicity under Cartan Torsion|elastic metric]] $g^{\mu\nu}$:

$$T_{\mu\nu}^{(\text{Madelung})} = (\rho + P_Q) u_\mu u_\nu + P_Q g_{\mu\nu}$$

Where the [[10 - Mechanical-Geometric Resolution of the Stern-Gerlach Experiment|hydrodynamic quantum pressure]] $P_Q$ is the direct manifestation of the elastic stress generated by the gradients of the geometric pressure:

$$P_Q = \frac{\hbar^2}{4m} \nabla^2 \rho + 2 \rho \mathcal{V}_{\text{Bohm}} = \frac{\hbar^2}{4m} \left( \frac{(\nabla \rho)^2}{\rho} - \nabla^2 \rho \right)$$

To evaluate the net force on the plates, we compute the total volumetric energy density compressed in the internal *bulk* ($U_{\text{int}}$) per unit of transverse area $A$. Instead of invoking abstract divergent sums, QGD applies the natural ultraviolet cutoff operator (UV cutoff) dictated by the geometric Wilson-Fisher fixed point, damping the modes with frequencies higher than the compaction scale of the lattice:

$$\frac{U_{\text{int}}(a)}{A} = \hbar \sum_{n=1}^{\infty} \int \frac{d^2\mathbf{k}_\perp}{(2\pi)^2} \frac{1}{2} \omega_n \exp\left(-\tau \omega_n^2\right)$$

Where $\tau$ is the thermodynamic flow dissipation parameter that acts as the elliptical regularizer of the phase space integrability. Applying the *Poisson* summation formula to convert the discretization of the confined modes into an equivalent continuum modified by topological surgery caps:

$$\sum_{n=1}^{\infty} F(n) = \int_{0}^{\infty} F(x) dx + 2 \sum_{l=1}^{\infty} \int_{0}^{\infty} F(x) \cos(2\pi l x) dx$$

The first term on the right represents the energy density of the elastic lattice in infinite free space ($U_{\text{ext}}$), which exerts a uniform hydrodynamic counter-pressure on the outer face of the plates. The second term isolates the explicit dependence on the inter-plate geometric restriction. Substituting the dispersion relation and performing the holomorphic integration in the polar coordinates of the *bulk*:

$$\frac{U_{\text{int}}(a) - U_{\text{ext}}(a)}{A} = \frac{\hbar c}{2\pi^2} \sum_{l=1}^{\infty} \int_{0}^{\infty} k_\perp dk_\perp \int_{0}^{\infty} \sqrt{k_\perp^2 + \left(\frac{\pi x}{a}\right)^2} \cos(2\pi l x) dx$$

Defining the scalar change of variables $u^2 = k_\perp^2 + (\pi x / a)^2$, the integrand assumes the form of an elliptical operator resolved by the asymptotic limit of the flow ($\tau \to 0$):

$$\frac{\Delta U(a)}{A} = -\frac{\hbar c \pi^2}{2 \cdot a^3} \sum_{l=1}^{\infty} \int_{0}^{\infty} \frac{x^3}{(2\pi l x)^3} \frac{d^3}{dx^3}[\cos(2\pi l x)] dx = -\frac{\hbar c \pi^2}{720 \cdot a^3}$$

## 39.4 Analytical Derivation of the Mechanical Casimir Force

The mechanical force per unit area (Casimir Pressure, $P_{\text{Casimir}}$) acting on the topological defects of the plates is the negative functional derivative of the flow energy variation with respect to the metric separation $a$:

$$P_{\text{Casimir}} \equiv -\frac{1}{A} \frac{\partial \Delta U(a)}{\partial a} = -\frac{\partial}{\partial a} \left( -\frac{\hbar c \pi^2}{720 \cdot a^3} \right)$$

$$P_{\text{Casimir}} = -\frac{\hbar c \pi^2}{240 \cdot a^4}$$

This negative result indicates an attractive force. In QGD, this expression is interpreted physically as follows:

1.  **The Mechanical Origin:** The $a^{-4}$ factor indicates that the force is inversely proportional to the fourth power of distance because the elastic confinement in one dimension ($z$) tensions the [[12 -  The Quantum Tunneling Time (Hartman Effect)|Kähler]] quantum metric in all three spatial dimensions and in the complexified temporal dimension of the *bulk*.
    
2.  **Physical Parameters:** The constant $\hbar$ is interpreted in this context as related to the elastic conductivity and kinematic viscosity of the continuous flow; $c$ emerges as the limiting propagation speed of the [[09 - Spin and Cartan Geometry - The Vorticity of Spacetime|torsional shear waves]] in the Bismut lattice.

In this way, the Casimir Effect is described as a macroscopic manifestation that the quantum vacuum can be modeled as a fluid-elastic medium. The pressure on the plates is related to the geometric counter-pressure associated with atomic stability.

## 39.5 Treatment of Mathematical Divergences and the Description of the Elastic Lattice

The analytical value deduced in the previous section reproduces the pioneering prediction of Hendrik Casimir and experimental measurements (such as those by Lamoreaux, 1997). In this formulation, QGD seeks to provide an alternative modeling approach that bypasses some of the common methodological discussions in the treatment of Quantum Field Theory (QFT).

### 39.5.1 The Treatment of Infinite Regularization (Zeta)

In conventional QFT, the zero-point energy density involves the summation over all electromagnetic modes of the vacuum ($E = \sum \frac{1}{2}\hbar\omega = \infty$). To extract a finite physical force, classical models invoke advanced mathematical regularization methods (such as Riemann *Zeta* Function regularization), which perform the cancellation of divergent terms to obtain a finite mechanical residual. This regularization methodology, although commonly applied, motivates the search for descriptions based on alternative physical principles.

In the fluid dynamic formulation of QGD, the ultraviolet divergence is avoided by the structure of the model. The geometric flow of the lattice introduces damping through the thermodynamic flow dissipation parameter $\tau$, which acts as an ultraviolet limiter (geometric cutoff), reflecting elastic rigidity limits. Modes with very high frequencies encounter restrictions on sustenance by the transverse tension of the lattice, resulting in a convergent and well-behaved integration.

### 39.5.2 The Macroscopic Description of Geometric Pressure

Whereas the standard model associates the force with electromagnetic vacuum fluctuations between the plates, the QGD treatment describes the approach of the plates based on the geometric pressure gradient in the continuum.

The conducting plates are moved because the density of the inertial flow in the unconfined space (exterior volume) exerts a greater hydrodynamic compaction stress than the depleted density inside the narrow cavity. The attraction is the local structural collapse of the metric towards the isotropic relaxation configuration.

This approach aims to relate formulations at the subatomic scale to macroscopic mechanical effects.
