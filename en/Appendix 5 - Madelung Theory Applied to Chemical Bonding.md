## Ap.5.1 Mechanics of the Covalent Bond and Phase Coupling

In conventional chemical bonding theory, the covalent bond (such as that of the Hydrogen molecule $H_2$) is frequently described by the sharing of electrons in molecular orbitals obtained by the linear combination of atomic orbitals (LCAO), where stabilization is quantified through the exchange integral.

In [[02 - The Geometrization of Matter|QGD]], electrons are represented by first-order [[26 - Proton - The Composite Ricci Soliton|Ricci solitons]] ($n=1$, vacuum [[08 - Black Hole Singularity|stomata]]). The covalent chemical bond is redefined as the stationary hydrodynamic coupling of the phase currents of the [[37 - The Double Slit Experiment|Madelung fluids]] belonging to two nearby stomata.

### Ap.5.1.1 The Molecular Hydrodynamic Equation

For a system of two atomic nuclei and two electronic stomata, the total quantum phase field of the molecule $\Phi_M = \sqrt{\rho_M} e^{i S_M / \hbar}$ solves the coupled Madelung equation under the influence of the atomic potentials:

$$\frac{\partial S_M}{\partial t} + \frac{|\nabla S_M|^2}{2m} + V_{\text{nuclei}} + \mathcal{V}_{\text{Bohm}} = 0$$

Where:
*   $\rho_M(\mathbf{r})$ is the distributed electronic vacuum volumetric density.
*   $\mathcal{V}_{\text{Bohm}} = -\frac{\hbar^2}{2m} \frac{\Delta \sqrt{\rho_M}}{\sqrt{\rho_M}}$ is the local [[10 - Mechanical-Geometric Resolution of the Stern-Gerlach Experiment|Bohm potential]].
*   $V_{\text{nuclei}}(\mathbf{r})$ is the classical electrostatic potential generated by the immersed atomic nuclei.

### Ap.5.1.2 Phase Matching Coupling

The stabilization of the molecular system requires that the velocity field of the [[12 -  The Quantum Tunneling Time (Hartman Effect)|Kähler]] fluid ($\mathbf{v} = \frac{\hbar}{m} \nabla S_M$) form a continuous and closed flow circuit around both atomic nuclei. This imposes the condition of **quantum phase matching** (Sommerfeld-Sudarshan coherence):

$$\oint_{\Gamma} \nabla S_M \cdot d\mathbf{x} = N h$$

Where $\Gamma$ is any closed curve circling both stomata.

When the two stomata approach the interatomic equilibrium distance ($R_{eq}$), their density clouds $\rho_1$ and $\rho_2$ overlap. The phase $S_M$ self-organizes to minimize the rotational kinetic energy. The phase gradient in the interatomic region is reduced due to the constructive alignment of velocities (cooperative flow):

$$\left| \nabla S_M \right|^2 < \left| \nabla S_1 \right|^2 + \left| \nabla S_2 \right|^2$$

This reduction in the phase shear stress decreases the total energy of the system, being associated with the chemical bond attraction. From this perspective, the covalent bond is related to the flow of the [[12 -  The Quantum Tunneling Time (Hartman Effect)|Kähler vacuum]] between the two [[08 - Black Hole Singularity|topological sinks]], which contributes to the stabilization of the molecule.

## Ap.5.2 Interatomic Metric Tension and Kähler Geometry

The presence of massive atomic nuclei and electronic stomata actively deforms the local [[12 -  The Quantum Tunneling Time (Hartman Effect)|Kähler metric]] $g_{ij}$ of the background spatial manifold. The chemical bond can alternatively be visualized as the stabilization of these deformations under the [[17 - Monotonicity under Cartan Torsion|Ricci-Perelman flow]].

### Ap.5.2.1 The Quantum Stress Tensor

We define the **Madelung Quantum Stress Tensor** $T_{ij}^{(Q)}$, which expresses the force per unit area exerted by the vacuum superfluid on the complex manifold:

$$T_{ij}^{(Q)} = \frac{\hbar^2}{4m} \left( \partial_i \partial_j \ln \rho_M - g_{ij} \Delta_K \ln \rho_M \right) \rho_M$$

This stress tensor couples directly to the Ricci curvature tensor in the field equations of [[02 - The Geometrization of Matter|QGD]]:

$$R_{ij} + \nabla_i \nabla_j f = \kappa T_{ij}^{(Q)} + R_{ij}^{\text{electrostatic}}$$

### Ap.5.2.2 The Geometric Connectivity Throat

Between the two atomic nuclei of a molecule, the modified [[17 - Monotonicity under Cartan Torsion|Ricci flow]] creates a [[08 - Black Hole Singularity|compressed metric throat (Kähler neck)]].
*   **At large distances ($R > R_{eq}$):** The two Bohmian potential wells are isolated. The vacuum curvature presents no constructive coupling, and the Kähler metric between them returns to a flat profile with no attraction.
*   **At the equilibrium distance ($R = R_{eq}$):** The Madelung stress tensor $T_{ij}^{(Q)}$ in the interatomic region develops a negative energy density (tensile stress). This tensile stress deforms the local metric, creating a hyperbolic geometric channel (Kähler metric bridge) that connects the two stomata.
*   **At very short distances ($R < R_{eq}$):** The excessive overlap of electronic density compresses the electronic stomata against the atomic nuclei. The density gradient converges towards the barycenter, and the local [[10 - Mechanical-Geometric Resolution of the Stern-Gerlach Experiment|Bohm potential]] $\mathcal{V}_{\text{Bohm}}$ assumes high values, acting as a quantum repulsion force that hinders the coalescence of the nuclei.

The molecular equilibrium geometry ($R_{eq}$) corresponds precisely to the metric saddle point where the attractive force generated by the Kähler bridge of the Madelung stress tensor balances exactly with the quantum repulsive force generated by the [[09 - Spin and Cartan Geometry - The Vorticity of Spacetime|Bohm-Cartan]] divergence at the nuclear centers:

$$\mathbf{F}_{\text{Kähler}} + \mathbf{F}_{\text{Bohm}} \equiv \mathbf{0}$$

The mechanical properties of molecules (such as force constants and bond lengths) are described by the geometrodynamics of the Kähler vacuum under the evolution of the Ricci-Perelman flow.
