### 38.1.1 System Ontology and Weak Field Simplification

To describe atomic stability, conventional quantum mechanics postulates the existence of non-radiating stationary states through the Schrödinger equation.

We fix this defect at the origin of the relativistic spherical coordinate system ($r=0$).

It has a localized metric volumetric density function, denoted by $\rho_{\text{electron}}(r) = R^2(r)$, with rest mass $m_e$ arising from the elastic self-energy of confinement.

The classical Coulomb interaction is described in this formalism as resulting from the asymptotic deformation profile of the [[17 - Monotonicity under Cartan Torsion|elastic vacuum]] metric generated by the asymmetry of the [[12 -  The Quantum Tunneling Time (Hartman Effect)|Kähler]] coupling. In the **weak field** limit (distal distances where $r \gg r_s$), the radial vacuum elastic relaxation function $f(r)$ is approximated linearly by:

$$f(r) \approx 1 - \frac{2r_s}{r}$$

Where $r_s$ is the characteristic deformation radius of the proton.

### 38.1.3 Frobenius Resolution and Sommerfeld-Dirac Spectrum

Where $\rho_0 = \frac{E\alpha}{\hbar c \gamma}$.

#### Critical Limits Analysis:

2.  **For $\rho \to 0$**: The centrifugal and torsional barrier imposes the indicial equation for the regularity of the topological knot:
    $$s(s+1) - [\ell(\ell+1) - 4\alpha^2] = 0 \implies s = -\frac{1}{2} + \sqrt{\left(\ell+\frac{1}{2}\right)^2 - 4\alpha^2}$$

$$a_{k+1} = \frac{k + s + 1 - \rho_0}{(k + 1)(k + 2s + 2)} a_k$$

$$\rho_0 = n_r + s + 1$$

$$\rho_0 = n - \left(\ell + \frac{1}{2}\right) + \sqrt{\left(\ell+\frac{1}{2}\right)^2 - 4\alpha^2}$$

Substituting $\rho_0$ back into its kinematic definition and isolating the temporal holonomy constant $E$, we obtain the energy spectrum of the weak field limit in QGD:

### 38.2.2 The Physical Effects of High Near Field Powers

## 38.3 Bidirectional Solitonic Coupling and Muonic Hydrogen

### 38.3.1 The Muon as a Near Field Probe

In **muonic hydrogen**, the genus $n=1$ electron is replaced by a muon (also an elementary genus $n=1$ soliton, but with mass $m_\mu \approx 207\,m_e$).

$$\gamma_\mu = \frac{\sqrt{m_\mu^2 c^4 - E^2}}{\hbar c} \gg \gamma_e$$

Since the quantum vacuum flow coordinate is given by $\rho = 2\gamma r$, the corresponding physical Bohr orbital radius shrinks by a factor of 207:

$$a_{0,\mu} \approx 2.56 \times 10^{-13} \text{ m}$$

Unlike the electron, which orbits in the weak field periphery, the muon soliton is forced to flow in the proximal vicinity of the proton knot ($n=3$), activating the higher-order terms ($\frac{\chi_3}{r^3}$, $\frac{\chi_4}{r^4}$ and the short-range viscous drag).

### 38.3.2 Reactive Ricci Flow and Mutual Soliton Deformation

In QGD, elastic knots do not behave as rigid bodies. The coupling between the muon and the proton is **bidirectional**. The rheological stress and torsion generated by the high-energy muon flow inject a reactive source term directly into the metric evolution equation of the proton itself, governed by the modified [[17 - Monotonicity under Cartan Torsion|Ricci flow]]:

$$\frac{\partial g_{ij}}{\partial \tau} = -2\left( R_{ij} + \nabla_i\nabla_j f \right) + \kappa_{\text{vac}} \left( \mathcal{T}_{\text{proton}} \cdot \mathcal{T}_{\text{muon}} \right)$$

Where the source $(\mathcal{T}_{\text{proton}} \cdot \mathcal{T}_{\text{muon}})$ represents the local contraction of the antisymmetric Cartan torsion densities of the two solitons in extreme proximity.

#### The Sign of the Torsional Contraction

Since the muon and the proton possess opposite topological orientations in the elastic lattice (representing opposite charges), their intrinsic Cartan vorticities are oriented antiparallel. The tensorial contraction of the product of their torsions is negative:

$$\kappa_{\text{vac}} \left( \mathcal{T}_{\text{proton}} \cdot \mathcal{T}_{\text{muon}} \right) < 0$$

### 38.3.3 The Resolution of the Proton Radius Anomaly

Experimentally, the measurement of the proton charge radius using muonic hydrogen yields a significantly smaller value ($\Delta r_p \approx -0.042 \text{ fm}$) than traditional measurements with electronic hydrogen or electron scattering.

In QGD, this discrepancy is analyzed by decoupling the characterization of the proton from an invariant static radius:
-   When the proton is coupled to the light electron, the elastic impedance generated by the electron in the periphery is negligible, and the proton maintains its free vacuum relaxation radius ($r_p \approx 0.88 \text{ fm}$).
-   When the proton is coupled to the heavy muon in an ultra-close orbit, the shear stress of the mutual torsion conformally contracts the metric of the hyperbolic throat. The hyperbolic throat associated with the proton experiences an elastic contraction during muonic coupling, presenting a smaller effective radius ($r_{p,\mu} \approx 0.84 \text{ fm}$).

In this way, the observed variation in the proton charge radius is described as resulting from the rheological stress associated with bidirectional solitonic coupling in the near field, in which the effective radius of the topological defect behaves as a dynamic property determined by the stress of the bidirectional solitonic coupling.
