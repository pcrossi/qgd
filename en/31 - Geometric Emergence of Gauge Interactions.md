## 31.1 Killing Vectors and Vacuum Symmetries

In the QGD formalism, the background spacetime is described by a three-dimensional complex Kähler manifold ($\mathcal{M}^3_{\mathbb{C}}$), which has 6 real dimensions. The Hermitian metric structure $g_{\alpha\bar{\beta}}$ and its corresponding Kähler 2-form $\omega = i g_{\alpha\bar{\beta}} dz^\alpha \wedge d\bar{z}^\beta$ determine the geometric dynamics.

### 31.1.1 Killing Vectors and Lie Algebra

The physical symmetries of the manifold correspond to its isometry vector fields. A complex vector field $\xi = \xi^\alpha \partial_\alpha + \bar{\xi}^\beta \partial_{\bar{\beta}}$ is a **Killing vector** if it preserves the Kähler metric, which is expressed by the nullity of the Lie derivative:

$$\mathcal{L}_\xi g_{\alpha\bar{\beta}} = 0 \implies \nabla_\alpha \xi_{\bar{\beta}} + \nabla_{\bar{\beta}} \xi_\alpha = 0$$

For a compactified manifold endowed with constant holomorphic sectional curvature, the Lie algebra generated by the complete set of holomorphic Killing vectors $\mathfrak{g} = \text{Isom}(\mathcal{M}^3_{\mathbb{C}})$ naturally decomposes into symmetry subalgebras.

### 31.1.2 The Origin of $SU(3) \times SU(2) \times U(1)$

1.  **The Chromodynamic Sector ($SU(3)$):** The special unitary symmetry group $SU(3)$ of rank 2 and dimension 8 emerges as the exact holomorphic isometry of the two-dimensional complex projective Kähler submanifold (the immersed complex projective plane $\mathbb{C}\mathrm{P}^2$):
    $$\text{Isom}(\mathbb{C}\mathrm{P}^2) = SU(3)/U(1) \cong SU(3)$$
    The 8 generators of the group (Gell-Mann matrices) correspond to the 8 independent Killing vectors that conserve the Fubini-Study metric in the internal phase space of the baryonic [[08 - Black Hole Singularity|soliton]]. The strong force is the geometric attraction generated by these Killing flows.
2.  **The Electroweak Sector ($SU(2) \times U(1)$):** The complex three-dimensional rotation group $SU(2)$ and the abelian phase $U(1)$ emerge from the isometry of the three-dimensional spherical boundary ($\partial \mathcal{M} = S^3$) under the action of the [[10 - Mechanical-Geometric Resolution of the Stern-Gerlach Experiment|Bohm quantum potential]]:
    $$\text{Isom}(S^3) \cong SU(2)_L \times SU(2)_R$$
    Where the chiral restriction of the [[03 - Complex Causality and the End of the Wick Paradox|Sudarshan]] flow selects the left-handed subalgebra $SU(2)_L$ and couples the longitudinal component $U(1)_Y$ generated by the global Kähler phase rotation.

In this way, the gauge bosons (gluons, $W^\pm$ bosons, $Z^0$, and the photon) are represented by the **elementary metric fluctuations** along the directions of these Killing vectors in the Kähler configuration space.

## 31.2 Electroweak Symmetry Breaking via Ricci Flow

In conventional particle physics, the electroweak symmetry breaking $SU(2)_L \times U(1)_Y \to U(1)_{EM}$ is described by the Higgs mechanism, starting from a potential associated with a non-zero vacuum expectation value ($v \approx 246 \text{ GeV}$).

In the QGD formalism, the symmetry breaking is modeled as a **geometric phase transition** associated with the Ricci-Perelman flow in the vicinity of the solitonic throats.

### 31.2.1 The Metric Deformation under the Flow

The modified Ricci flow acts as a non-linear diffusion equation for the metric:

$$\frac{\partial g_{ij}}{\partial \tau} = -2 R_{ij} + \nabla_i \nabla_j f$$

At large distances (asymptotic limit $r \to \infty$), the manifold approaches a flat and symmetric geometry, where the isometries of $SU(2)_L$ and $U(1)_Y$ are conserved globally. However, as we approach the center of a soliton (stoma), the strong localized curvature $R_{ij}$ and the gradient of the [[12 -  The Quantum Tunneling Time (Hartman Effect)|dilaton field]] $\nabla_i \nabla_j f$ break the isotropy of the configuration space.

### 31.2.2 Emergence of the Higgs Vector

The conformal fluctuation of the metric (the volumetric scale factor $\phi$) acts mathematically as the Higgs field. When the local curvature reaches a critical threshold dictated by the [[29 - The Fine Structure Constant|fine structure constant]], the Kähler metric undergoes a conformal collapse (metric strangulation). The basin of attraction of the Ricci flow forces the conformal factor to stabilize at a stable minimum stationary eigenvalue:

$$\langle \phi \rangle = v_K = \frac{M_e}{\alpha} \cdot \left( 1 - \frac{3}{4\pi^2} \right)^{-1/2} \approx 246 \text{ GeV}$$

This metric strangulation breaks the rotation symmetry of the spherical Killing space $S^3$, destroying the generators of $SU(2)_L$. The associated gauge bosons acquire inertial mass mechanically by the strangulation of their Killing directions. Only the abelian Killing direction $U(1)_{EM}$ (the photon) remains unhindered as it does not suffer from the conformal gradient, keeping its mass rigorously zero.

## 31.3 The Geometric Emergence of Chirality

The chiral asymmetry of weak interactions represents a central feature of the Standard Model: only left-handed (levogyrous) fermionic fields participate in the charged coupling of the $W^\pm$ bosons, while right-handed neutrinos are inert or considered non-existent.

In QGD, this parity violation is associated with the **intrinsic chirality of the Cartan torsion** in the complex Kähler manifold.

### 31.3.1 The Chiral Torsion Tensor

In the Kähler manifold $\mathcal{M}^3_{\mathbb{C}}$, the holomorphic ($z^\alpha$) and anti-holomorphic ($\bar{z}^\beta$) complex coordinates introduce a natural orientation structure. The totally antisymmetric Cartan torsion $B_{\mu\nu\lambda}$ couples to the quantum spin of the Madelung fluid.

The Cartan chiral spin operator describes the phase circulation around the stoma. Due to the complex orientation of the Kähler manifold, the contour integral of the complex 1-form $\omega$ along a closed holomorphic curve imposes a strict chirality restriction:

$$\oint_{\partial \mathcal{M}} \left( p_\alpha dz^\alpha + p_{\bar{\beta}} d\bar{z}^\beta \right) = n h$$

### 31.3.2 The Left-Handed Selection

During the evolution of the quantum fluid, the Sudarshan viscosity acts asymmetrically on the anti-holomorphic rotation modes. The quantum phase propagation equation shows that the opposite helicity modes (right-handed, dextrogyrous neutrinos) represent perturbations that do not close the Sommerfeld causal circuit.

Such configurations undergo rapid destructive interference and scatter to asymptotic infinity. The left-handed helicity mode is the one that reaches the variational stability point of the Perelman flow, offering a geometric representation for the left-handed nature of neutrinos in the weak interaction, associated with the complex orientation of the Kähler geometry.

### 31.4.2 Determination of the Constants

## 31.5 Mathematical Formalization via Killing Algebra in Kähler Manifolds

Let $M$ be a complex Kähler manifold with local metric given by the second derivatives of the Kähler potential $K$:

$$g_{a\bar{b}} = \partial_a \partial_{\bar{b}} K$$

The transformations that preserve the metric (isometries) are governed by the Killing equation for complex vector fields $\xi = \xi^a \partial_a + \xi^{\bar{a}} \partial_{\bar{a}}$:

$$\nabla_a \xi_b + \nabla_b \xi_a = 0 \quad \text{e} \quad \nabla_a \xi_{\bar{b}} + \nabla_{\bar{b}} \xi_a = 0$$

In a Calabi-Yau manifold stable under the Ricci flow (where the first Chern class vanishes, $c_1 = 0$), the restricted [[14 - The Sagnac Effect and Spacetime Torsion|holonomy]] group is explicitly $SU(3)$. According to Yau's theorem, the existence of a Ricci-flat metric guarantees that the moduli space of isometric deformation has exactly 8 independent Killing vectors, $\xi_A$ ($A = 1, \dots, 8$).

Each quantum generator $T_A$ of the $SU(3)$ gauge group (geometric equivalent to the Gell-Mann matrices) is expressed as the Killing momentum mapped from the Lie derivative of the Kähler potential:

$$T_A = i \xi_A^a \frac{\partial K}{\partial z^a} - i \xi_A^{\bar{a}} \frac{\partial K}{\partial z^{\bar{a}}}$$

The geometric commutation of these vectors rigorously closes the $SU(3)$ Lie algebra:

$$[\xi_A, \xi_B] = f_{ABC} \xi_C$$

Where $f_{ABC}$ are the structure constants of the strong interaction. Thus, the gauge connection forms $A_\mu^A$ (the gluons) naturally emerge from the mixed components of the Kaluza-Klein metric tensor.

We define the 8 generating curvature forms through the holomorphic Killing potentials $P_A$, such that $\partial_a P_A = i g_{a\bar{b}} \xi_A^{\bar{b}}$. The expansion of the differential commutator under the Kähler metric imposes:

$$\{P_A, P_B\}_{\text{Poisson}} = \xi_A^a \partial_a P_B - \xi_B^a \partial_a P_A = f_{ABC} P_C$$

This formulation establishes a direct correspondence between curvature oscillations and strong interactions, integrating hadronic dynamics with differential geometry.
