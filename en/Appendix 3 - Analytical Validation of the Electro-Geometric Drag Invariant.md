## Ap.3.5 Eigenvalue Stability under Gauge Flow

The stability of the eigenvalue $\lambda_1 = \frac{1}{4\pi^3}$ against metric perturbations $\delta g_{ij}$ at the stoma throat is analyzed under gauge transformations (diffeomorphisms).

### Ap.3.5.1 The Perturbed Laplace-Beltrami Operator

Let us consider the stable base metric $g^0_{ij}$ of the soliton at the saddle point of the [[17 - Monotonicity under Cartan Torsion|Perelman functional]]. The base Laplacian is $\Delta_0 = g^{ij}_0 \nabla_i \nabla_j$. Under an infinitesimal metric perturbation $\delta g_{ij} = h_{ij}$, the perturbed operator is written as:

$$\Delta_g = \Delta_0 - h^{ij} \nabla_i \nabla_j - \left( \nabla^i h_{ij} - \frac{1}{2}\nabla_j h^i_i \right) \nabla^j + \mathcal{O}(h^2)$$

By the perturbation theory of self-adjoint operators, the first-order variation of the first eigenvalue $\delta\lambda_1$ is given by sandwiching the perturbed operator with the normalized eigenfunction of the fundamental mode $\psi_1$:

$$\delta\lambda_1 = \langle \psi_1 | \delta(\Delta_g) | \psi_1 \rangle = \int_{\Omega} \psi_1 \left( -h^{ij} \nabla_i \nabla_j \psi_1 - \left[ \nabla^i h_{ij} - \frac{1}{2}\nabla_j h^i_i \right] \nabla^j \psi_1 \right) dV_{g^0}$$

### Ap.3.5.2 Stability under Diffeomorphisms (Gauge Symmetry)

At the stable saddle point of the Perelman flow, all stable metric fluctuations $h_{ij}$ are purely generated by coordinate diffeomorphisms (gauge transformations), expressed by the Lie derivative of the metric along a generating vector field $v^i$:

$$h_{ij} = \mathcal{L}_v g^0_{ij} = \nabla_i v_j + \nabla_j v_i$$

Substituting this form of $h_{ij}$ into the integral and integrating by successive parts over the closed domain $\Omega$ with Dirichlet boundary conditions ($\psi_1|_{\partial\Omega} = 0$), the boundary terms vanish. Contraction with the symmetry of the eigenfunction $\psi_1$ and the use of the base Helmholtz equation ($\Delta_0 \psi_1 = -\lambda_1 \psi_1$) lead to:

$$\delta\lambda_1 = -2 \int_{\Omega} \left( \nabla^i v^j + \nabla^j v^i \right) \nabla_i \psi_1 \nabla_j \psi_1 \, dV_{g^0} + \lambda_1 \int_{\Omega} \left( \nabla_i v^i \right) |\psi_1|^2 \, dV_{g^0}$$

Integrating the first term by parts:

$$-2 \int_{\Omega} \nabla^i v^j \nabla_i \psi_1 \nabla_j \psi_1 \, dV = 2 \int_{\Omega} v^j \nabla^i \left( \nabla_i \psi_1 \nabla_j \psi_1 \right) dV = 2 \int_{\Omega} v^j \left( \Delta_0 \psi_1 \nabla_j \psi_1 + \nabla^i \psi_1 \nabla_i \nabla_j \psi_1 \right) dV$$

Using the commutation of derivatives and the fact that $\Delta_0 \psi_1 = -\lambda_1 \psi_1$, this term exactly cancels the contribution of the trace of the divergence of $v^i$, resulting in:

$$\delta\lambda_1 \equiv 0$$

This demonstration suggests that perturbations in the Kähler lattice metric do not alter the fundamental eigenvalue $\lambda_1$ to first order, given that they correspond to gauge symmetries. From this perspective, the $2.4 \times 10^{-7}$ residue in the mass ratio is related to QED *loop* corrections.

$$\mathbf{F}_{\text{Bohm}} = - \nabla Q = \frac{\hbar^2}{2m} \nabla \left( \frac{\Delta_g \psi}{\psi} \right)$$

This analysis provides a geometric foundation for imposing the Dirichlet boundary condition ($\psi|_{\partial\Omega} = 0$) on the [[26 - Proton - The Composite Ricci Soliton|quantum soliton]].
