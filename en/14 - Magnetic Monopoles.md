## 14.1 The Magnetic Field as Torsional Vorticity

In classical physics and grand unified theories (GUTs), the electric charge is described as the radial source of the electrostatic field, commonly assuming that the magnetic field could possess an analogous source particle (the magnetic monopole) capable of emanating field lines radially.

$$\mathbf{B} \propto \boldsymbol{\Omega} = \nabla \times \mathbf{v},$$

A magnetic field is, by definition in the QGD formalism, a **whirlpool** in the [[12 -  The Quantum Tunneling Time (Hartman Effect)|Kähler fabric]].

## 14.2 The Topological Incompatibility of the Monopole

If magnetism is modeled as the quantum hydrodynamic vorticity of the fluid, the existence of an isolated magnetic monopole would be equivalent to postulating a particle that emanates radial circulation from a singular point singularity.

Mathematically and hydrodynamically, this configuration presents an integrability contradiction, since the divergence of a curl operator is identically null in any coordinate manifold:
$$\nabla \cdot (\nabla \times \mathbf{v}) = 0 \implies \nabla \cdot \mathbf{B} = 0$$

A vortex (whirlpool) necessarily requires an axis of rotation. The fluid descends on one side of the axis (South Pole) and rises on the other (North Pole). The dipole structure is not a union of two opposite magnetic charges; it is the **minimum and inevitable anatomy of any torsion in spacetime**. There is no hydrodynamic representation for circulation without an axis or without topological continuity.
