### Spurious Modes and the Cancellation of Gauge Ghosts

When poles associated with gauge transformations arise, one deals with the excitation of non-physical longitudinal and temporal modes. In the standard quantization of non-Abelian gauge theories, gauge redundancy is traditionally handled by introducing Faddeev-Popov ghost fields with unconventional spin-statistics (integer-spin Grassmann fields) to preserve unitarity and invariance under the BRST transformation.

In the QGD formalism, unitarity and the cancellation of non-physical longitudinal contributions are analyzed through alternative geometric and causal mechanisms:

**1. The Algebraic Cancellation of Gauge Poles (Sudarshan):** When a spurious longitudinal mode $A_L$ associated with the gauge is excited, its transition amplitude in a closed loop in the complex plane is calculated by the residue of the energy pole $k_0$. Since the fluid's effective physical propagator in the theory is the synchronous sum of advance and delay ($\Delta_{GDQ} = \frac{1}{2}[\Delta_{\text{ret}} + \Delta_{\text{adv}}]$), the contour integration is performed using Sudarshan's prescription.

For purely longitudinal modes (which do not couple to physical torsion or intrinsic angular momentum), the dispersion relation does not generate inertial mass in the Kähler metric. Consequently, the contributions of the advanced and retarded poles in the contour integration present opposite signs:

$$\oint_C \frac{k^\mu k^\nu}{(k^2 + i\epsilon)} \, dk_0 + \oint_C \frac{k^\mu k^\nu}{(k^2 - i\epsilon)} \, dk_0 = 0$$

The probability amplitude of the spurious longitudinal state generated by the pole annihilates algebraically in the causal integration circuit.

**2. The Relation to Cartan Torsion:** The diffeomorphism anomaly that commonly requires the introduction of ghosts is proportional to the volume of the gauge group's orbit. In the complex Kähler manifold, where the real dimension is $D=4$ (complex dimension $n=2$), gauge transformations are modeled with respect to local diffeomorphisms along the direction of the Cartan Torsion ($T^\lambda_{\mu\nu}$).

In this dimensionality, the contribution associated with the quantum diffeomorphism anomaly can mutually compensate with the degrees of freedom of the Cartan Torsion. The degrees of freedom associated with ghosts in conventional gauge formulations find geometric correspondence in QGD through the antisymmetric curvature (torsion) associated with the spacetime structure.

Thus, Sudarshan's symmetry and torsional coupling act jointly to maintain vacuum unitarity, so that non-physical excitations are mitigated through complex geometry.
