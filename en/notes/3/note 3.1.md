### Sudarshan's Works

**1. In Classical and Quantum Theories of Action-at-a-Distance** In the article _"ACTION-AT-A-DISTANCE"_, Sudarshan argues that, since action and reaction must be equal, if there is a retarded action, there must be (from the second particle's point of view) an advanced reaction. Therefore:
- He explicitly defines the symmetric kernel $\overline{\Delta} = \frac{1}{2}(\Delta_R + \Delta_A)$ (where $R$ is retarded and $A$ is advanced).
- He states that, in an action-at-a-distance theory, the "natural Green's function" is exactly this time-symmetric kernel (half advanced, half retarded).

**2. To Isolate "Shadow States" and Save Unitarity** The most important use of this function (in the quantum-mechanical form corresponding to $G_{sym}$) appears in the formulation of finite field theories using indefinite metrics. This is detailed both in _"ACTION-AT-A-DISTANCE"_ and in the articles _"Analyticity, Covariance, and Unitarity in Indefinite-Metric Quantum Field Theories"_ and _"INDEFINITE METRIC AND SHADOW STATES"_.

Sudarshan divides all states of the system into two classes: **physical states** (normal particles with real mass and positive norm) and **shadow states** (particles with complex mass or negative norm). The idea he applied with the Green's function is the following:

- **Different Propagators:** He chooses usual causal (retarded) propagators **only for the physical states**. For the **shadow states**, he stipulates the use of a "standing wave" propagator, which is exactly the **arithmetic mean of the advanced and retarded propagators**.
- **Mathematical Formula:** He defines this symmetric Green's function for the shadow states as $G^S = \frac{1}{2} \left[ \frac{1}{H^0 - E + i\varepsilon} + \frac{1}{H^0 - E - i\varepsilon} \right]$.
- **The Physical Consequence:** The purely time-symmetric Green's function produces a scattering amplitude that is **entirely real**. By forcing the shadow states to interact only through this symmetric Green's function, they contribute solely to the indirect dynamics (the real part), but **do not generate an imaginary part** in the transition amplitude.
- **Saving Probability:** Since only the imaginary part is related to probability conservation (via the Optical Theorem), the shadow states are magically excluded from the unitarity sum. This allows solving the major problem of indefinite metric theories: you can use mathematical "ghosts" to cancel the infinities (divergences) of field theory, but, thanks to the use of $G_{sym}$, these "ghosts" never materialize as real particles violating probability.

In summary, Sudarshan uses $G_{sym}$ ($G^S$) as a tool to allow non-physical states to affect the system's forces (dynamics), while ensuring they never violate the probability conservation laws of the physical world.
