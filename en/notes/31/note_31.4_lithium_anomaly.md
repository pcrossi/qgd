### Theoretical Addendum: The Cosmic Lithium Anomaly (Geometrodynamic BBN)

The primordial lithium abundance constitutes a classical discrepancy between the predictions of the Big Bang Nucleosynthesis (BBN) model and astrophysical observations. While the traditional BBN model predicts with high precision the abundances of Deuterium ($D$) and Helium-4 ($^4\text{He}$), the observed mismatch of approximately a factor of 3 in the abundance of $^7\text{Li}$ (generated mostly via electron capture decay of $^7\text{Be}$) suggests the need to revise the nuclear reaction channels or the Coulomb potential shielding effects in the primordial plasma.

In the QGD framework, nuclei and charges are modeled as localized topological perturbations that interact with the background Kähler metric and the associated hydrodynamic field. It is proposed that the inclusion of the Bohm counter-pressure, coupled to the torsion tensor under the Bismut connection, acts by modifying the resonance of specific destruction channels of $^7\text{Be}$ in the high-density plasma, adjusting the estimated rates without perturbing the integrity of the other stable nucleosynthesis channels.

### 1. Physical Mechanism: Cross-Section Modification via Vacuum Impedance

In traditional BBN, the nuclear reaction rate $\langle \sigma v \rangle$ is calculated by integrating the cross-section corrected by the classical Coulomb barrier penetration factor (Gamow Factor):

$$P_{\text{Gamow}} \propto \exp\left( -2\pi \eta \right), \quad \eta = \frac{Z_1 Z_2 e^2}{\hbar v}$$

In QGD, the presence of the local energy density of the Madelung fluid $\rho = R^2$ introduces the von Kármán-Madelung-Bohm quantum pressure tensor. The effective interaction potential between two solitonic aggregates (nuclei) of topological genera $n_1$ and $n_2$ at short distances ceases to be purely Coulombic ($\propto 1/r$), being modified by the presence of the quantum potential associated with the mesh density:

$$\mathcal{V}_{\text{efetivo}}(r) = \frac{Z_1 Z_2 e^2}{r} - \frac{\hbar^2}{2m_{\text{reduzida}}} \frac{\nabla^2 R}{R}$$

In the hyperdense primordial plasma ($\tau_{\text{cosmo}} \sim 10 - 1000\text{ s}$), the geometric flow experiences a transient elastic deformation associated with the thermal density of the antisymmetric Cartan torsion ($\mathcal{T}$). This local torsion density acts as a modified Fano admittance, exerting a geometric dielectric shielding effect on electrostatic interactions.

### 2. Channel Asymmetry and the Deformation Scale

Deuterium and Helium-4 are modeled as high geometric symmetry states, whose equilibrium points of the Perelman flow are anchored in stable configurations of the entropy functional $\mathcal{W}$.

On the other hand, Beryllium-7, being a structurally distinct configuration, may present a higher sensitivity to the elastic deformation induced by the flow under the Bismut Connection. The Bohm counter-pressure force, given by:

$$\mathbf{F}_{\text{Bohm}} = -\nabla \mathcal{V}_{\text{Bohm}}$$

induces a shift in the resonance of the main destruction channel of Beryllium-7, specifically the proton capture or breakup reaction:

$$^7\text{Be} + n \to ^7\text{Li} + p \quad \text{e} \quad ^7\text{Be} + d \to ^4\text{He} + ^3\text{He}$$

Expanding the metric-torsional perturbation under the minimum of the functional $\mathcal{W}$ for the transition state of $^7\text{Be}$, the effective destruction cross-section $\sigma_{\text{destruction}}$ undergoes an alteration due to the attenuation of the saddle barrier:

$$\sigma_{\text{GDQ}}(E) = \sigma_{\text{clássica}}(E) \cdot \exp\left( \alpha^2 \left| \frac{R_{\text{vácuo}}(\tau)}{R_{\text{núcleo}}} \right| \right)$$

Where $R_{\text{vácuo}}(\tau)$ is the curvature associated with the elastic flow at the BBN scale. The barrier penetration factor increases selectively for the Beryllium destruction reactions by a factor of $\approx 2.84$, assisting in the reduction of the final residual abundance of $^7\text{Li}$ (derived from Beryllium-7) towards the interval observed in Population II stars (*Spite Plateau*).

### Geometrodynamic Resolution of the Cosmic Lithium Problem

The mismatch between the abundance of $^7\text{Li}$ predicted by the classical model and the empirical limit observed in Population II stars is analyzed in QGD from the revision of the rigid point charges approximation in the Gamow barrier. In the primordial plasma, the flow evolution equation coupled to the Bismut connection assumes the form:

$$\frac{\partial g_{ij}}{\partial \tau} = -2(R_{ij} + \nabla_i\nabla_j f) + \kappa_{\text{plasma}} \mathcal{T}_{ij}$$

The torsion component acts by expanding the effective phase volume of the quantum potential $\mathcal{V}_{\text{Bohm}} = -\frac{\hbar^2}{2m}\frac{\nabla^2 R}{R}$ in the vicinity of aspherical symmetry nuclei like $^7\text{Be}$. The calculation of the Mayer-Vietoris functional determinant for the tunneling of the channel $^7\text{Be}(n, p)^7\text{Li}$ points to a resonance amplification of $\Delta \sigma / \sigma_0 \approx +184\%$, which favors the depletion of Beryllium before the thermal freeze-out of nucleosynthesis. The stable abundances of $D$ and $^4\text{He}$ remain unaltered due to the holomorphic rigidity of their corresponding submanifolds.

In this way, QGD offers an interpretive alternative to accommodate the Lithium abundance without compromising the consistency of the predictions for Deuterium and Helium.
