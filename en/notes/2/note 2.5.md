### Note on $\rho \propto u = \frac{e^{-f}}{(4\pi\tau)^{n/2}}$

The relation $\rho \propto u = \frac{e^{-f}}{(4\pi\tau)^{n/2}}$ constitutes the condition for the hydrodynamic conservation of matter and the geometric stability of the manifold to manifest in a unified way. To analyze how geometry acts to concentrate density and avoid singular collapse, we examine the balance between the geometric flow and the Bohm quantum potential in the ultraviolet limit.

**1. Concentration of Probabilistic Density (The Geometric Well):** In the formalism, the scalar field $f$ acts as the dilaton potential that describes the geometric response of the manifold. In regions where the action $S$ assumes high values, this potential assumes negative values to stabilize the system. Through the flow equation, this gradient induces a localized elliptic contraction in the metric $g_{ij}$, characterizing a topological depression (a *Shrinking Ricci Soliton*). Local spacetime modifies itself to cluster and contain the probability density $\rho$, acting as a barrier that limits diffusive dissipation.

**2. Behavior in the Ultraviolet Limit:** If the volumetric contraction were to proceed indefinitely, the characteristic width of the wave packet would tend to zero ($\sigma \to 0$). In this limit, the divergence rate of the geometric contractive term (governed by the Ricci Tensor $R_{ij}$) would scale inversely proportional to the area, at a rate of $\mathcal{O}(\sigma^{-2})$, which classically and locally would lead to the development of a metric singularity.

**3. The Repulsion Mechanism of the Bohm Potential:** The stabilization that obstructs the formation of the singularity stems from the spatial variation of the probability density at the core of the soliton. With density localization, the Laplacian of the amplitude ($\nabla^2 \sqrt{\rho}$) grows, generating the Bohm quantum potential, which acts as an internal counter-pressure:

$$Q = \mathcal{V}_{\text{Bohm}} = -\frac{\hbar^2}{2m} \frac{\nabla^2 \sqrt{\rho}}{\sqrt{\rho}}$$

For a wave packet in the limit of extreme localization ($\sigma \to 0$), this contribution scales at a rate of $\mathcal{O}(\sigma^{-5})$.

**4. Equilibrium in Metric Evolution:** In QGD, this repulsive pressure couples to the evolution of the vacuum metric:

$$\frac{\partial g_{ij}}{\partial t} = -2 \left( R_{ij} + \nabla_i \nabla_j f \right) + \kappa T_{ij}^{(\text{Bohm})}$$

When comparing the limit rates as $\sigma \to 0$, the stress associated with the Bohm potential ($\sim \sigma^{-5}$) dominates the Ricci contraction ($\sim \sigma^{-2}$). Consequently, the temporal derivative of the spatial metric exhibits a dominant positive contribution.

The radial component of the local metric ($g_{rr}$) expands in the face of the increased local energy density. The integration of the corresponding proper physical distance ($d = \int \sqrt{g_{rr}} dr$) indicates a formal divergence in the asymptotic limit.

Thus, the geometry of the manifold acts to contain the fluid distribution, establishing a dynamical equilibrium configuration in which the contractive Ricci effects and repulsive Bohm potential effects mutually compensate each other, avoiding singular collapse and favoring the formation of a stationary state.
