### The Geometric Noether's Theorem: Proof that Continuity is the Conserved Current of Phase Symmetry

Noether's Theorem establishes that for every continuous symmetry (invariance) in the Action of a physical system, there is a corresponding conservation law. In our theory, matter is governed by Perelman's complex field $f = -\frac{S_I - iS_R}{\hbar}$, where $S_R$ is the wave phase (Mechanical Action) and $S_I$ is the density potential (related to the statistical measure $\rho$).

Our objective is to prove that, if the universe is indifferent to a global change in the "angle" of this phase ($U(1)$ Symmetry), then the Madelung fluid is forced to be conserved along the Ricci-Kähler flow.

#### 1. The Structure of the Unified Lagrangian (Fluid Fraction)

We define the Lagrangian density of the quantum fluid coupled to the geometry as follows:
$$\mathcal{L} = \rho \sqrt{g} \left[ \frac{\partial S_R}{\partial \tau} + \tau t_0 \left( \frac{\hbar^2}{2m} \mathcal{R} + \frac{1}{2m} g^{\mu\bar{\nu}} \partial_\mu S_R \partial_{\bar{\nu}} S_R + \mathcal{V}_{\text{Bohm}} \right) \right]$$
Where:
- $\rho = e^{S_I/\hbar}$ is the probability density.
- $\sqrt{g}$ is the square root of the determinant of the Kähler metric (the volume of space).
- $\frac{\partial S_R}{\partial \tau}$ is the temporal evolution of the phase in the dimensionless flow scale $\tau$.
- $\frac{\hbar^2}{2m} \mathcal{R}$ is the coupling of the geometric curvature scalar to the quantum scale, converting it into a unit of energy density ($[\text{M}][\text{L}]^2[\text{T}]^{-2}$).
- $t_0 = \frac{2m L_0^2}{\hbar}$ is the characteristic diffusion time constant associated with a length scale $L_0$ of the stoma, converting the energy block into a unit of Action for compatibility with the phase derivative.
- The term with $g^{\mu\bar{\nu}}$ is the ballistic kinetic energy flowing through the metric lines.
- $\mathcal{V}_{\text{Bohm}}$ represents the Quantum Potential (the curvature terms generated by $S_I$).
#### 2. The Continuous Symmetry Transformation (The Phase "Spin")
Let us subject this universe to a mathematical transformation. We propose an infinitesimal and arbitrary displacement in the wave function's phase, denoted by a parameter $\alpha(z, \tau)$:
$$S_R \to S_R' = S_R + \alpha$$
The phase variation is, therefore, $\delta S_R = \alpha$. Since $\rho$ strictly depends on the imaginary part $S_I$ (amplitude), the density remains unaltered ($\delta \rho = 0$). The spacetime $\sqrt{g}$ also does not change initially. The Lagrangian is invariant under a constant phase rotation (global symmetry). However, to find the conserved Noether current, we let the parameter $\alpha$ vary locally in space and time and calculate the variation of the Lagrangian ($\delta \mathcal{L}$).
#### 3. The Noether Variational Calculus
Substituting the variation into our Lagrangian, we apply the chain rule only where derivatives of $S_R$ exist:
$$\delta \mathcal{L} = \frac{\partial \mathcal{L}}{\partial \left( \frac{\partial S_R}{\partial \tau} \right)} \delta \left( \frac{\partial S_R}{\partial \tau} \right) + \frac{\partial \mathcal{L}}{\partial \left( \partial_\mu S_R \right)} \delta \left( \partial_\mu S_R \right) + \frac{\partial \mathcal{L}}{\partial \left( \partial_{\bar{\nu}} S_R \right)} \delta \left( \partial_{\bar{\nu}} S_R \right)$$
Calculating the conjugate momenta for each term:

**I. The Temporal Momentum (The Density):**
$$P^\tau = \frac{\partial \mathcal{L}}{\partial (\partial_\tau S_R)} = \rho \sqrt{g}$$
**II. The Spatial Momentum (The Geometric Current):**
By differentiating the kinetic energy with respect to the spatial phase gradient, we obtain the holomorphic and anti-holomorphic conjugate momenta:
$$P^\mu = \frac{\partial \mathcal{L}}{\partial (\partial_\mu S_R)} = \tau t_0 \rho \sqrt{g} \left( \frac{1}{2m} g^{\mu\bar{\nu}} \partial_{\bar{\nu}} S_R \right)$$
$$P^{\bar{\nu}} = \frac{\partial \mathcal{L}}{\partial (\partial_{\bar{\nu}} S_R)} = \tau t_0 \rho \sqrt{g} \left( \frac{1}{2m} g^{\mu\bar{\nu}} \partial_\mu S_R \right)$$
Notice a hydrodynamic detail that arises here: the two momenta are Hermitian conjugates. When summing both contributions in the integration by parts ($\int [P^\mu \partial_\mu \alpha + P^{\bar{\nu}} \partial_{\bar{\nu}} \alpha] d^{2n}z$), the $1/2$ factor of the kinetic energy is cancelled by the sum of the two complex halves. The spatial divergence of the current is expressed in terms of the physical velocity field ($\mathbf{v}^\mu = \frac{1}{m} g^{\mu\bar{\nu}} \partial_{\bar{\nu}} S_R$) as:
$$\partial_\mu P^\mu + \partial_{\bar{\nu}} P^{\bar{\nu}} = \partial_\mu (\tau t_0 \rho \sqrt{g} \, \mathbf{v}^\mu)$$
#### 4. The Derivative Closure and the Conservation Proof
Substituting the calculated momenta back into the total variation of the Action ($\delta \mathcal{S} = \int \delta \mathcal{L} \, d\tau d^{2n}z = 0$):
$$\int \left[ (\rho \sqrt{g}) \frac{\partial \alpha}{\partial \tau} + (\tau t_0 \rho \sqrt{g} \mathbf{v}^\mu) \partial_\mu \alpha \right] d\tau d^{2n}z = 0$$
To isolate the arbitrary factor $\alpha$, we integrate the equation by parts. By doing this, the sign of the derivatives inverts and acts on our momenta, while the boundary terms vanish at infinity (or annihilate in Sudarshan's closed contour that we proved in Section 3):
$$-\int \alpha \left[ \frac{\partial}{\partial \tau} \left( \rho \sqrt{g} \right) + \partial_\mu \left( \tau t_0 \rho \sqrt{g} \, \mathbf{v}^\mu \right) \right] d\tau d^{2n}z = 0$$
Since the symmetry requires this integral to be zero for _any_ conceivable perturbation $\alpha$, the term contained within the brackets must be rigorously equal to zero.
Note that, since the metric $g$ evolves with the scale $\tau$ in the Ricci flow ($\partial_\tau \sqrt{g} = -\frac{1}{2} \mathcal{R} \sqrt{g}$), the temporal derivative $\frac{\partial}{\partial\tau}(\rho\sqrt{g})$ acts on the volume measure. However, by the very definition of Perelman's conjugate measure ($dm = \rho\sqrt{g}d^{2n}z$), the dilation density and the metric are dynamically coupled in such a way that the total probability measure is invariant along the flow. Simplifying the background metric under the covariant derivative, the volume variation is absorbed in the balance and the coordinate density satisfies the usual continuity equation:
$$\frac{\partial \rho}{\partial \tau} + \tau t_0 \nabla_\mu (\rho \, \mathbf{v}^\mu) = 0$$
