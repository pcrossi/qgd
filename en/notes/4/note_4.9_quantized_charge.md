### Theoretical Addendum: 4. Quantized Electric Charge and Grand Unification Theories (GUT)

In the standard formulation of the Standard Model, matter charge neutrality is ensured by tuning the weak hypercharges. Grand Unification Theories (GUTs) propose charge quantization by incorporating the gauge group into broader symmetries, which frequently predicts proton decay channels not yet observed experimentally.

In the scope of Quantum Geometrodynamics (QGD), elementary charge quantization is approached from the geometric structure of the complexified Kähler 2-form, emerging from Cauchy's integral residue formula applied to isolated polar singularities during the flow and stabilization of the manifold.

### 1. The Gauge Flux as a Complex Cauchy Residue Integral

In QGD, the complex extension of spacetime allows the description of gauge coordinates through a holomorphic transition structure. The gauge field intensity associated with electric charge is formalized by the projection of the holomorphic curvature form onto a closed contour $\gamma$ circling the soliton's geometric pole.

The total electric charge $Q$ associated with the Kähler vacuum is analyzed from the complex contour integration of the logarithmic derivative of the gauge transition function $\phi(z)$:

$$Q = \frac{1}{2\pi i} \oint_{\gamma} \frac{\phi'(z)}{\phi(z)} \, dz = \sum_{k} \text{Res}\left( \frac{\phi'}{\phi}, z_k \right) = N \in \mathbb{Z}$$

By **Cauchy's Residue Theorem**, the holomorphy of the mapping restricts the value of $Q$ to integer values ($N$), which define the elementary charge $e$.

### 2. Fractional Charge Structure and Proton Stability

The presence of fractional charges associated with quarks ($e/3, 2e/3$) in conventional quantum chromodynamics is analyzed in QGD under the hypothesis that such values represent local kinematic projections of a global integer residue.

In the QGD confinement model (Chapter 27), the proton is described as a stable solitonic structure whose elastic closure encloses a singularity with an invariant Cauchy residue $N = +1$.

When deep inelastic scattering (DIS) experiments probe the internal structure of the baryonic soliton through local flow currents, the foliation symmetry of the Kähler metric causes the flow to divide geometrically along the symmetry axes of the corresponding Clifford torus. The degrees of freedom associated with fractionally charged quarks can be understood as the partition of the integral along sub-contours in the complex manifold:

$$\oint_{\gamma_{\text{global}}} = \oint_{\gamma_1} + \oint_{\gamma_2} + \oint_{\gamma_3} = 1$$

Since the Cauchy integral over the total isolation contour $\gamma_{\text{global}}$ computes the sum of the residues ($\sum \text{Res} = 1$), the configuration's stability is structurally maintained. Under this prism, proton stability against decay is founded on the conservation of the Cauchy pole number as a topological invariant in the Alexandrov manifold, without the need to predict additional decay channels as in typical GUT models.
