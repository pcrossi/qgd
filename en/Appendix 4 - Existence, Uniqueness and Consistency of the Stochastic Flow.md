## Ap.4.1 The Stochastic DeTurck Flow

The classical Ricci flow equation is a weakly parabolic system of non-linear PDEs. Due to diffeomorphism invariance (coordinate gauge invariance), strict ellipticity is lost along the directions generated by Killing generators.

To restore strict parabolicity and prove the short-term existence of solutions, we apply DeTurck's trick: we modify the [[17 - Monotonicity under Cartan Torsion|Ricci flow]] by adding a transport term generated by the [[09 - Spin and Cartan Geometry - The Vorticity of Spacetime|DeTurck vector]] $W^i(g) = g^{jk} \left( \Gamma^i_{jk} - \hat{\Gamma}^i_{jk} \right)$ with respect to a smooth background metric $\hat{g}$.

### Ap.4.1.1 The Metric Stochastic Differential Equation (SDE)

In [[02 - The Geometrization of Matter|QGD]], the temporal evolution of the [[12 -  The Quantum Tunneling Time (Hartman Effect)|Kähler metric]] $g_{ij}$ under osmotic and thermal vacuum fluctuations incorporates a multiplicative Wiener stochastic noise term. The metric SDE of the Stochastic DeTurck Flow is expressed by:

$$dg_{ij} = -2 \left( R_{ij} + \nabla_{(i} W_{j)} \right) d\tau + \sigma\left(g_{ij}\right) dW_{ij}(\tau)$$

Where:
*   $\tau$ represents the flow time parameter.
*   $\nabla_{(i} W_{j)}$ is the DeTurck term that forces strict parabolicity.
*   $W_{ij}(\tau)$ is a standard Brownian Wiener matrix process in $Sym^2(T^*\mathcal{M})$.
*   $\sigma(g_{ij})$ represents the stochastic noise diffusion tensor that couples fluctuations to the local metric.

### Ap.4.1.2 The Short-Term Existence Theorem

**Theorem:** *Given a smooth and bounded initial Kähler metric $g_{0}$ over the compact manifold $\mathcal{M}$, there exists a critical time $\tau^* > 0$ such that the SDE of the stochastic DeTurck flow admits a unique strong solution $g(\tau)$ in the interval $\tau \in [0, \tau^*)$ almost certainly.*

**Proof Sketch:**
1.  **Strict Parabolicity:** The central differential operator of the deterministic part of the equation is:
    $$P(g)_{ij} = -2 \left( R_{ij} + \nabla_{(i} W_{j)} \right) = \Delta_g g_{ij} + \mathcal{Q}(g, \partial g)_{ij}$$
    Where $\Delta_g$ is the metric's Laplace-Beltrami operator, which is strictly elliptic. The addition of the DeTurck term eliminates the degeneracy along diffeomorphisms, making the deterministic equation strictly parabolic.
2.  **Lipschitz Condition:** The noise diffusion tensor $\sigma(g)$ satisfies the regularity conditions of local Lipschitz and uniform linear growth in the Banach space of metric sections $C^{2, \alpha}(\mathcal{M})$.
3.  **Infinite-Dimensional Itô Theorem:** Applying the semigroup formulation for stochastic differential equations in Hilbert spaces (Galerkin approximation), the strict deterministic parabolicity and the Lipschitz regularity of the noise guarantee uniform convergence and the short-term existence of stochastic trajectories almost certainly.

## Ap.4.2 The Maximum Principle and the Bohm Domain

Among the scenarios studied in geometric flows under Ricci flow is the formation of [[08 - Black Hole Singularity|collapse singularities]]. In the [[02 - The Geometrization of Matter|QGD]] formalism, modeling suggests that the [[10 - Mechanical-Geometric Resolution of the Stern-Gerlach Experiment|Bohm quantum potential ($\mathcal{V}_{\text{Bohm}}$)]] acts as a barrier that prevents the collapse of local volume.

### Ap.4.2.1 The Maximum Principle Applied to Density

The volumetric vacuum density $\rho = e^{-f}$ evolves coupled with the [[17 - Monotonicity under Cartan Torsion|metric]] according to the transport equation under the quantum potential:

$$\frac{\partial \rho}{\partial \tau} = \Delta_g \rho - \nabla_i \left( \rho \nabla^i f \right) + \mathcal{V}_{\text{Bohm}} \rho$$

Where the [[09 - Spin and Cartan Geometry - The Vorticity of Spacetime|Bohm-Cartan potential]] is expressed by:

$$\mathcal{V}_{\text{Bohm}} = 2\Delta_K (\ln \rho) - |\nabla (\ln \rho)|^2 + \frac{1}{4} B_{\mu\nu\lambda}B^{\mu\nu\lambda}$$

If the density $\rho$ approaches zero in a region of the lattice (an attempt to form a discontinuity or sink), the gradient and the Laplacian of the density logarithm diverge positively in an extreme way:

$$\lim_{\rho \to 0} \mathcal{V}_{\text{Bohm}} = +\infty$$

### Ap.4.2.2 Prevention of Collapse Singularities

Applying Hopf's **Parabolic Maximum Principle** to the density functional under the influence of the Bohmian potential:

$$\frac{d}{d\tau} \left( \rho_{\text{min}}(\tau) \right) \ge \left( \min_{\mathcal{M}} \mathcal{V}_{\text{Bohm}} \right) \rho_{\text{min}}(\tau)$$

Since $\mathcal{V}_{\text{Bohm}}$ assumes high values when the density decreases beyond the elastic threshold, the time derivative of $\rho_{\text{min}}$ becomes positive, favoring the return of the density to equilibrium. The Bohm barrier acts as a quantum pressure that prevents the occurrence of zero density in the *bulk*, reducing the probability of collapse into an isolated singularity in the [[08 - Black Hole Singularity|stomata]].

## Ap.4.3 The Generalized Perelman-Wiener Propagator ($\mathcal{G}$)

The statistical propagation of fluctuations and radiative corrections in the complex metric is described by the **Perelman-Wiener Propagator** ($\mathcal{G}$), which unifies the classical Feynman path integral with the Wiener stochastic diffusion integral over the Kähler space.

### Ap.4.3.1 Definition of the Propagator

The propagator $\mathcal{G}(z_1, z_2; \tau)$ represents the geometric phase transition amplitude between two complex Kähler coordinates under the temporal flow evolution $\tau$. It is the fundamental solution to the Schrödinger-Ricci equation coupled to the dilaton:

$$\left( \frac{\partial}{\partial \tau} - \Delta_K + f_0 \right) \mathcal{G}(z_1, z_2; \tau) = \delta(z_1 - z_2)$$

Where $\Delta_K = g^{\alpha\bar{\beta}} \partial_\alpha \partial_{\bar{\beta}}$ is the Kähler Laplacian and $f_0 = \ln(2\pi^2)$ is the dilatonic equilibrium potential.

### Ap.4.3.2 The Path Integral Formulation

Expressing the propagator in the stochastic path integral formulation (Wiener-Itô integral) under the Perelman invariant volume measure $d\mu = e^{-f} dV$:

$$\mathcal{G}(z_1, z_2; \tau) = \int_{\mathcal{C}[z_1, z_2]} \exp\left( - \frac{1}{2\nu} \int_0^\tau \left[ \left| \dot{z} \right|^2_g + R(z) + \mathcal{V}_{\text{Bohm}}(z) \right] d\tau' \right) \mathcal{D}_g z$$

This Wiener integral calculates the weighted sum over all continuous stochastic metric fluctuation paths. The Sudarshan viscosity factor ($\nu$) acts by regulating the integration measure $\mathcal{D}_g z$, converting the complex Feynman path integral into a strictly convergent Wiener integral under the complexified Euclidean plane.

## Ap.4.4 Uniqueness under the Sudarshan Elliptic Constraint

Although the strict parabolicity of the DeTurck flow guarantees short-term existence, the stability and uniqueness of long-term stationary solutions require the imposition of the Sommerfeld-Sudarshan elliptic boundary constraints.

### Ap.4.4.1 The Phase Closure Constraint

In the stationary limit ($\tau \to \infty$), the [[17 - Monotonicity under Cartan Torsion|Ricci-Perelman flow]] stabilizes into a stable shrinking soliton. For the resulting metric to be physically acceptable, the circulation of the phase currents around the stomata must satisfy the Sommerfeld topological quantization:

$$\oint_{\gamma_a} \omega = q_a h$$

This elliptic boundary constraint acts as a **global geometric gauge condition** that eliminates parasitic or chaotic solutions of the Ricci flow.

### Ap.4.4.2 Global Uniqueness Study

**Theorem:** *The set of stationary solutions of the stochastic DeTurck flow that satisfy the Sommerfeld phase closure constraint and have a normalized Kähler volume is unique for each topological index $n$.*

**Proof Sketch:**
Suppose the existence of two distinct stable metrics $g_1$ and $g_2$ that satisfy the same topological constraints. We define the geometric Wasserstein distance $\mathcal{D}(g_1, g_2)$ under the [[17 - Monotonicity under Cartan Torsion|Perelman entropy $\mathcal{W}$]].

The evolution of the distance under the modified Ricci-Perelman flow obeys the Perelman monotonicity inequality:

$$\frac{d}{d\tau} \mathcal{W}(g_1(\tau), g_2(\tau)) \ge C \cdot \int_{\mathcal{M}} \left| R_{ij}(g_1) - R_{ij}(g_2) \right|^2 e^{-f} dV$$

Since $\mathcal{W}$ is bounded from above and monotonically increasing, the flow forces the integrand to vanish asymptotically:

$$\lim_{\tau \to \infty} \left| R_{ij}(g_1) - R_{ij}(g_2) \right| = 0 \implies g_1 \equiv g_2$$

This result indicates the stability of the [[12 -  The Quantum Tunneling Time (Hartman Effect)|Kähler vacuum]] in the asymptotic limit under the Sudarshan elliptic constraint, supporting the formal consistency of the model.
