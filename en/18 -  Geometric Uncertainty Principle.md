## 18.4 Geometrization of Robertson-Schrödinger and Kähler Positivity

The Robertson-Schrödinger inequality generalizes the Heisenberg limit by explicitly incorporating the symmetric covariance between two self-adjoint operators:
$$\sigma_A^2 \sigma_B^2 \ge \left( \frac{\langle \{\hat{A}, \hat{B}\} \rangle}{2} - \langle \hat{A} \rangle \langle \hat{B} \rangle \right)^2 + \left( \frac{\langle [\hat{A}, \hat{B}] \rangle}{2i} \right)^2$$

$$h_{\alpha\bar{\beta}} = g_{\alpha\bar{\beta}} - i \omega_{\alpha\bar{\beta}}$$

Where $g_{\alpha\bar{\beta}} = \text{Re}(h_{\alpha\bar{\beta}})$ describes the local symmetric fluctuations and $\omega_{\alpha\bar{\beta}} = -\text{Im}(h_{\alpha\bar{\beta}})$ fixes the classical symplectic structure.    For a single vector ($u = v$), this provides the boundary variances $\sigma_A^2 = g(u, u)$.
2. **The Imaginary Component and Non-Commutativity:** The antisymmetric projection onto the Kähler form $\omega$ accounts for the commutator:

The physical requirement that the Hermitian metric $h$ be positive definite ($h(u, u) \ge 0$) and the application of the Cauchy-Schwarz inequality for the Hermitian form:
