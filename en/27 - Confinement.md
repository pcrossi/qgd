## 27.2 Theorem I: Confinement (Linear Growth of the Potential)

**Proposition:** Given two topological sources of vorticity (quarks) separated by a distance $r$ along a geodesic axis $z$, the energetic cost of separation $V(r)$ obeys the limit $\lim_{r \to \infty} V(r) = \infty$, presenting a strictly linear asymptotic behavior $V(r) = \sigma r$.

### Strict Mathematical Proof

**Step 1: Curvature Induction by Vorticity**  
The separation of two topological singularities of opposite vorticity along the $z$-axis deforms the surrounding Madelung fluid. The transversal vortical velocity field $\Omega_{\perp}$ induces shear stresses that manifest as a positive Ricci curvature in the directions perpendicular to the separation axis:
$$R_{\perp\perp} > 0$$

**Step 2: Transversal Dimensional Collapse (*Pinch-Off*)**  
The metric evolution in the perpendicular components $g_{\perp}$ is governed by the spatial component of the modified Ricci flow. In the absence of longitudinal Bohm perturbations on the sides, the dynamics are dominated by the transversal Ricci term:
$$\frac{\partial g_{\perp}}{\partial \tau} \approx -2 R_{\perp\perp} < 0$$

By the contraction theorem under positive sectional curvature, the perpendicular directions undergo a rapid exponential contraction. The process of metric collapse to zero is halted and stabilized by the short-range quantum repulsion generated by the transversal gradients of the Bohm Quantum Potential ($\nabla_{\perp} \nabla_{\perp} Q$).

This reconciliation of forces prevents the singularity and fixes the transversal space in a stable constant cross-sectional area $\mathcal{A}_0$. The original three-dimensional flow deforms into a **one-dimensional (1D) flow tube** of constant cross-section.

**Step 3: Integration of the Energy Functional and String Tension**  
The total potential energy of the system, $V(r)$, is the integral of the curvature energy density $\mathcal{E}$ over the entire perturbed volume of the manifold $\mathcal{V}$:
$$V(r) = \int_{\mathcal{V}} \mathcal{E} \sqrt{g} \, d^3x$$

Given the stable transversal dimensional collapse proven in Step 2, the three-dimensional integration measure simplifies to an integral along the separation axis $z$:
$$\sqrt{g} d^3x \longrightarrow \mathcal{A}_0 dz$$

The energy density $\mathcal{E}$ becomes a translational invariant along the flow tube between the sources ($z \in [0, r]$). Thus, the expression for the potential energy reduces to:
$$V(r) = \int_0^r \mathcal{E} \mathcal{A}_0 \, dz$$

Since the energy density $\mathcal{E}$ and the transversal area $\mathcal{A}_0$ are constant along the asymptotic profile of the flow tube, they can be extracted from the integral:
$$V(r) = (\mathcal{E} \mathcal{A}_0) \int_0^r dz = \sigma r$$

Where $\sigma$ is the **string tension** of the geometric flow tube, defined as $\sigma = \mathcal{E} \mathcal{A}_0 > 0$.  
Thus, the linear asymptotic behavior $V(r) = \sigma r$ is demonstrated. $\blacksquare$

## 27.4 The Geometric Origin of the *Mass Gap* ($\Delta$)

The Yang-Mills *Mass Gap* problem states that the lightest massive particle described by the theory must have a strictly positive mass ($\Delta > 0$). In the Standard Model, this is interpreted as the mass of the lightest *glueball* in the gauge spectrum.

In QGD, the *Mass Gap* ($\Delta$) possesses a purely mechanical and geometric interpretation: it is the **minimum transversal excitation energy** of the confined flow tube.

$$p_\perp \approx \hbar \sqrt{\frac{\pi}{\mathcal{A}_0}}$$

Therefore, the minimum quantum of inertial energy required to excite the tube's geometry — the physical equivalent to the *Mass Gap* — is given by:
$$\Delta = m_{\text{gap}} c^2 \approx \hbar c \sqrt{\frac{\pi}{\mathcal{A}_0}} > 0$$

Since the area $\mathcal{A}_0$ is strictly finite due to Bohm repulsion, the minimum energy $\Delta$ is rigorously greater than zero. This result points to the existence of a geometric mass gap in the QGD vacuum, establishing a correspondence with hadronic phenomenology.

## 27.6 The Strong Coupling Constant $\alpha_s$ as Kähler Impedance Ratio

Unlike Quantum Chromodynamics (QCD), where the strong coupling constant $\alpha_s$ is treated as a free empirical parameter that runs with the energy scale via the renormalization group, in Quantum Geometrodynamics it emerges as a rigid topological eigenvalue of the Kähler manifold $\mathcal{M}_{\mathbb{C}}$. $$\psi(\theta) = \phi_0(\theta) + \lambda \int_{0}^{2\pi} K(\theta, \theta') \psi(\theta') d\theta'$$

Where:
*   $\phi_0(\theta) = \frac{n}{4\pi}$ represents the topological source term determined by the number of stomata ($n=3$ for baryons) uniformly distributed on the three-dimensional spherical boundary of the purified vacuum.
*   $K(\theta, \theta') = \mathbf{I}$ is the symmetric chiral coupling kernel (phase transmission in the complex plane).
*   $\lambda = -\frac{1}{2\pi}$ is the [[03 - Complex Causality and the End of the Wick Paradox|Sudarshan]] parameter associated with the unitarity of the retrocausal temporal closure.

By discretizing the integral over a periodic angular mesh of $N$ points with trapezoidal weights ($W = \text{diag}(d\theta)$ where $\sum d\theta = 2\pi$), the matrix operator simplifies to:
$$(\mathbf{I} - \lambda \mathbf{K} \mathbf{W}) \mathbf{\psi} = \mathbf{\phi}_0 \implies \left( \mathbf{I} - \left(-\frac{1}{2\pi}\right) \mathbf{I} (2\pi \mathbf{I}) \right) \mathbf{\psi} = \mathbf{\phi}_0 \implies 2 \mathbf{\psi} = \mathbf{\phi}_0$$
