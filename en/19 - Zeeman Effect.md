## 19.2 Mathematical Equivalence of the 1-Form and 2-Form Couplings

In the manuscript, the magnetic coupling is expressed in two representations: the local coupling of the 1-form potential (hydrodynamic current representation) and the tensorial contraction of the 2-form (spin-torsion representation). ## 19.8 The Stark Effect as Conformal Metric Polarization

Unlike the magnetic field, a uniform external electric field $\mathbf{E} = (0, 0, E_z)$ introduces a scalar electrostatic potential $\Phi(z) = -E_z z$ (or $A_0 = -E_z z$).

$$f \to f + \frac{e}{\hbar c} \Phi$$

$$H_{\text{Stark}} = -e \mathbf{E} \cdot \mathbf{r}$$

### The Linear Stark Effect

For the ground state of the isolated electronic soliton, the spatial parity of the density $\rho_0(r) = e^{-f_0(r)}$ is strictly symmetric (even function). The integral of the linear electric dipole moment over the symmetric manifold is zero:
$$\langle d \rangle = \int_{\mathcal{M}} e \mathbf{r} \rho_0(r) dV = 0$$

Therefore, the linear Stark Effect is zero for the electronic soliton in the ground state, preserving the stability of the spherical/toroidal symmetry.

### The Quadratic Stark Effect

When the external electric field $\mathbf{E}$ is applied, the potential gradient asymmetrically deforms the conformal factor $\phi$ of the local metric. The geometric flow of the soliton undergoes an elastic shear, displacing the dilaton density $f$ in the direction opposite to the field force. This displacement polarizes the Ricci soliton.

$$\mathcal{W}(g, f, \mathbf{E}) = \mathcal{W}_0 - \frac{1}{2} \alpha_e \mathbf{E}^2$$

Where $\alpha_e$ is the **geometric polarizability** of the soliton, determined by the elastic rigidity of the Perelman flow against Weyl deformation. The second-order energy shift (Quadratic Stark Effect) is:
$$\Delta E_{\text{Stark}} = -\frac{1}{2} \alpha_e \mathbf{E}^2$$

The Stark Effect is, therefore, the expression of the thermodynamic stress suffered by the Kähler metric, which alters its local proper energy to accommodate the conformal polarization imposed by the external EFD (Electric Force Distribution).
