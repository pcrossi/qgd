# 21.1. The strong CP problem as seen by GDQ

The strong CP problem is simple to state.

In the effective color sector, there is a topological density:

$$
q_C
=
\frac{1}{8\pi^2}
\operatorname{Tr}(F_C\wedge F_C).
$$

Its integral defines a charge:

$$
Q_C
=
\int_N q_C.
$$

For admissible bundle configurations:

$$
Q_C\in\mathbb Z.
$$

> ![[notes/topology/cp_periodicity_integer_charge]]

For this reason, the energy of the sector depends on a periodic angle:

$$
\theta\sim\theta+2\pi.
$$

In a usual operational formulation, if the physical minimum is not at $\theta=0$, CP-violating effects appear, such as a neutron electric dipole moment. Experimentally, this moment is extremely small. Therefore, the physical question is:

$$
\text{why does the observed strong sector relax to }
\theta_{\rm eff}\simeq0?
$$

## 1. GDQ Answer

GDQ does not introduce a new fundamental field to solve this. The angle that compensates for $\theta$ is a torsional mode:

$$
\vartheta_B\sim\vartheta_B+2\pi.
$$

The physical combination is:

$$
\theta_{\rm eff}
=
\theta_0+\vartheta_B.
$$

If we want to use effective scalar field language, we write:

$$
a
=
f_B\vartheta_B,
$$

but $a$ is only the canonically normalized variable of the torsional mode. It is not postulated as an elementary particle.

## 2. What Must Be Proven

The chapter needs to demonstrate:

1. why the potential is periodic;
2. why $\theta_{\rm eff}=0$ is a minimum;
3. why the flow goes to this minimum;
4. how to compare with the experimental limit of the EDM;
5. what still depends on canonical normalization.
