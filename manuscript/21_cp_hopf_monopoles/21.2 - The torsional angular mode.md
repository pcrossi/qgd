# 21.2. The torsional angular mode

In GDQ, the Bismut/Cartan torsion is part of the admissible geometry of the Hermitian bulk. Let us denote the effective torsional form by $B$. In a four-dimensional readout of the strong sector, the dual of a 3-form is an axial 1-form:

$$
\star B
\in
\Omega^1(N).
$$

The topological component of this 1-form defines an angular variable:

$$
\vartheta_B
\sim
\vartheta_B+2\pi.
$$

The physical point is that $\vartheta_B$ is not an arbitrary scalar. It measures a torsional orientation/circulation of the geometric background.

## 1. Effective Canonical Variable

When the angular mode is rewritten as a dimensional variable:

$$
a
=
f_B\vartheta_B,
$$

the periodicity becomes:

$$
a
\sim
a+2\pi f_B.
$$

The coefficient $f_B$ is defined by the effective kinetic term:

$$
S_{\rm tor}^{(2)}
=
\frac12
\int
f_B^2
(\partial\vartheta_B)^2
d{\rm vol}.
$$

Therefore:

$$
\boxed{
f_B^2
=
\text{kinetic rigidity of the torsional angle}.
}
$$

This definition is more important than the number itself. The number only becomes metrological when the canonical normalization of the torsional mode is calculated from the Hessian of the official action.

## 2. Physical Interpretation

The torsional angular mode is the capacity of the geometric vacuum to rotate its internal topological phase to cancel a CP tilt.

There is no need to postulate a new fundamental global symmetry. Periodicity already comes from the fact that the mode is angular.
