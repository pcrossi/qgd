# 21.8. Hopf, Cauchy and half-monodromy

The Hopf fibration is:

$$
S^1
\hookrightarrow
S^3
\to
S^2
\simeq
\mathbb{CP}^1.
$$

Locally, the normal slice of the stoma is compatible with:

$$
S^3
\subset
\mathbb C^2.
$$

We write:

$$
u\in S^3\simeq SU(2),
\qquad
P=uu^\dagger\in\mathbb{CP}^1.
$$

Since:

$$
u\sim -u
$$

represents the same projective physical direction, the double cover appears:

$$
SU(2)\to SO(3).
$$

This gives:

$$
2\pi\mapsto -1,
\qquad
4\pi\mapsto +1.
$$

## 1. Local Cauchy Form

On a punctured disk normal to the stoma, use a complex coordinate $z$. A local spinorial section has the form:

$$
s(z)
=
z^{1/2}s_0(z),
$$

with $s_0$ holomorphic and non-zero.

The logarithmic form is:

$$
\Omega_S
=
d\log s
=
\frac12\frac{dz}{z}
+d\log s_0.
$$

Since $d\log s_0$ has no residue:

$$
\operatorname{Res}_{z=0}\Omega_S
=
\frac12.
$$

By the residue theorem:

$$
\frac{1}{2\pi i}
\oint_\gamma
\Omega_S
=
\frac12.
$$

## 2. Physical Circulation

The circulation of the real action is:

$$
\oint_\gamma dS_R
=
h\frac12
=
\frac h2
=
\pi\hbar.
$$

Therefore:

$$
\operatorname{Hol}_\gamma
=
\exp
\left(
\frac{i}{\hbar}
\right.
\oint_\gamma dS_R
\left.
\right)
=
-1.
$$

This result shows that the half-circulation is not a fit. It is the topological residue of the spinorial/Hopf class of the normal boundary.
