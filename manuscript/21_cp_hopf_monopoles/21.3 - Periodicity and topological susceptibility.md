# 21.3. Periodicity and topological susceptibility

The energy of the topological sector must respect:

$$
E(\theta)
=
E(\theta+2\pi).
$$

The minimal form compatible with periodicity and stability is:

$$
V_{\rm CP}(\theta_{\rm eff})
=
\chi_{\rm top}^{\rm GDQ}
\left(
1-\cos\theta_{\rm eff}
\right).
$$

For small angles:

$$
V_{\rm CP}
=
\frac12
\chi_{\rm top}^{\rm GDQ}
\theta_{\rm eff}^2
+O(\theta_{\rm eff}^4).
$$

Therefore, the quadratic form is only a local approximation near the minimum. The global potential is periodic.

## 1. Topological Susceptibility

The rigidity of the vacuum against changes in $\theta$ is:

$$
\chi_{\rm top}^{\rm GDQ}
=
\left.
\frac{\partial^2E_{\rm vac}(\theta)}
{\partial\theta^2}
\right|_{\theta=0}.
$$

> ![[notes/topology/hessian_cp_susceptibility]]

Equivalently:

$$
\chi_{\rm top}^{\rm GDQ}
=
\int d^4x\,
\langle q_C(x)q_C(0)\rangle_{\rm GDQ}.
$$

If the torsional mode has a propagating pole, its effective scale satisfies:

$$
m_B^2f_B^2
=
\chi_{\rm top}^{\rm GDQ}.
$$

If it does not have a propagating pole, this relationship defines a relaxation scale, not an asymptotic particle.

## 2. External Comparison Used

For optional numerical comparison, the strong topological susceptibility scale is frequently parameterized by:

$$
\chi_{\rm top}^{1/4}
\approx
75.46\,{\rm MeV}.
$$

It does not enter as a GDQ axiom. It is used only to estimate the effective mass that would be read if the torsional mode were treated as an axion-like excitation.
