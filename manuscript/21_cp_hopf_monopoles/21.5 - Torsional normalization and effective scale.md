# 21.5. Torsional normalization and effective scale

The geometric proposal used in this chapter is that the torsional rigidity of the baryonic soliton is controlled by the Kähler volume:

$$
V_K
=
6\pi^5.
$$

The suggested effective scale is:

$$
f_B
=
M_{\rm Pl}^{\rm red}
\sqrt{
\frac{3}{\sqrt{6\pi^5}}
}.
$$

With:

$$
M_{\rm Pl}^{\rm red}
=
2.435\times10^{18}\,{\rm GeV},
$$

one obtains:

$$
f_B
\simeq
6.442945\times10^{17}\,{\rm GeV}.
$$

## 1. Classification

This value must be read with precision:

$$
\boxed{
\text{proposed geometric derivation for the torsional rigidity, conditioned on the full canonical normalization.}
}
$$

In other words, it is not a guess, but it should not be presented as final metrology either, until the kinetic coefficient is extracted directly from the physical Hessian:

$$
K_{\rm tor}^{\rm phys}
=
P_{\rm phys}
\delta^2\mathcal S_{\rm GDQ}[\Phi_\ast]
P_{\rm phys}.
$$

## 2. Effective Mass if There Is a Pole

If the mode is propagating:

$$
m_B
=
\frac{\sqrt{\chi_{\rm top}}}{f_B}.
$$

Using $\chi_{\rm top}^{1/4}=75.46\,{\rm MeV}$ only as an external comparison, the effective value is on the order of:

$$
m_B
\sim
8.8379\times10^{-12}\,{\rm eV}.
$$

This is much smaller than the micro-eV scale associated with $f_a\sim10^{12}\,{\rm GeV}$ in usual axion-like models. The difference comes from the fact that $f_B$ is close to the reduced Planck scale.
