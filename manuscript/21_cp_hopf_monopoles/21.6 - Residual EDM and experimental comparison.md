# 21.6. Residual EDM and experimental comparison

The neutron electric dipole moment is the direct experimental test of the CP residue.

For small angles:

$$
d_n
\simeq
C_n\theta_{\rm residual}\,e\,{\rm cm}.
$$

We will use as a phenomenological comparison coefficient:

$$
C_n
\simeq
3.8\times10^{-16}.
$$

The current experimental limit used as a comparison is:

$$
|d_n|
<
1.8\times10^{-26}\,e\,{\rm cm}.
$$

This requires:

$$
|\theta_{\rm residual}|
<
\frac{1.8\times10^{-26}}{3.8\times10^{-16}}
\simeq
4.74\times10^{-11}.
$$

## 1. Conservative GDQ Prediction

The Lyapunov relaxation provides:

$$
\theta(\tau)
\to
0\pmod{2\pi}.
$$

Therefore, in a sufficiently long relaxation time:

$$
|d_n|
\to
0.
$$

The conservative statement is not "the EDM is exactly zero under any condition". The correct statement is:

$$
\boxed{
\text{the residual EDM is exponentially suppressed by the torsional flow.}
}
$$

To declare $d_n=0$ exactly, it would be necessary to prove the complete absence of:

1. boundary residue;
2. finite volume;
3. stationary noise;
4. boundary misalignment;
5. remaining propagating mode.
