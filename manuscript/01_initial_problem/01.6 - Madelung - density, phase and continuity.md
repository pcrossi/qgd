# Madelung — density, phase and continuity

Wick rotation highlights the relationship between damping and phase, but it does not show how both pieces of information coexist in the same state. Hydrodynamic decomposition makes this coexistence explicit.

## One complex variable, two real equations

In a region where the wave function does not vanish, we write:

$$
\psi=\sqrt\rho\,e^{iS/\hbar}.
$$

This writing does not add degrees of freedom: it swaps the real and imaginary parts of $\psi$ for a positive density $\rho$ and a real phase $S$. Upon substituting it into the Schrödinger equation, we obtain two equations.

The imaginary part yields:

$$
\partial_t\rho
+\nabla\cdot(\rho v)=0,
\qquad
v=\frac{\nabla S}{m}.
$$

The real part yields:

$$
\partial_tS
+\frac{|\nabla S|^2}{2m}
+V+Q[\rho]=0,
$$

where

$$
Q[\rho]
=-\frac{\hbar^2}{2m}
\frac{\Delta\sqrt\rho}{\sqrt\rho}.
$$

Madelung introduced this hydrodynamic representation for quantum theory^[[[../ref/Madelung 1927 - Quantentheorie in hydrodynamischer Form|Madelung 1927, p. 322–326]]].

## The meaning of the conjugate

In complex writing, $\bar\psi$ allows the formation of the density:

$$
\rho=\bar\psi\psi
$$

and the conserved current. In the isolated Hamilton–Jacobi writing, the function $S$ determines the kinematics of the phase, but it does not determine the transported distribution. Therefore, the hydrodynamic formulation has an additional continuity equation.

This point explains why the adjoint has operational meaning in the quantum formulation: the bilinear structure builds the norm and current. The continuity equation is not redundant information; it is the evolution of the measure that accompanies the phase.

## The quantum potential is not always a repulsive force

$Q[\rho]$ measures a combination of derivatives of the amplitude. Its sign depends on the profile of $\rho$, and the force $-\nabla Q$ can point in different directions. In certain localized profiles or close to zeros of the density, it can form a barrier. This property must be demonstrated for the considered profile.

Therefore, the secure interpretation is: $Q$ is an internal term dependent on the geometry of the density, and not an external potential added to the dynamics.

## What is still missing

The decomposition is an exact identity of the Schrödinger equation where $\rho>0$. It does not resolve the irregularity of Wiener trajectories: the field $v$ can be regular even when almost no individual path has instant velocity. To understand this difference, we need directional mean derivatives.
