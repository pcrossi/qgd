# Universal diffusion and geometric inertia

In the Nelson reduction that reproduces non-relativistic quantum mechanics, the diffusion coefficient is:

$$
\nu=\frac{\hbar}{2m}.
$$

This formula works operationally, but it raises a constructive question: if diffusion belongs to the geometric medium, why should its fundamental coefficient know the mass of each excitation beforehand?

## Hypothesis of a universal scale

The proposal of GDQ is to investigate a reference diffusion:

$$
\nu_0=\frac{\hbar}{2m_0}
$$

and represent the local inertial response by a positive geometric factor:

$$
\Omega=\frac{m}{m_0}.
$$

If the local reduction produces:

$$
\nu_{\rm eff}=\nu_0\Omega^{-1},
$$

then

$$
\nu_{\rm eff}=\frac{\hbar}{2m}.
$$

This calculation demonstrates the compatibility of the hypothesis with Nelson's coefficient. It does not demonstrate that $\Omega$ emerges from the official action, nor that $m_0$ is uniquely selected by the vacuum.

## The correction absent in the original argument

When $\Omega$ varies in space, the diffusion coefficient also varies. In this case, it is not enough to replace a constant with a function in Nelson's formula: the noise becomes multiplicative and the Fokker–Planck equation receives the corresponding Itô terms.

On the spatial sheet with metric $h_{ij}$, the diffusion tensor is:

$$
D^{ij}=\nu_0\Omega^{-1}h^{ij},
$$

and the forward process can be written as:

$$
dX_t^i
=b_+^i(X_t,t)\,dt
+\sigma^i{}_a(X_t,t)\,dW_t^a,
\qquad
\sigma^i{}_a\sigma^j{}_a=2D^{ij}.
$$

The density satisfies exactly:

$$
\partial_t\rho
=-\nabla_i(b_+^i\rho)
+\nabla_i\nabla_j(D^{ij}\rho).
$$

In the isotropic case, this becomes:

$$
\partial_t\rho
=-\nabla_i(b_+^i\rho)
+\nu_0\Delta_h(\Omega^{-1}\rho),
$$

or, in expanded form,

$$
\begin{aligned}
\partial_t\rho={}&-\nabla_i(b_+^i\rho)
+\nu_0\Omega^{-1}\Delta_h\rho\\
&+2\nu_0\nabla^i\Omega^{-1}\nabla_i\rho
+\nu_0\rho\,\Delta_h\Omega^{-1}.
\end{aligned}
$$

The compatible forward and backward evolutions yield the osmotic velocity:

$$
u^i
=\nu_0\Omega^{-1}
\left(\nabla^i\ln\rho-\nabla^i\ln\Omega\right).
$$

Therefore, the terms with $\nabla\Omega$ are not optional corrections. They disappear only in the homogeneous sector, when $\Omega$ is constant, and then:

$$
u^i=\frac{\hbar}{2m}\nabla^i\ln\rho.
$$

To close the hypothesis, GDQ must show that the weighted operator derived from $g$, $f$, and $\mathcal U$ produces exactly the geometric drift and the necessary connection terms. Only in this case can the apparent dependence of diffusion on mass be interpreted as a scaling response of the background itself.

## Status of the proposal

The logical status, after the complete stochastic derivation, is:

1. the Itô and Fokker–Planck equations with $D^{ij}=\nu_0\Omega^{-1}h^{ij}$ are demonstrated;
2. for constant $\Omega$, we recover exactly $\nu_{\rm eff}=\hbar/(2m)$;
3. $\Omega=m/m_0$ is an operational definition in the stochastic sector;
4. its identification with a specific functional of $g$, $f$, the contour, and the topology of the soliton still belongs to the geometric construction of masses;
5. no experimental mass is used here to declare this last step proven.

This distinction preserves the original idea without turning a promising parameterization into a premature conclusion.

The role of this section is, therefore, to prepare a question for the geometrization of matter: if mass is a response of the background, then the factor $\Omega$ must not be chosen after knowing the particle. It must appear as a consequence of an admissible geometric solution, its constraints, and its boundary conditions. This point can only be addressed after the geometric domain and the official action are defined.
