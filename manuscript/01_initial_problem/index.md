# 01. The initial problem — The divergence between Feynman and Wiener path integrals

This chapter isolates the question that gave rise to Quantum Geometrodynamics. Wiener and Feynman path integrals are both described as integrals over paths, but they assign mathematical objects of different natures to the paths: a positive probability measure and a complex oscillatory amplitude. The goal is to determine exactly where this divergence lies, to what extent Wick rotation relates them, and what still needs to be explained by a physical construction.

The route of the chapter is progressive. First, we establish the methodological motivation and vocabulary. Next, we separate Wiener, Feynman, and Wick. Then, we show why boundary, gauge, density, phase, and non-differentiability are not technical details. The chapter ends by formulating the central requirement that the subsequent chapters must answer: a geometric theory needs to contain, within the same framework, positive diffusion, oscillatory phase, causal boundary, and physical reconstruction.
