# Wick rotation and analytical continuation

The similarity between Feynman and Wiener weights suggests replacing real time with an imaginary variable. This operation is useful, but its meaning must be delimited before employing it as a physical bridge.

## From the unitary group to the semigroup

If $H$ is self-adjoint, the formal physical evolution is:

$$
U(t)=e^{-itH/\hbar}.
$$

For real time, $U(t)$ forms a unitary group. If the spectrum of $H$ is bounded below and we write:

$$
t=-i\tau,
\qquad
\tau>0,
$$

we obtain:

$$
U(-i\tau)=e^{-\tau H/\hbar}.
$$

This operator is a contractive semigroup once the origin of energy is properly chosen. In coordinate representation, its kernel can admit an expression by Wiener functionals under hypotheses on the potential.

The mathematical sense of the transition is not "changing a letter." First, one defines an analytical function of the complex parameter in a domain where the operator is controlled; then, their boundary values are compared.

## The role of the spectrum

If

$$
H\phi_n=E_n\phi_n,
$$

then

$$
e^{-itH/\hbar}\phi_n
=e^{-itE_n/\hbar}\phi_n,
$$

while

$$
e^{-\tau H/\hbar}\phi_n
=e^{-\tau E_n/\hbar}\phi_n.
$$

In the first case, each mode undergoes a phase rotation. In the second, higher energy modes are more strongly damped. The continuation relates two functions of the same operator, but it does not identify their physical interpretations.

## Why the continuation can fail

The rotation requires controlling:

1. the domain of $H$;
2. the position of poles, cuts, and continuous spectrum;
3. the initial and boundary conditions;
4. the asymptotic behavior;
5. the positivity necessary for reconstruction;
6. the boundary terms produced by the action;
7. the causal prescription used in returning to physical time.

Wick formulated the rotation as a contour deformation in the complex plane, with attention to the singularities of the integrand^[[[../ref/Wick 1954 - Properties of Bethe-Salpeter Wave Functions|Wick 1954, p. 1124–1129]]]. The Euclidean reconstruction requires additional conditions, subsequently formalized by Osterwalder and Schrader^[[[../ref/Osterwalder and Schrader 1973 - Axioms for Euclidean Green Functions|Osterwalder–Schrader 1973, p. 83–112]]].

## Result of this stage

Analytical continuation explains why oscillatory propagation and diffusion can be represented by related functions of the same operator. It does not explain by itself why positive measure, phase, causality, and boundary terms belong to a single physical dynamics.

The first concrete obstacle appears when two actions differ by a total derivative. This is the subject of the next section.
