# 16.8. Relation to the leptonic hierarchy

Chapter 15 provides the charged leptonic backgrounds:

$$
\Phi_e,\qquad \Phi_\mu,\qquad \Phi_\tau.
$$

This is necessary for $g-2$, but it is not sufficient. The mass hierarchy does not replace the magnetic response.

The correct chain is:

$$
\Phi_\ell
\to
H_{C,\ell}
\to
c_\ell
\to
m_{\perp,\ell
}
\to
a_\ell.
$$

An important reduced test showed that using only a diagonal susceptibility proportional to $1/R_\ell$ fails for the muon. This prevents an undue simplification: the mass of the lepton provides the background stiffness, but the anomaly depends on the transverse magnetic channel.

Therefore:

$$
\boxed{
\text{the leptonic hierarchy provides the background; it does not replace }H_{C,\ell}^{+}m_{\perp,\ell}.
}
$$

This separation prevents confusing the leptonic hierarchy with the magnetic response.

In the preserved effective blocks, the upper stiffnesses used as a diagnostic are:

| lepton | $M_\ell/M_e$ | effective $K_2$ | result |
|---|---:|---:|---|
| electron | $1$ | $8.6102257658\times10^2$ | leading term |
| muon | $206.7685934706$ | $1.7803242711\times10^5$ | leading term |
| tau | $3477.4464050984$ | $2.9941598636\times10^6$ | leading term |

The extraction confirms that the minimal effective backgrounds reproduce $\alpha/(2\pi)$ for all leptons, but do not by themselves generate the metrological residuals. This is a positive organizational result: the hierarchy enters in the correct place, as a stiffness background, and not as a substitute for the magnetic operator.
