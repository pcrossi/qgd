# 16.6. The factor $\alpha$ over $2\pi$

The first transverse term of the magnetic response comes from the phase cycle. In the $S^1$ cycle, the normalized harmonic 1-form is:

$$
h
=
\frac{d\vartheta}{2\pi},
\qquad
\int_{S^1}h=1.
$$

Its norm is:

$$
\langle h,h\rangle
=
\int_0^{2\pi}
\frac{d\vartheta}{(2\pi)^2}
=
\frac{1}{2\pi}.
$$

Multiplying by the elementary electrogeometric intensity:

$$
a_{\rm geom}^{(1)}
=
\alpha\langle h,h\rangle
=
\frac{\alpha}{2\pi}.
$$

Therefore:

$$
g_{\rm GDQ}^{(1)}
=
2
\left(
1+\frac{\alpha}{2\pi}
\right).
$$

With $\alpha^{-1}=137.035999177$:

$$
a^{(1)}
\simeq
1.161409732097665\times10^{-3},
$$

$$
g^{(1)}
\simeq
2.002322819464196.
$$

This is the leading closure. The term is not called a fundamental loop. It is the first transverse response of the projected physical Hessian.
