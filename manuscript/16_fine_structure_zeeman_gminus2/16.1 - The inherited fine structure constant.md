# 16.1. The inherited fine structure constant

The fine structure constant is not inserted in this chapter as an experimental number. It is the electrogeometric normalization of the $U(1)_Q$ channel inherited from the global-local bridge.

The conceptual point is simple. The laboratory measures the relative intensity of the electrical response, but this intensity is not chosen locally in each experiment. It comes from the global normalization of the primitive electromagnetic mode, transported to the local chart when the photonic channel does not escape laterally and preserves its symplectic current.

In QGD, the electrical sector is obtained by restricting the physical Hessian of the official action to the horizontal mode generated by a primitive direction of the torus. In the Einstein cosmological space, this normalization is written as:

$$
Z_Q^E
=
v^T
\left(
Z_{QQ}
-
Z_{Q\perp}Z_{\perp\perp}^{-1}Z_{\perp Q}
\right)
v.
$$

The second term is the Schur complement: it represents the linear response of the geometric modes that are not directly observed, but which stiffen or soften the effective impedance of the electrical channel. The relation to the fine structure constant, in natural units, is:

$$
\alpha_E
=
\frac{1}{4\pi Z_Q^E}.
$$

In the Einstein isotropic ensemble, the mean Hessian becomes scalar in the physical subspace of four directions. The projective contraction of the $U(1)_Q$ channel yields:

$$
\mathcal P_{\rm iso}
=
\frac{9}{8\pi^4}.
$$

The mean physical compliance is the geometric mean of the four response eigenvalues:

$$

=
\left(
\frac{\pi^5}{1920}
\right)^{1/4}.
$$

Thus, the current result for the Einstein cosmological mean is:

$$
\alpha_E^{\rm mean}
=
\frac{9}{8\pi^4}
\left(
\frac{\pi^5}{1920}
\right)^{1/4}.
$$

Numerically:

$$
\left(\alpha_E^{\rm mean}\right)^{-1}
\simeq
137.036082448.
$$

The role of the global-local bridge is to transport the normalization of the electromagnetic channel to the laboratory chart:

$$
Z_Q^{\rm lab}=Z_Q^E,
\qquad
\alpha_{\rm lab}=\alpha_E.
$$

The chapter uses this normalization as an already inherited datum. The hypothesis that remains visible is the isotropic ensemble class of the Einstein cosmological space.

Compared to the recorded metrological value $\alpha_{\rm ref}^{-1}\simeq 137.035999$, the geometric mean provides a relative deviation of the order of $6.1\times10^{-7}$ in $\alpha^{-1}$. This comparison does not participate in the derivation; it merely shows the metrological scale achieved by the geometric normalization.

Physical meaning: $\alpha$ measures the effective electrogeometric ratio of the charge channel. The laboratory does not reinvent this number locally; it inherits the normalization of the global space by compatible transport.
