# 16.4. Zeeman effect by Noether and isotropy

The stationarity of the linear energy:

$$
E(C,B)
=
E_0(C^2)-\gamma_{\rm eff}C\cdot B
$$

imposes:

$$
C\times B=0.
$$

Therefore, the stable orientations are parallel and antiparallel to the field:

$$
C_\pm
=
\pm
\frac{\hbar}{2}
\frac{B}{|B|}.
$$

The energies are:

$$
E_\pm
=
E_0
\mp
\gamma_{\rm eff}
\frac{\hbar}{2}
|B|.
$$

If the field is non-uniform, the gradient produces opposing forces:

$$
F_\pm
=
\pm
\gamma_{\rm eff}
\frac{\hbar}{2}
\nabla |B|.
$$

In the case $B=B_z\hat z$, the reduced tensorial form is:

$$
S^{\alpha\beta}F_{\alpha\beta}
=
2S_zB_z.
$$

Thus:

$$
\Delta E_\pm
=
\mp
\frac12
g_{\rm geom}
\mu_BB_z.
$$

The Zeeman effect is, therefore, the experimental reading of a local geometric contraction between circulation and magnetic source.
