# 16.9. Comparison, limits and metrological program

Using $\alpha^{-1}=137.035999177$, the leading term yields:

$$
a^{(1)}
=
\frac{\alpha}{2\pi}
\simeq
1.161409732097665\times10^{-3},
$$

$$
g^{(1)}
\simeq
2.002322819464196.
$$

For the electron, the recorded comparison is:

$$
g_e^{\rm exp}
\simeq
2.002319304361180.
$$

Therefore, the leading term is higher by:

$$
g^{(1)}-g_e^{\rm exp}
\simeq
3.5151030\times10^{-6}.
$$

In anomaly:

$$
a_e^{\rm exp}-a^{(1)}
\simeq
-1.7575515\times10^{-6}.
$$

For the muon:

$$
a_\mu^{\rm exp}-a^{(1)}
\simeq
4.5108579\times10^{-6}.
$$

Organizing the comparison in absolute and relative error:

| observable | leading QGD value | measured value used | absolute error | relative error |
|---|---:|---:|---:|---:|
| $g_e$ | $2.002322819464196$ | $2.002319304361180$ | $3.5151030\times10^{-6}$ | $1.7555\times10^{-6}$ |
| $a_e$ | $1.1614097321\times10^{-3}$ | $1.1596521806\times10^{-3}$ | $1.7575515\times10^{-6}$ | $1.5156\times10^{-3}$ |
| $a_\mu$ | $1.1614097321\times10^{-3}$ | $1.1659205900\times10^{-3}$ | $4.5108579\times10^{-6}$ | $3.8689\times10^{-3}$ |

In percentage, this corresponds approximately to:

| observable | percent error |
|---|---:|
| $g_e$ | $0.0001755\%$ |
| $a_e$ | $0.1516\%$ |
| $a_\mu$ | $0.3869\%$ |

These residuals are not failures of structural closure. They measure the size of the upper channels not yet evaluated:

$$
H_{C,\ell}^{+}m_{\perp,\ell}.
$$

The closure analysis excluded two incorrect routes:

1. one must not choose $\mu_2$ based on the experimental target, because the same anomaly can be reconstructed by infinitely many triples $(J_2,K_2,\mu_2)$;
2. one must not postulate a universal direct upper source in a uniform field, because the Hodge rule gives $\mu_{2,\ell}^{\rm direct}=0$.

The remaining route is specific:

$$
\Delta H_{12}
=
\eta_\ell T_{123},
\qquad
T_{123}\simeq -2\pi.
$$

In the normalized reduced angular saddle, the only root found was:

$$
\eta_\ell\simeq0,
$$

with a negative eigenvalue in the reduced Hessian. Therefore, the upper metrological correction requires the physical 8D leptonic saddle, not the homogeneous angular truncation.

The chapter closes:

1. structural Zeeman;
2. $g=2$;
3. leading term $\alpha/(2\pi)$;
4. QGD operator that defines the anomaly.

The chapter does not close:

1. complete metrological $g_e$;
2. complete $g_\mu-2$;
3. non-homogeneous/warped or mixed 8D leptonic saddle;
4. contraction of the upper tensors of the official action on this saddle.
