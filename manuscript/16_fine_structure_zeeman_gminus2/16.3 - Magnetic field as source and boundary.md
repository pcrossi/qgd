# 16.3. Magnetic field as source and boundary

The magnetic field of the experiment is a classical datum of the apparatus. It is not a new fundamental field added to the official action. In the weak regime, its function is to deform the background via an external source:

$$
J_{\rm app}^{\rm classical}
\to
\delta\Phi_{\rm app}.
$$

The response is calculated by the physical Hessian of QGD:

$$
K_{\rm phys}
=
P_{\rm phys}^{\dagger}
\left.
\delta^2\mathcal S_{\rm GDQ}
\right|_{\Phi_*}
P_{\rm phys}.
$$

The projector $P_{\rm phys}$ removes gauge modes, zero modes, variations that change the Cauchy charge, and variations incompatible with the boundary of the apparatus.

The effective impedance seen by the apparatus is a Schur reduction:

$$
\mathsf R_{\rm app}
=
K_{pp}-K_{pI}K_{II}^{-1}K_{Ip},
$$

where $p$ denotes degrees of freedom monitored by the apparatus and $I$ internal degrees of freedom eliminated.

In the linear limit, the coupling allowed by isotropy has the form:

$$
E(C,B)
=
E_0(C^2)
-
\gamma_{\rm eff}C\cdot B
+O(B^2).
$$

Physical meaning: the magnetic field does not create the spin. It selects an axis and measures the boundary response of the already existing circulation.
