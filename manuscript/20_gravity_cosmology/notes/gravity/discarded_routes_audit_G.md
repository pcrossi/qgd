# Discarded routes in the derivation of $G$

This note preserves important negative results. They prevent the manuscript from mixing GDQ with external structures again or from declaring a numerical coincidence as a theorem.

## 1. Simple meromorphic warp

A warp depending only on the flow parameter does not generate external spatial curvature in the homogeneous background. The equations reduce to:

$$
A'(z_\tau)=0.
$$

Furthermore, a fractional power such as:

$$
(z_\tau-z_\ast)^{5/6}
$$

has a branch point, but no Laurent residue. Therefore, it does not, on its own, provide the causal residue that would define $C_R$.

## 2. BPST/Yang-Mills instanton

The official action of GDQ contains the Bismut curvature linearly in the geometric sector:

$$
\mathcal R_B.
$$

It does not contain as a fundamental term:

$$
\operatorname{tr}(\mathcal F_B\wedge\star\mathcal F_B).
$$

Therefore, a BPST/Yang-Mills action cannot be imported as an official derivation of $G$. It can serve as an analogy or external audit, but not as a foundation of GDQ.

## 3. Isolated thermal determinant

For:

$$
Z=\operatorname{Tr}e^{-\beta H},
\qquad
\Gamma=-\log Z,
$$

we have:

$$
\partial_\beta\Gamma=\langle H\rangle,
\qquad
\partial_\beta^2\Gamma=-\operatorname{Var}(H)\le0.
$$

Thus, the isolated thermal determinant does not select a finite minimum in $\beta$. In the reduced calculation of $G$, $\beta_E$ comes from the Euclidean regularity of the horizon:

$$
\beta_E=2\pi R_H.
$$

## 4. Normalized smooth sector

In the smooth causal sector, the regular gravitational insertion has the expansion:

$$
F_R(z)=a_0+a_1z+\cdots.
$$

The normalized spatial integration cancels the apparent pole of the heat kernel. Therefore:

$$
\operatorname{Res}F_R=0.
$$

Conclusion: a non-zero $G$ requires global boundary data, defect, jump, causal monodromy, or a non-trivial gluing operator. It does not emerge from the completely normalized smooth sector.

## 5. Status

These exclusions do not reopen the global calculation of $G$. They only delimit which routes must not be used as ab initio proof.
