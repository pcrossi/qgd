# 20.1. Gravity as a global GDQ response

The most important point of this chapter is methodological.

GDQ should not try to calculate Newton's constant from a small flat piece of the local bulk. This would confuse a global boundary datum with an infinitesimal property of the fiber.

In the theory, local matter is described by admissible deformations of the Hermitian bulk. Macroscopic gravitation appears when this deformation is read in an effective projection, after the global solution has fixed the energy scale, the horizon, and the normalization of the observed real channel.

Thus, the correct chain is:

$$
\mathcal S_{\rm GDQ}
\to
\Phi_\ast^{\rm cos}
\to
\text{cosmological boundary}
\to
\text{local metric projection}
\to
G_{\rm eff}.
$$

It is not:

$$
\text{isolated local fiber}
\to
G.
$$

## 1. The three reading spaces

The official local bulk is

$$
M_{\rm loc}
=
\mathbb R^4\times T^4.
$$

It is the domain for local reconstruction and laboratory.

The cosmological/spectral space is

$$
M_E
=
T^5\times S^3.
$$

It is used when the problem requires global regularity, thermal cycle, horizon, and cosmological normalization.

Observable classical gravitation is a third reading: the macroscopic effective projection of the GDQ solution in the weak-field limit.

## 2. What the action determines

The official action determines which backgrounds are admissible and what the physical Hessian is around them:

$$
K_{\rm cos}^{\rm phys}
=
P_{\rm phys}\,
\delta^2\mathcal S_{\rm GDQ}[\Phi_\ast^{\rm cos}]\,
P_{\rm phys}.
$$

Here $\Phi_\ast^{\rm cos}$ represents the stationary set

$$
\Phi_\ast^{\rm cos}
=
(g,J,H,f,\mathcal U)_\ast.
$$

The projector $P_{\rm phys}$ removes gauge modes, normalization modes, and fluctuations that do not correspond to macroscopic observables.

What has not yet been done at the metrological level is to solve the complete $\Phi_\ast^{\rm cos}$ and diagonalize $K_{\rm cos}^{\rm phys}$ for all cosmological channels. For this reason, this chapter separates structural results from numbers that can already be evaluated.

## 3. Physical meaning

Physically, $G$ measures the gravitational admittance of the observed universe. It relates total boundary energy, causal radius, and effective metric response.

This is different from saying that $G$ is a free number. GDQ restricts its dimensionless form, its transport scales, and its compatibility with the fine structure. But the cosmological boundary still needs to be specified to produce an absolute metrological value.

This distinction is the same reason why one does not calculate the temperature of a thermodynamic solution without specifying the boundary state. The action provides the laws. The physical solution provides the boundary data.
