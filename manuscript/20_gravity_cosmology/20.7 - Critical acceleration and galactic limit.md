# 20.7. Critical acceleration and galactic limit

The critical acceleration used by GDQ comes from the circular normalization of the horizon. The adopted formula is:

$$
a_0^{\rm GDQ}
=
\frac{c^2}{2\pi R_H}
=
\frac{cH_0}{2\pi}.
$$

The factor $2\pi$ is the circular normalization of the radial horizon response. It is not chosen to coincide with MOND.

> ![[notes/gravity/critical_acceleration]]

The auxiliary de Sitter scale:

$$
\frac{cH_0\sqrt{\Omega_\Lambda}}{2\pi}
$$

is not the main route. It belongs to the de Sitter cosmological reading. The adopted galactic scale uses the same global Hubble boundary used in the normalization of the vacuum energy.

## 1. Numerical evaluation

a_0^{\rm GDQ}

$$

## 2. Galactic limit

In the low-acceleration regime, the reduced effective response takes the form:

$$
g_{\rm obs}
\simeq
\sqrt{g_Na_0^{\rm GDQ}}.
$$

With:

$$
g_N
=
\frac{GM_b(r)}{r^2}.
$$

Then:

$$
\frac{v^2}{r}
\simeq
\sqrt{\frac{GM_b}{r^2}a_0^{\rm GDQ}},
$$

and:

$$
v^4
\simeq
GM_ba_0^{\rm GDQ}.
$$

This is the form of the baryonic Tully-Fisher relation.

## 3. Difference from MOND

GDQ does not take MOND as a fundamental law. It contains a MOND-like limit as a galactic low-acceleration projection.

Gravitational lensing, clusters, and CMB should not be treated by a single scalar law. They belong to the effective metric and the gravitational Hessian:

$$
K_{\rm grav}^{\rm phys}\delta\Phi
=
J_{\rm bar}
+J_{\rm tor}.
$$

Thus, rotation curves are the first reduced limit. Lenses, clusters, and CMB belong to the same geometric sector, but require solving the physical gravitational Hessian on a cosmological/astrophysical background:

$$
K_{\rm grav}^{\rm phys}
=
P_{\rm grav}^{\rm phys}
\delta^2\mathcal S_{\rm GDQ}[\Phi_\ast^{\rm cos}]
P_{\rm grav}^{\rm phys}.
$$

This is the future metrological closure. It does not reopen the structural scale $a_0^{\rm GDQ}=cH_0/(2\pi)$.
