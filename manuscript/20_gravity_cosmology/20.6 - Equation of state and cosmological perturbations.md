# 20.6. Equation of state and cosmological perturbations

In the homogeneous stationary background, the dark energy contribution has the form:

$$
T_{\mu\nu}^{(\Lambda)}
=
-\rho_\Lambda c^2g_{\mu\nu}.
$$

Comparing with a perfect fluid:

$$
T_{\mu\nu}
=
(\rho c^2+p)u_\mu u_\nu
+p g_{\mu\nu},
$$

it follows:

$$
p_\Lambda
=
-\rho_\Lambda c^2.
$$

Therefore:

$$
w
=
\frac{p_\Lambda}{\rho_\Lambda c^2}
=
-1.
$$

## 1. What this resolves

This result shows that, in the homogeneous sector, GDQ dark energy has the same operational signature as a cosmological constant.

But the interpretation is not "adding a constant" to the action. The density comes from materialized UV tension, global dilution, and geometric projection.

It is important to distinguish this sector from a free homogeneous 3-form. A purely spatial 3-form in FLRW dilutes as $a^{-6}$ and has an equation of state $w_B=1$, meaning it behaves as a rigid fluid, not as dark energy.

## 2. Perturbations

Perturbations are not free independent fields. They are fluctuations of the cosmological saddle:

$$
\Phi_\ast^{\rm cos}
=
(g,J,H,f,\mathcal U)_\ast.
$$

The physical Hessian defines:

$$
K_{\rm cos}^{\rm phys}
=
P_{\rm phys}
\delta^2\mathcal S_{\rm GDQ}[\Phi_\ast^{\rm cos}]
P_{\rm phys}.
$$

In an effective scalar reduction, the perturbative mode satisfies:

$$
\left[
\partial_t^2
+3H\partial_t
+c_s^2\frac{k^2}{a^2}
+m_{\rm gap}^2
\right]\delta\Phi_k
=
J_k^{\rm matter}.
$$

If:

$$
m_{\rm gap}^2>0,
$$

the free modes are suppressed or decay.
