# 19.3. Variational potential and stability

The electroweak potential is not added to the action. It is the restriction of the official action to a spectral direction:

$$
\mathfrak G(\varphi)
=
\Phi_\ast+\varphi\Phi_{\rm EW}.
$$

We define:

$$
S_{\rm eff}(\varphi)
=
\mathcal S_{\rm GDQ}[\mathfrak G(\varphi)].
$$

The reduced expansion is:

$$
S_{\rm eff}
=
S_0
+
\frac12a_2|\varphi|^2
+
\frac14a_4|\varphi|^4
+
O(|\varphi|^6).
$$

The geometric electroweak breaking consolidated:

$$
\boxed{
a_2=-0{,}253196676<0.
}
$$

This sign comes from the non-homogeneous $\ell=1$ backreaction in the metric-dilatonic-torsional sector. It proves that the symmetric point has an unstable direction.

The isolated conformal backreaction, however, does not stabilize. The stabilizing term comes from the stoma interface at fixed volume. The radial deformation:

$$
r(Y)=R(1+\varepsilon Y)
$$

with volume conservation produces:

$$
\left.
\frac{A_\varepsilon}{A_0}
\right|_{V=V_0}
=
1+\frac5{128}\varepsilon^4+O(\varepsilon^6).
$$

Using the surface torsional stiffness already derived in the baryonic sector:

$$
S_\partial
=
\alpha
\left(
\frac{3\pi}{2}
+
\frac{3}{4\pi^3}
\right),
$$

we obtain:

$$
a_4^\partial
=
2134{,}360262.
$$

Adding the reduced bulk:

$$
a_4^{\rm bulk}
=
-0{,}805755288,
$$

it follows:

$$
\boxed{
a_4^{\rm total}
=
2133{,}554507>0.
}
$$

The dimensionless minimum is:

$$
\boxed{
\beta_\ast
=
\sqrt{-\frac{a_2}{a_4^{\rm total}}}
=
0{,}0108937431.
}
$$

The physical meaning is direct: the bulk torsion pushes the system away from the symmetric point; the stoma surface prevents runaway and creates the electroweak basin.
