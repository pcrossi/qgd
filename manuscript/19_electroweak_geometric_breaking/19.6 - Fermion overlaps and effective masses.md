# 19.6. Fermions, overlaps and effective masses

In GDQ, "Yukawa" is not a free fundamental parameter. It is the effective name for an overlap integral between geometric modes.

Fermions are modes of the effective Dirac-Bismut operator reconstructed in the physical sector. Electroweak breaking provides a response:

$$
Y_{ij}^{\rm geom}
=
\mathcal N_Y
\int_{\mathcal I}
\langle
\psi_{L,i},
\Phi_{\rm EW}\psi_{R,j}
\rangle
d\mu_g.
$$

Then:

$$
\boxed{
M_{ij}
=
M_{ij}^{(0)}
+
\frac{v}{\sqrt2}Y_{ij}^{\rm geom}.
}
$$

This expression is important because it prevents a common confusion. GDQ does not say that all masses arise from fundamental Yukawas. It says that:

1. primary masses come from the geometric spectrum;
2. electroweak breaking alters, mixes, and transports modes;
3. the effective coefficients are overlaps to be calculated, not free numbers to be tuned.

The natural quantitative extension is to calculate CKM, PMNS, and fermionic corrections by real overlap integrals in the global-local background.
