# 19.1. The electroweak problem as seen by GDQ

In the operational Standard Model, electroweak breaking is described by a fundamental Higgs field. In GDQ, the interpretation is different.

The electroweak sector appears when a normal direction of the geometry becomes unstable in the symmetric background and stabilizes in a new basin. Therefore, the question is not "which scalar field was added?", but:

$$
\boxed{
\text{which mode of the physical Hessian of the official action becomes unstable?}
}
$$

The construction begins at the official action and goes through the second variation:

$$
K_{\rm GDQ}
=
\delta^2\mathcal S_{\rm GDQ}[\Phi_\ast].
$$

Since the action has redundancies and constraints, the physical operator is:

$$
K_{\rm phys}
=
P_{\rm phys}K_{\rm GDQ}P_{\rm phys}.
$$

The electroweak mode is a spectral direction of this operator:

$$
K_{\rm phys}\Phi_{\rm EW}
=
a_2\Phi_{\rm EW}.
$$

For breaking to occur:

$$
a_2<0.
$$

But an isolated unstable direction is not enough. The basin must have a bottom:

$$
a_4>0.
$$

In GDQ, these two signs are not postulated by an external potential. They come from the expansion of the official action in the correct geometric mode and from the interface stiffness of the stoma.

The main conceptual correction of this chapter is:

$$
\boxed{
\text{the electroweak mode is not the homogeneous conformal breathing.}
}
$$

The homogeneous breathing is a singlet. It may be useful as historical intuition, but it does not carry the necessary quantum numbers. The correct mode is a charged Hopf mode.
