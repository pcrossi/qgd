# Neutral matrix and massless photon

In the effective sector, the neutral mass is:

$$
M_0^2
=
\frac{v^2}{4}
\begin{pmatrix}
g^2 & -gg'\\
-gg' & g'^2
\end{pmatrix}.
$$

The determinant of the internal block is:

$$
g^2g'^2-(-gg')^2=0.
$$

Thus there is a null eigenvalue:

$$
m_\gamma^2=0.
$$

The null eigenvector is proportional to:

$$
\begin{pmatrix}
g'\\
g
\end{pmatrix}.
$$

This is the effective photon:

$$
A_\mu
=
\sin\theta_W W_\mu^3+\cos\theta_W B_\mu.
$$

The massive eigenvalue is:

$$
m_Z^2
=
\frac{v^2}{4}(g^2+g'^2).
$$

The physical result is that the zero mass of the photon comes from the kernel imposed by $Q=T_3+Y$, not from numerical tuning.
