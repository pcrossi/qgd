# Variational potential of the geometric electroweak breaking

Let $\Phi_\ast$ be an admissible symmetric background and $\Phi_{\rm EW}$ a physical direction of the Hessian. The test family is:

$$
\mathfrak G(\varphi)
=
\Phi_\ast+\varphi\Phi_{\rm EW}.
$$

The effective potential is simply the restricted action:

$$
S_{\rm eff}(\varphi)
=
\mathcal S_{\rm GDQ}[\mathfrak G(\varphi)].
$$

Expanding:

$$
S_{\rm eff}
=
S_0
+
\frac12a_2|\varphi|^2
+
\frac14a_4|\varphi|^4
+
O(|\varphi|^6).
$$

The quadratic term is:

$$
a_2
=
\langle\Phi_{\rm EW},K_{\rm phys}\Phi_{\rm EW}\rangle.
$$

With:

$$
K_{\rm phys}
=
P_{\rm phys}\delta^2\mathcal S_{\rm GDQ}[\Phi_\ast]P_{\rm phys}.
$$

The quartic term is the fourth physical variation in the same direction, after respecting volume, flux, and interface constraints:

$$
a_4
=
\delta^4\mathcal S_{\rm GDQ}
[
\Phi_{\rm EW},
\Phi_{\rm EW},
\bar\Phi_{\rm EW},
\bar\Phi_{\rm EW}
]_{\rm phys}.
$$

The minimum exists if:

$$
a_2<0,
\qquad
a_4>0.
$$

In the adopted normalization:

$$
|\varphi_\ast|^2
=
-\frac{a_2}{a_4}.
$$

When the variable is written as an effective doublet:

$$
\langle\Phi\rangle
=
\frac1{\sqrt2}
\begin{pmatrix}
0\\
v
\end{pmatrix},
$$

we have:

$$
v^2
=
-\frac{2a_2}{a_4}
$$

after fixing the corresponding kinetic normalization.
