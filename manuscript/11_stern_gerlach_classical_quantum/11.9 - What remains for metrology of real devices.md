# 11.9. What remains for metrology of real devices

This chapter completes the conceptual structure of the experiment. The metrological closure of a real apparatus requires more data:

1. real geometry of the magnets;
2. profile $\mathbf B(\mathbf x,t)$;
3. material, losses, and temperature;
4. beam width and velocity;
5. interface response $\mathsf R_{\rm SG}$;
6. causal mobility and response time;
7. noise and basins of registration.

These data belong to the apparatus. They are not new axioms of GDQ. They define the specific physical problem.

Thus, Stern--Gerlach is divided into:

$$
\text{universal structure}
\quad
\text{and}
\quad
\text{apparatus metrology}.
$$

The universal structure explains why there are two channels and what the angular weights are. The metrology determines width, actual beam separation, efficiency, and noise.

At the already verified level, the self-contained scripts of the chapter show:

| Block | Preserved result | Status |
|---|---:|---|
| Conditioned capture | largest threshold deviation $2.518\sigma$ | statistical test |
| Complete beam | relative separation error $4.737\times10^{-4}$ | effective reduction |
| Sequence $z\to x\to z$ | $P(z+)=0.499975$ | operational test |
| Landau--Zener | maximum error $2.920\times10^{-4}$ | non-adiabatic test |
| Reduced Robin | positive gaps at $N=1600$ | method test |
| Cylindrical DtN | $z_H=3\sqrt\pi/4$ | reduced structural calculation |
| Cylindrical radius | $\mathcal W''(2\sqrt\tau)=3/(2\tau)>0$ | homogeneous stability |

The physical evaluator preserved in the chapter only calculates $\kappa_H^{\rm SG}$ and $\Gamma_{\rm SG}$ when it receives the real spectrum $\{\lambda_\nu,Z_\nu,j_{\nu A},\gamma_\nu,C_\nu\}$. This avoids transforming a test potential into a physical prediction.
