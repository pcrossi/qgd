# 11.8. Adiabatic condition and transitions between channels

The clean separation into two beams requires that the internal orientation of the soliton tracks the local axis of the field. This is an adiabatic hypothesis.

An operational form of the condition is:

$$
\left|
\frac{
\langle -;\mathbf n(t)|\dot H_{\rm int}|+;\mathbf n(t)\rangle
}{
\Delta E(t)^2
}
\right|
\ll1.
$$

Here:

$$
\Delta E(t)=E_+(t)-E_-(t).
$$

In geometric language, the direction $\mathbf n(\mathbf x)$ must vary slowly along the support of the soliton, compared to the gap between the two eigen-bundles.

If the condition fails, transitions between channels can occur. In this case, the description via a trajectory with a fixed $\kappa$ is not sufficient; one must solve the dynamic response of the detector and the object.

The reduced test preserved in this chapter uses:

$$
H(t)
=
\frac12
\left(vt\,\sigma_z+\Delta\sigma_x\right),
$$

and recovers the Landau--Zener formula:

$$
P_{\rm LZ}
=
\exp\left(
-\frac{\pi\Delta^2}{2v}
\right).
$$

In the self-contained execution, the largest absolute error against this expression was $2.920\times10^{-4}$. The same test shows that if $[H,P_{\mathbf n}]\ne0$, the probability of the channel receives Hamiltonian drift and ceases to be a martingale. Therefore, the capture/Born proof is a proof of the adiabatic or QND sector, not of any arbitrarily fast apparatus.
