# 12.6. Loss of visibility via Schur complement

If the detector distinguishes the two paths, there is a field difference at the boundary:

$$
\Delta\Phi_\partial
=
\Phi_{\partial,1}-\Phi_{\partial,2}.
$$

The quadratic cost of distinguishing the paths is:

$$
\Gamma_{\rm det}
=
\frac12
\left\langle
\Delta\Phi_\partial,
\mathsf R_{\rm det}
\Delta\Phi_\partial
\right\rangle.
$$

The cross coherence term is damped by:

$$
\mathcal C_{\rm det}=e^{-\Gamma_{\rm det}}.
$$

Thus:

$$
\rho_{\rm det}
=
I_1+I_2
+
2e^{-\Gamma_{\rm det}}
\sqrt{I_1I_2}\cos\Delta\phi.
$$

Limits:

1. without detector:

   $$
   \Gamma_{\rm det}=0,
   \qquad
   \mathcal C_{\rm det}=1;
   $$

2. strong detector:

   $$
   \Gamma_{\rm det}\gg1,
   \qquad
   \mathcal C_{\rm det}\simeq0.
   $$

This is the distinctive point of the formulation: the loss of visibility comes from interface impedance, not from a mystical collapse.

In the self-contained reduced validation, with:

$$
\lambda_{\rm det}=1.1,
\qquad
L=1,
\qquad
C_{\rm path}=1,
$$

we obtained:

$$
\mathsf R_{\rm det}=1.37414284103.
$$

The principal values at $N=8000$ were:

| $\zeta_{\rm det}$ | $\Gamma_{\rm det}$ | $e^{-\Gamma_{\rm det}}$ | raw central visibility |
|---:|---:|---:|---:|
| $0$ | $0$ | $1$ | $0.987400675$ |
| $0.5$ | $0.171767855$ | $0.842174657$ | $0.893408543$ |
| $1.25$ | $1.073549095$ | $0.341793305$ | $0.547559863$ |
| $2.5$ | $4.294196378$ | $0.013647535$ | $0.270891364$ |

The value that must be read as coherence is $e^{-\Gamma_{\rm det}}$. The raw central visibility is useful as a numerical diagnostic, but also contains the incoherent envelope $I_1+I_2$.

Compared to the standard operational description, the reduced GDQ coincides with the coherent limit when $\Gamma_{\rm det}=0$ and tends to the incoherent limit when $\Gamma_{\rm det}\gg1$. What GDQ adds is the geometric law that interpolates the two limits:

$$
\mathcal C_{\rm det}(\zeta_{\rm det})
=
\exp\left[
-\frac12
\zeta_{\rm det}^2
C_{\rm path}
\lambda_{\rm det}\coth(\lambda_{\rm det}L)
\right].
$$
