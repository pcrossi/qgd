# 12.5. Detector as interface impedance

The detector does not enter as a manual quantum operator. It is a classical, material, and dissipative apparatus, represented by a source or a boundary.

In the first controlled closure, a reduced linear channel of the detector is:

$$
K_{\rm det}
=
-\partial_s^2+\lambda_{\rm det}^2,
\qquad
s\in[0,L].
$$

With boundaries:

$$
\varphi(0)=\varphi_0,
\qquad
\varphi(L)=0.
$$

The stationary solution eliminates the interior and yields the DtN impedance:

$$
\mathsf R_{\rm det}
=
\lambda_{\rm det}\coth(\lambda_{\rm det}L).
$$

In the general block form:

$$
\mathsf R_{\rm det}
=
K_{\partial\partial}
-
K_{\partial I}K_{II}^{-1}K_{I\partial}.
$$

This is the effective response felt by the flow at the detector interface.

In the reduced case numerically validated in this chapter:

$$
\lambda_{\rm det}=1.1,
\qquad
L=1,
$$

the value is:

$$
\mathsf R_{\rm det}
=
1.37414284103.
$$

This number is not universal. It belongs to the reduced detector chosen to verify the DtN/Schur chain. In a real apparatus, $\lambda_{\rm det}$, $L$, and the coupling $\zeta_{\rm det}$ must come from the material, geometry, and effective Hessian of the apparatus.
