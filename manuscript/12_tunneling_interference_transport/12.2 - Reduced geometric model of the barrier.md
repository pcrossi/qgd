# 12.2. Reduced geometric model of the barrier

In the reduced one-dimensional evanescent sector, the density is:

$$
\rho(x)=\rho_0e^{-2\kappa x}.
$$

The reduction of the official action is performed by freezing the transverse modes and keeping only the stationary longitudinal channel. The official action does not change; what changes is the admitted functional domain for the application.

The interface constraint fixes:

$$
g_{xx}(0)=g_0,
\qquad
\rho(0)=\rho_0.
$$

The minimum geometric energy condition of the channel, without real carrier current inside the barrier, selects:

$$
g_{xx}(x)=g_0\frac{\rho(x)}{\rho_0}.
$$

Since:

$$
\rho(x)=\rho_0e^{-2\kappa x},
$$

we have:

$$
\sqrt{g_{xx}(x)}=\sqrt{g_0}\,e^{-\kappa x}.
$$

The proper distance inside the barrier is:

$$
D_{\rm proper}(L)
=
\int_0^L\sqrt{g_{xx}(x)}\,dx.
$$

Thus:

$$
D_{\rm proper}(L)
=
\frac{\sqrt{g_0}}{\kappa}
\left(1-e^{-\kappa L}\right).
$$

In the limit $L\to\infty$:

$$
D_{\rm proper}\to\frac{\sqrt{g_0}}{\kappa}.
$$

This calculation shows how a geometric saturation can remove the superluminal interpretation of the Hartman effect.

The hypothesis:

$$
g_{xx}\propto\rho
$$

is not promoted to a general theorem. It is the reduced solution of the evanescent channel under the above hypotheses.

The effective proper time is:

$$
\tau_{\rm GDQ}(L)
=
\int_0^L\frac{ds}{v_{\rm prop}}.
$$

If $v_{\rm prop}=v_0\le c$ is constant in the stationary regime:

$$
\tau_{\rm GDQ}(L)
=
\frac{\sqrt{g_0}}{v_0\kappa}
\left(1-e^{-\kappa L}\right).
$$

Thus:

$$
\lim_{L\to\infty}\tau_{\rm GDQ}(L)
=
\frac{\sqrt{g_0}}{v_0\kappa}.
$$

The ratio $L/\tau_{\rm GDQ}$ can grow with $L$, but it is a coordinate ratio. The local physical velocity remains:

$$
v_{\rm prop}
=
\frac{ds}{dt}
\le c.
$$

For real packets, the transmitted packet must be treated spectrally:

$$
\Psi_T(x,t)
=
\int T(E)A(E)e^{i(kx-\omega t)}\,dE.
$$

The peak can be reshaped by $T(E)$. This is not a causal front.
