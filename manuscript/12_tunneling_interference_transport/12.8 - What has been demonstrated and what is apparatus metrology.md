# 12.8. What has been demonstrated and what is apparatus metrology

The chapter demonstrates, in the reduced sector, that:

1. Hartman can be geometrically interpreted by saturated proper distance, under the conditional evanescent reduction;
2. double slit is a boundary value problem for density/phase;
3. fringes are channels and barriers of geometric pressure;
4. detectors enter via interface impedance;
5. the loss of coherence is $\exp(-\Gamma_{\rm det})$;
6. delayed choice is a change of boundary/transport, not retrocausality.

For Hartman, the demonstrated chain is:

$$
\rho(x)=\rho_0e^{-2\kappa x}
\quad\Longrightarrow\quad
g_{xx}(x)=g_0\frac{\rho(x)}{\rho_0}
=
g_0e^{-2\kappa x},
$$

in the sector of stationary barrier, real current suppressed, transverse modes frozen, normalized interface, and longitudinal gauge.

Hence:

$$
D_{\rm proper}(L)
=
\frac{\sqrt{g_0}}{\kappa}
\left(1-e^{-\kappa L}\right),
$$

and:

$$
\tau_{\rm GDQ}(L)
=
\frac{\sqrt{g_0}}{v_0\kappa}
\left(1-e^{-\kappa L}\right).
$$

With $\kappa=g_0=v_0=1$, the self-contained script returns:

| $L$ | $D_{\rm proper}(L)$ | fraction of the limit |
|---:|---:|---:|
| $0.1$ | $0.095162581964$ | $0.095162581964$ |

This demonstrates the reduced geometric saturation. The coordinate ratio $L/\tau_{\rm GDQ}(L)$ is neither local velocity nor front velocity.

For a double slit with a reduced linear detector, the complete demonstrated chain is:

$$
K_{\rm det}
=
-\partial_s^2+\lambda_{\rm det}^2
\quad\Longrightarrow\quad
\mathsf R_{\rm det}
=
\lambda_{\rm det}\coth(\lambda_{\rm det}L)
$$

and:

$$
\Gamma_{\rm det}
=
\frac12
\zeta_{\rm det}^2
C_{\rm path}
\mathsf R_{\rm det}.
$$

Thus:

$$
\rho_{\rm det}
=
I_1+I_2
+
2e^{-\Gamma_{\rm det}}
\sqrt{I_1I_2}\cos\Delta\phi.
$$

In the self-contained numerical validation:

$$
\lambda_{\rm det}=1.1,
\qquad
L=1,
\qquad
\mathsf R_{\rm det}=1.37414284103.
$$

This table shows the continuous transition between the standard coherent limit and the path-marker incoherent limit. The positive result is structural: the coherence loss factor comes from the detector impedance, not from a phenomenological factor inserted manually.

What remains for metrology:

1. derive $g_{xx}\propto\rho$ directly from the official action, if it is to be used outside the reduced Hartman model;
2. calculate $\lambda_{\rm det}$, $L$, and detector couplings for real materials;
3. resolve the complete Hessian of $(g,J,H,f,\mathcal U)$ in real geometries of slits and detectors;
4. compare with specific interferometers.

$$
T_{\rm MZI}
=
C(\theta_2)P(\phi,\eta)C(\theta_1),
\qquad
\phi(V)=\pi\frac{V}{V_\pi}.
$$

With $V_\pi=2{,}445\,{\rm V}$, $\tau_{\rm sw}=18{,}1\,{\rm ps}$ and external crosstalk of $-30\,{\rm dB}$, the reduced model yields:

$$
\Gamma_\infty=3{,}453877639491,
\qquad
\mathcal C_\infty=3{,}162277660168\times10^{-2}.
$$

The reduced material Hessian shows that the ideal MZI has:

$$
p_{\rm dark}^{\rm ideal}
=
3{,}749399456655\times10^{-33},
$$

and that $-30\,{\rm dB}$ can be produced, in isolation, by:

$$
\delta\phi=6{,}322448399238\times10^{-2}\,{\rm rad},
$$

or:

$$
\delta V=4{,}920557195241\times10^{-2}\,{\rm V},
$$

or even by amplitude imbalance:

$$

$$

Therefore the structure is closed; the first-principles closure for a real device requires calculating $\delta K_{\rm app}$ from the geometry and the constitutive data of the material.

These pending issues do not reopen the conceptual structure. They delimit what is experimental application.
