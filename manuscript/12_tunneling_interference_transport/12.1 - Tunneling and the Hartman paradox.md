# 12.1. Tunneling and the Hartman paradox

In the Hartman effect, the tunneling time through a wide barrier seems to saturate as the width $L$ grows. The naive interpretation is to conclude that the effective coordinate velocity grows without limit. This interpretation is inadequate: phase time, coordinate distance, and proper distance are not the same thing.

In the evanescent regime:

$$
V_0>E,
$$

the reduced solution has:

$$
\psi(x)=\psi_0e^{-\kappa x},
$$

with:

$$
\kappa=\frac{\sqrt{2m(V_0-E)}}{\hbar}.
$$

Thus:

$$
\rho(x)=\rho_0e^{-2\kappa x}.
$$

The geometric interpretation used here is that the barrier can contract the proper distance accessible to the soliton. This preserves local causality without requiring superluminal travel.

This interpretation has reduced and conditional status. It is not a universal tensorial identity, but can be obtained in the sector:

1. stationary barrier;
2. evanescent mode;
3. real current suppressed inside the barrier;
4. transverse modes frozen;
5. interface normalization at $x=0$;
6. longitudinal gauge of measurement.

In this sector, the geometric density defines the longitudinal impedance of the channel. The effective metric relation is:

$$
g_{xx}(x)
=
g_0\frac{\rho(x)}{\rho_0}.
$$

Therefore:

$$
g_{xx}(x)
=
g_0e^{-2\kappa x}.
$$

The essential point is:

$$
\boxed{
g_{xx}\propto\rho
\text{ is a conditional reduced theorem, not a fundamental postulate.}
}
$$
