# Bound modes, massless channels and normalizations

Akmons's argument applies to bound modes. An extended massless field, like the photonic channel, requires a different formulation. Confusing the two cases is a frequent source of incorrect conclusions about couplings.

## Case A: bound mode

If the mode belongs to an isolated $L^2$ cluster, Lemmas 4 and 5 yield:

$$
q_\varepsilon[I_\varepsilon A,I_\varepsilon B]
\longrightarrow q_0[A,B],
$$

uniform gap, exponential localization, and convergence of projectors. If the channel is coupled to auxiliary modes, the Schur complement also converges, since

$$
\|K_{\perp\perp,\varepsilon}^{-1}\|
\le\Delta^{-1}
$$

uniformly. Therefore,

$$
K_{Q,\varepsilon}^{\rm eff}
\longrightarrow K_{Q,0}^{\rm eff}.
$$

## Symplectic current and normalization

The first variation of the action defines a symplectic potential $\Theta_{\rm GDQ}$. Its antisymmetrized variation defines the current

$$
\omega_{\rm GDQ}
(\delta_1\Phi,\delta_2\Phi)
=\delta_1\Theta_{\rm GDQ}(\delta_2\Phi)
-\delta_2\Theta_{\rm GDQ}(\delta_1\Phi).
$$

On-shell and for linearized perturbations,

$$
d\omega_{\rm GDQ}=0.
$$

In the effective two-derivative electromagnetic sector, the current takes the form

$$
\omega_Q(A_1,A_2)
=Z_Q
\left(
A_1\wedge\star F_{A_2}
-A_2\wedge\star F_{A_1}
\right)
+\omega_Q^{(>2)}.
$$

$Z_Q$ is therefore the coefficient of the physical quadratic form, not a topological integer. Once the primitive amplitude of the generator is fixed, it can be extracted by the ratio between the corresponding symplectic fluxes.

If two global and local leaves bound a region $\Omega$, Stokes yields

$$
\int_{\Sigma_\varepsilon}\omega_Q
-\int_{\Sigma_0}\omega_Q
=\int_{\partial\Omega_{\rm lat}}\omega_Q.
$$

For a bound mode, Agmon localization makes the lateral flux tend to zero. With the clock and the primitive amplitude transported in the same normalization,

$$
Z_Q^{\rm lab}=Z_Q^E.
$$

## Case B: extended massless channel

A massless channel does not necessarily have a normalizable eigenvector in $L^2$. One cannot use a projector of an isolated eigenvalue or the Agmon decay. One chooses generalized solutions normalized by flux and compares the DtN operator or the scattering matrix.

Inheritance requires

$$
\int_{\partial\Omega_{\rm lat}}\omega_Q=0
$$

for the complete channel, in addition to the convergence of DtN at positive frequency and a controlled limit when the frequency tends to zero.

On the normal link $(B^4,S^3)$, the exclusion of a spurious transverse zero can be formulated by relative cohomology:

$$
H^1(B^4,S^3)=0.
$$

Under positivity of the physical Hessian, this eliminates a non-trivial relative harmonic 1-form. The transport of the photonic channel is then conditional on positivity, normal topology, and the absence of leakage.

## What the equality does not calculate

Even when

$$
Z_Q^{\rm lab}=Z_Q^E,
$$

the bridge does not provide the value of $Z_Q^E$. This value must be calculated once in the global background by the official Hessian, including the Schur complement. Only then, in the appropriate convention, do we obtain

$$
\alpha_E=\frac{e_E^2}{4\pi\hbar c}
$$

and transport

$$
\alpha_{\rm lab}=\alpha_E.
$$

This is a conditional equality of inheritance, not a numerical evaluation of $\alpha$.
