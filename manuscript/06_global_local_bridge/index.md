# 06. The global-local bridge: from Einstein's Universe to the laboratory

The previous chapters constructed the geometry, the action, its equations, and the conservation laws. One indispensable question remains: **how can a result obtained in Einstein's cosmological space appear as local physics in the laboratory?**

The problem is not solved by simply saying that a space "becomes approximately flat." It is necessary to transport the fields, the measure, the Hessian, the operator domains, and the spectral sectors. It is also necessary to separate topological quantities, which can survive by homotopy, from continuous normalizations, which require control of the current and the absence of leakage.

This chapter demonstrates the bridge in the class where its hypotheses are satisfied. The construction uses a pointed family that decompactifies $T^5\times S^3$ to $\mathbb R^4\times T^4$. There is no physical wall between the two spaces. Interfaces and Dirichlet-to-Neumann operators appear only at the material boundary of the stoma.

## Status of the result

The geometry of the family and the abstract transport mechanism are demonstrated. The spectral implication is conditional on the existence of an admissible local background and a physical gap. In the stationary Gaussian background with $C_3$ symmetry, these hypotheses have been verified and the bridge is an **applied theorem**, with a primitive gap $\Delta_0=1/2$. For arbitrary backgrounds, the verification must be redone.
