# The pointed family and the flat limit

## Construction of the family

For $\varepsilon>0$, define

$$
M_\varepsilon
=T^4\times S^1_{R_\varepsilon}\times S^3_{R_\varepsilon},
\qquad
R_\varepsilon=\varepsilon^{-1}.
$$

More generally, the radii of the circle and the sphere can grow at different rates, as long as their ratio remains bounded above and below by positive constants. The common choice simplifies the estimates without altering the mechanism.

Choose a basepoint $p_\varepsilon$ at the geometric center associated with the stoma. The point cannot be chosen in a region that causes the defect to escape to infinity.

## The large circle viewed locally

If $\theta$ is the angular coordinate of $S^1_R$, define

$$
x^0=R\theta.
$$

The metric of the circle is

$$
R^2d\theta^2=(dx^0)^2.
$$

For a fixed window $|x^0|<L$ and $R>L/\pi$, the chart does not perceive the periodicity. Therefore, the circle converges locally to $\mathbb R$.

## The large sphere in normal coordinates

Around a point of $S^3_R$, use normal coordinates $x=(x^1,x^2,x^3)$. The expansion of the metric is

$$
(g_{S^3_R})_{ij}(x)
=\delta_{ij}
-\frac13R_{ikjl}(0)x^kx^l
+O\left(\frac{|x|^3}{R^3}\right).
$$

For the round sphere,

$$
R_{ikjl}
=\frac1{R^2}
(\delta_{ij}\delta_{kl}-\delta_{il}\delta_{kj})
$$

in an orthonormal frame. Therefore, on every compact set $|x|\le L$,

$$
\|g_{S^3_R}-g_{\mathbb R^3}\|_{C^k(B_L)}
\le C_{k,L}R^{-2}.
$$

Substituting $R=\varepsilon^{-1}$,

$$
\|g_{S^3_{R_\varepsilon}}-g_{\mathbb R^3}\|_{C^k(B_L)}
\le C_{k,L}\varepsilon^2.
$$

## Pointed convergence

For each compact set

$$
K_L=T^4\times[-L,L]\times B_L(0)
\subset T^4\times\mathbb R^4,
$$

there exist, for small $\varepsilon$, maps

$$
\Phi_{\varepsilon,L}:K_L\longrightarrow M_\varepsilon
$$

such that

$$
\|\Phi_{\varepsilon,L}^*g_\varepsilon-g_0\|_{C^k(K_L)}
\le C_{k,L}\varepsilon^2.
$$

The injectivity radius at the basepoint grows and the curvature derivatives tend to zero. This is the smooth pointed convergence of Cheeger--Gromov:

$$
(M_\varepsilon,g_\varepsilon,p_\varepsilon)
\longrightarrow
(T^4\times\mathbb R^4,g_0,p_0).
$$

## How the defect remains in the limit

Choose radii $L_\varepsilon$ such that

$$
L_\varepsilon\to\infty,
\qquad
\varepsilon L_\varepsilon\to0.
$$

The first limit makes the chart cover increasing local regions; the second ensures that the cosmological curvature remains small in these regions. The stoma and its neighborhood are placed in a fixed compact set of these charts.

This choice avoids two opposite errors: letting the defect escape or expanding the chart so quickly that it starts to perceive the global curvature before the limit.

## Geometric lemma

**Lemma 1.** The above family converges smoothly, in the pointed sense, to $\mathbb R^4\times T^4$. Relative charges and defect classes preserved by homotopy remain defined in the transported compact set.

The lemma proves the geometric bridge. It does not yet prove that the fields satisfy the action or that the spectral modes remain isolated.
