# The $C_3$ sector and the scope of the theorem

## Why a concrete background was necessary

The six lemmas form a transport theorem: if an admissible local background with a gap exists, its isolated sector is inherited. For the bridge to stop being merely conditional, it is necessary to evaluate these hypotheses in a physical class.

This evaluation was performed on the reduced stationary background of three Gaussian centers, with local equilibrium of torsions, Noether closure, and cyclic symmetry $C_3$.

## Decomposition of modes

The displacements of the three centers can be decomposed into a common rotation or translation and two relative modes. The common mode belongs to the symmetry orbit and is removed by $P^{\rm phys}$. The relative modes feel the rigidity of the equilibrium constraint.

In the demonstrated reduction, the lowest physical eigenvalue satisfies

$$
\Delta_0
=\min\left\{
\frac32\kappa_{\rm rel}T^2,
\frac1{2\tau}
\right\}>0.
$$

In the primitive normalization used in the construction,

$$
\boxed{\Delta_0=\frac12.}
$$

Therefore, the trimodal sector has a gap after the removal of the Noether zeroes. Lemmas 4 and 5 apply: the $C_3$ cluster remains localized, its projector converges, and its multiplicity is preserved in the pointed limit.

## Correct status

For the $C_3$ class:

$$
\boxed{
\text{the global--local bridge is closed as an applied theorem.}
}
$$

This means that the abstract incompatibility between $T^5\times S^3$ and $\mathbb R^4\times T^4$ has been removed for this sector. It does not mean that every possible solution of GDQ has the same gap.

## Consequences already permitted

The bridge transports, in the $C_3$ sector:

- the multiplicity of three centers;
- the class and relative charges preserved;
- the removal of the common mode by Noether;
- the stability against perturbations smaller than the gap;
- the trimodal spectral subspace.

These results support the transport of the three-stomata background used in the baryonic sector. They also transport the relevant multiplicity for the leptonic hierarchy.

## What remains separate

The bridge does not automatically demonstrate:

1. that the specific leptonic cluster $n=0,1,17$ matches the cluster of the physical Hessian $C_3$;
2. the dimensional coefficients of masses, radii, or moments;
3. the absolute global normalization of $Z_Q$;
4. the response of a specific apparatus;
5. the existence and stability of backgrounds outside the studied class.

These are pending issues of the corresponding sectors, not failures of the transport theorem.

## Logical synthesis of the chapter

The complete construction can be condensed, without losing its logic, into

$$
\begin{aligned}
&T^4\times S^1_{R}\times S^3_R
\xrightarrow[R\to\infty]{\text{pointed}}
T^4\times\mathbb R\times\mathbb R^3 = T^4\times\mathbb R^4,
\\
&(g_R,J_R,f_R,\mathcal U_R)
\longrightarrow(g_0,J_0,f_0,\mathcal U_0),
\\
&q_R^{\rm phys}\xrightarrow{\rm Mosco}q_0^{\rm phys},
\\
&\Delta_0>0
\Longrightarrow
\text{localization and uniform gap},
\\
&R_R(z)\to R_0(z)
\Longrightarrow
P_{R,I}\to P_{0,I},
\\
&\text{topology and cluster are inherited;}
\\
&\text{continuous normalizations require flow without leakage.}
\end{aligned}
$$

Einstein's Universe and the laboratory are not the same global space. They describe the same physical sector because the fields, the quadratic forms, and the projectors converge in a controlled limit. This is the mathematical bridge that allows using global results without confusing them with local responses.
