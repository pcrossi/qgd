# What the bridge transports and what it does not determine

The pointed limit preserves some information and does not preserve other information without additional hypotheses. Mixing these categories would make the bridge seem more powerful than it actually is.

## Topological invariants

If the stoma remains in a compact set and its interface is transported by homotopy, relative classes are preserved. This includes, depending on the sector:

$$
\text{integral charge},
\quad
\text{index},
\quad
\text{Chern class},
\quad
\text{discrete holonomy}.
$$

The normalization of the form representing the class must be fixed. A topological integer alone does not determine the physical unit of charge.

## Localized spectral data

By Lemmas 4 and 5, an isolated cluster transports:

- rank and multiplicity;
- bound eigenvalues in the controlled limit;
- dimensionless spectral ratios;
- decomposition by preserved representations.

This does not preserve the entire global spectrum. Extended cosmological modes can convert into continuum and do not belong to the cluster theorem.

## Dimensional scales

A spectral ratio can survive without fixing the unit of energy. If

$$
m_j=E_*\,\widehat m_j,
$$

the bridge preserving $\widehat m_i/\widehat m_j$ does not automatically determine $E_*$. The scale may come from the cosmological normalization, a derived length, or an explicitly declared calibration.

## Continuous couplings

Constants like electromagnetic normalization are not integer classes. They are coefficients of quadratic forms or symplectic currents. To transport them, it is necessary to prove convergence of the effective Hessian, temporal synchronization, and the absence of lateral flux.

Therefore,

$$
\text{topology and multiplicity}
\neq
\text{continuous normalization}.
$$

## Apparatus response

The global space provides the state and, in certain sectors, its normalization. The apparatus modifies the local problem via a source and interface condition. The measured response depends on

$$
J_{\rm app}^{\rm clássico}
\to
\delta\Phi_{\rm app}
\to
\mathbb H_*^{\rm phys}
\to
\mathsf R_{\rm app}
\to
\text{record}.
$$

The bridge does not calculate the detector material, its geometry, or its noise. Transporting the mode identity does not eliminate the need to solve the local experimental problem.

## Separation lemma

**Lemma 6.** The pointed limit transports topological invariants, bound clusters, multiplicities, and dimensionless ratios under the previous hypotheses. Absolute scales, continuous couplings, and responses to apparatuses require an additional normalization or flow problem.

This separation is a positive conclusion: it states exactly which calculations can be performed once and for all in Einstein's Universe and which belong to each local experiment.
