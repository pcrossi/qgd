# The global-local problem and its two geometries

## The origin of the difficulty

GDQ uses two spaces with different roles. The official local bulk is

$$
M_0=\mathbb R^4\times T^4.
$$

It is in it that we formulate apparatuses, sources, scattering, and local observations. Einstein's cosmological background is

$$
M_E=T^5\times S^3.
$$

It is compact and allows global calculations of topology, temperature, normalization, and spectrum. These spaces have the same real dimension, but not the same global topology. Therefore, they cannot be identified by a simple change of coordinates.

An equality calculated in $M_E$ is not automatically an equality in the laboratory. To transport it, we must answer:

1. how $M_E$ locally approximates $M_0$;
2. how GDQ fields are compared in different spaces;
3. whether the physical Hessian converges;
4. whether the studied mode remains isolated;
5. whether its normalization or flux escapes in the limit.

## The decomposition that makes the limit possible

Write

$$
T^5=T^4\times S^1.
$$

When the radius of the circle $S^1$ grows, a neighborhood of fixed size looks like a line. When the radius of $S^3$ grows, a normal neighborhood of fixed size looks like $\mathbb R^3$. Thus,

$$
T^4\times S^1_R\times S^3_R
\longrightarrow
T^4\times\mathbb R\times\mathbb R^3
=T^4\times\mathbb R^4
$$

as $R\to\infty$, in the pointed sense that will be defined in the next section.

This passage preserves the dimension and local structure, but does not transform a global compact space into a non-compact space by diffeomorphism. The limit only compares increasingly larger regions around chosen basepoints.

## There is no "cosmological-flat glueing"

A historical attempt introduced an interface to glue $M_E$ directly to $M_0$. This mixes two different operations. The correct relationship is

$$
\boxed{
\begin{aligned}
T^5\times S^3&\longrightarrow\mathbb R^4\times T^4
&&\text{by pointed limit};\\
Y_{\rm st}&=\partial\mathcal N_{\rm st}
&&\text{as the physical boundary of the stoma}.
\end{aligned}
}
$$

The DtN operator belongs to $Y_{\rm st}$. It describes how the exterior responds to a value imposed on the material boundary. There is no second DtN whose function is to glue Einstein's Universe to its local chart.

## The precise statement

We want to show that, for a physical sector localized around the stoma, there exists a family of identifications under which:

$$
\text{global backgrounds}
\to
\text{local background},
$$

$$
\text{global physical Hessians}
\to
\text{local physical Hessian},
$$

and the projectors of an isolated spectral cluster converge. This preserves the identity and multiplicity of the modes. It is not intended to preserve the entire spectrum: upon decompactification, part of the discrete spectrum becomes continuous.

## Data that must be declared

Before applying the bridge, the following must be fixed:

- the basepoint and the defect that will remain visible;
- the backgrounds $(g,J,f)$ and the measure $\mathcal U$;
- the bundle of perturbations;
- the domain and the conditions at the stoma;
- the normalization, charge, and Noether constraints;
- the spectral sector to be transported;
- the classification of the mode as bound or extended.

Without these data, "flat limit" is just an intuitive image.
