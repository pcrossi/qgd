# Resolvents, projectors and spectral inheritance

## Why not compare eigenvectors directly

Eigenvectors have arbitrary phases; in a degenerate space, any unitary basis of the subspace is valid. Furthermore, the Hilbert spaces change with $\varepsilon$. The stable quantity is not a chosen vector, but the projector of the spectral subspace.

The path is

$$
\text{convergence of forms}
\to
\text{convergence of resolvents}
\to
\text{convergence of projectors}.
$$

## Resolvent

For $z$ outside the spectrum, the resolvent is

$$
R_\varepsilon(z)
=(K_\varepsilon^{\rm phys}-z)^{-1}.
$$

The Mosco convergence of the semibounded forms implies, after the unitary identifications,

$$
I_\varepsilon^\dagger
R_\varepsilon(z)
I_\varepsilon
\longrightarrow
R_0(z)
$$

strongly.

Strong convergence means that each vector is transported correctly, but it does not uniformly control all vectors of the unit ball. In the isolated cluster, Agmon localization and local compactness eliminate mass leakage and promote the relevant norm convergence.

## Riesz projector

Choose a closed curve $\Gamma$ in the complex plane that encloses only the cluster $I$ and remains at a positive distance from the rest of the spectrum. The projector is

$$
P_{\varepsilon,I}
=\frac{1}{2\pi i}
\oint_\Gamma
(K_\varepsilon^{\rm phys}-z)^{-1}\,dz.
$$

Since the gap is uniform, the same curve can be used for all small $\varepsilon$. The convergence of the resolvents gives

$$
\left\|
I_\varepsilon^\dagger
P_{\varepsilon,I}
I_\varepsilon
-P_{0,I}
\right\|
\longrightarrow0.
$$

## What follows from the projector convergence

For small $\varepsilon$, the rank is constant:

$$
\operatorname{rank}P_{\varepsilon,I}
=\operatorname{rank}P_{0,I}.
$$

Consequently, the following are preserved:

- the total multiplicity of the cluster;
- its decomposition by symmetries that also converge;
- the class of the spectral bundle;
- indices constructed from this subspace;
- internal eigenvalue ratios, when the corresponding convergence is controlled.

A mode can split within the cluster under perturbation, but it cannot disappear or emerge without an eigenvalue crossing $\Gamma$, that is, without the gap closing.

## Spectral lemma

**Lemma 5.** Under Mosco convergence, uniform gap, and localization, the resolvents converge in the cluster and the Riesz projectors converge in norm. Rank, multiplicity, and spectral class are inherited by the local limit.

This is the mathematical demonstration of the term "the same particle" when passing from the global background to the laboratory: one does not follow a drawing, but an isolated physical subspace.
