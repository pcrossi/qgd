# Localization, gap and spectral stability

## The risk of decompactification

In a compact space, an elliptic operator typically has a discrete spectrum. By sending the global radii to infinity, the levels can accumulate and form a continuum. Therefore, the existence of an eigenvalue in $M_\varepsilon$ does not guarantee that it survives as a localized state in $M_0$.

What protects a mode is a **gap relative to the rest of the spectrum**, generated by the local dynamics of the defect, not by decompactification.

If $I$ is an interval containing the physical cluster of interest, define

$$
\Delta_0
=\operatorname{dist}
\left(
I,
\sigma(K_0^{\rm phys})\setminus I
\right).
$$

The necessary hypothesis is

$$
\Delta_0>0.
$$

## Separation between core and exterior

Choose a smooth function $\chi$ equal to one near the stoma and equal to zero far from it. For a perturbation $\eta$, the IMS formula separates the energy into internal and external regions:

$$
q_\varepsilon[\eta]
=q_\varepsilon[\chi\eta]
+q_\varepsilon
\left[\sqrt{1-\chi^2}\,\eta\right]
-\mathcal E_{\rm IMS}[\eta].
$$

The error $\mathcal E_{\rm IMS}$ depends on $|d\chi|^2$. By making the cutoff transition occur in a wide region, this error can be made small.

In the core, the convergence of the coefficients compares $q_\varepsilon$ with $q_0$. In the exterior, the operator approaches the vacuum operator. If the vacuum threshold is above the cluster, perturbations trying to escape pay a uniform energy.

## Gap transfer

Choose

$$
0<\delta<\frac{\Delta_0}{3}.
$$

First make the internal support large enough so that the IMS error and the tail of the mode are smaller than $\delta$. Then choose $\varepsilon$ small enough so that the local difference between the forms is also smaller than $\delta$. We obtain

$$
\operatorname{dist}
\left(
I_\varepsilon,
\sigma(K_\varepsilon^{\rm phys})\setminus I_\varepsilon
\right)
\ge\Delta_0-2\delta>0.
$$

Therefore, the gap is uniform for small $\varepsilon$.

This order of limits is important: first the exterior is controlled; then the geometry is approximated. Reversing the order can hide states that escape to increasingly distant regions.

## Agmon localization

If the eigenvalue is below the exterior threshold, introduce a weight $e^{a r}$, where $r$ is the distance to the stoma. The weighted identity for the Hessian provides, for $a$ smaller than the value allowed by the gap,

$$
\int e^{2ar}
\left(
|\nabla\eta_\varepsilon|^2
+|\eta_\varepsilon|^2
\right)
\mathcal U_\varepsilon dV_{g_\varepsilon}
\le C.
$$

Thus,

$$
|\eta_\varepsilon(r)|
\lesssim e^{-ar}
$$

in an energetic sense. The constant can be chosen uniformly in the family. This decay prevents the norm of the mode from spreading over the increasing volume.

## Compactification gap is not enough

The harmonics of a circle of radius $R$ have energies proportional to

$$
\frac{k^2}{R^2}.
$$

As $R\to\infty$, this spacing tends to zero. Therefore, global compactification alone does not provide a uniform gap. The gap must come from the physical Hessian of the stoma, the constraints, and its interface response.

## Stability lemma

**Lemma 4.** If the local background has an isolated physical cluster, the vacuum operator maintains an exterior threshold, and the forms converge locally, then the corresponding global cluster remains uniformly isolated and its modes are exponentially localized.

The lemma is an implication. It does not calculate $\Delta_0$ for every background. In the $C_3$ sector, this calculation has already been performed and will be presented in Section 06.10.
