# Hessian, constraints and physical projector

## Why the raw Hessian is not physical

The second variation of the action contains directions that do not represent observable deformations: diffeomorphisms, parameterization redundancies, normalization variations, and shifts along fixed charges. If these directions are kept, symmetry zeroes can be mistaken for instability and forbidden modes can contaminate the spectrum.

Gather the fields into $X=(g,J,f)$ when $J$ is included in the class of Hermitian deformations, always keeping

$$
H=d_J^c\omega_g.
$$

In the minimal formulation with fixed $J$, the corresponding block is simply eliminated. This does not alter the official action.

## Physical constraints

Group normalization, charge, and conserved balances into

$$
\mathcal C(X)=0.
$$

An example of decomposition is

$$
\mathcal C(X)
=\left(
N(X)-1,
Q(X)-q,
\mathcal F_Y(X),
\mathcal Q_{\rm N}(X)-q_{\rm N}
\right).
$$

Here $N$ normalizes $\mathcal U$, $Q$ fixes the defect class, $\mathcal F_Y$ imposes the variational balance at the stoma, and $\mathcal Q_{\rm N}$ collects the Noether charges kept fixed.

The augmented functional is

$$
\mathscr L(X,\lambda)
=S_{\rm phys}(X)-\langle\lambda,\mathcal C(X)\rangle.
$$

The background satisfies

$$
D_X\mathscr L(X_*,\lambda_*)=0,
\qquad
\mathcal C(X_*)=0.
$$

The multipliers implement the physical leaf. They are not new fundamental couplings.

## Physical tangent space

If

$$
C_*=D\mathcal C(X_*)
$$

is the linearization of the constraints and $R_*$ generates diffeomorphisms or other redundancies, a physical perturbation $\eta$ must satisfy

$$
C_*\eta=0
$$

and be orthogonal to the orbits of $R_*$. Let $\mathbb G_*$ be a positive metric on the gauge-fixed space of perturbations. Define

$$
A_*
=\begin{pmatrix}
C_* \\
R_*^\dagger\mathbb G_*
\end{pmatrix}.
$$

The joint projector is

$$
\boxed{
P^{\rm phys}
=I-\mathbb G_*^{-1}A_*^\dagger
\left(A_*\mathbb G_*^{-1}A_*^\dagger\right)^+A_*.
}
$$

The pseudoinverse handles dependencies between constraints and zero modes. This formula is preferable to the product of two projectors, which can fail when they do not commute.

## The constrained Hessian

The relevant Hessian is that of the augmented functional:

$$
\mathbb H_*
=D_X^2\mathscr L(X_*,\lambda_*)
=D^2S_{\rm phys}(X_*)
-\sum_a\lambda_*^aD^2\mathcal C_a(X_*).
$$

The physical operator is

$$
K_*^{\rm phys}
=P^{{\rm phys}\dagger}\mathbb H_*P^{\rm phys}.
$$

It is this operator, with its domain and boundary conditions, that must be compared along the pointed family.

## The boundary of the stoma

When integrating by parts, the first variation produces the boundary momentum. For a free interface, the natural condition is the continuity of this momentum. In the second variation, the exterior can be eliminated by the DtN operator on the same boundary $Y_{\rm st}$. If the space of perturbations is divided into a channel of interest and its complement,

$$
K=
\begin{pmatrix}
K_{QQ}&K_{Q\perp}\\
K_{\perp Q}&K_{\perp\perp}
\end{pmatrix},
$$

the effective operator is the Schur complement

$$
K_Q^{\rm eff}
=K_{QQ}
-K_{Q\perp}K_{\perp\perp}^{-1}K_{\perp Q}.
$$

The inverse only exists after the removal of zeroes and the proof of the gap.

## Convergence of forms

In the unitary identifications of the previous section, the correct statement is

$$
q_\varepsilon^{\rm phys}[I_\varepsilon\eta]
\longrightarrow
q_0^{\rm phys}[\eta]
$$

for a common core of compactly supported perturbations. It follows from the convergence of the coefficients, measures, projectors, and DtN of the same stoma.

**Lemma 3.** Under these hypotheses, the closed quadratic forms of the physical Hessian converge in the Mosco sense. The complete proof still requires the global liminf estimate, provided by localization and the gap.
