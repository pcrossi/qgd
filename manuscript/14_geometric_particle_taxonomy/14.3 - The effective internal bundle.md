# 14.3. The effective internal bundle

The laboratory does not directly observe all the bulk fields. It observes physical modes projected onto an effective base $N$.

Over this base, we define the internal bundle:

$$
E_{\rm int}\to N.
$$

The minimal decomposition used by the geometric taxonomy is:

$$
E_{\rm int}
=
E_C\oplus E_W\oplus L_Y.
$$

Each term has a function:

1. $E_C\simeq\mathbb C^3$ represents the color sector;
2. $E_W\simeq\mathbb C^2$ represents the chiral weak sector;
3. $L_Y$ is the complex hypercharge line.

This decomposition is effective. It does not introduce a new action nor transform GDQ into the Standard Model. It identifies the layer of internal modes on which the effective connections act.

In a local trivialization, an effective connection has the form:

$$
A_\mu
=
G_\mu^aT_a
+
W_\mu^it_i
+
B_\mu Y.
$$

A didactic way of visualizing the abelian sector is via an Ehresmann connection on the internal fiber, with $\Theta^a=d\theta^a+g_aA^a$. This language explains how a change of internal coordinate induces the gauge transformation, but remains an effective bridge; the fundamental action remains the official action of GDQ.

The reconstructed spectral operator is the coupled Dirac--Bismut operator:

$$
\slashed D_{B,A}
=
\gamma^\mu
\left(
\nabla_\mu^{\rm LC}
+
\frac18B_{\mu\nu\lambda}\gamma^{\nu\lambda}
-iA_\mu
\right).
$$

This operator is effective/reconstructed. The fundamental action remains the official action of GDQ.
