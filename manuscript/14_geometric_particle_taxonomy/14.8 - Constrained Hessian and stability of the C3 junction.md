# 14.8. Constrained Hessian and stability of the C3 junction

The number three is not enough. It is necessary to show that the $C_3$ junction is stable under collective modes that could undo the count.

Before using any geometric monotonicity, we must recall the technical rule: monotonicity is a Lyapunov statement; particle stability requires the physical Hessian. The general proof is in [[notes/monotonicity_does_not_imply_stability|Monotonicity does not imply stability without Hessian]].

The closure constraint is:

$$
\mathcal C
=
\sum_{a=1}^{3}\mathbf T_a=0.
$$

The augmented functional is:

$$
\widetilde{\mathcal S}
=
\mathcal S_{\rm GDQ}
+
\boldsymbol\lambda\cdot\mathcal C.
$$

Writing:

$$
\mathbf T_a
=
T(\cos\theta_a,\sin\theta_a),
$$

the Jacobian of the constraint is:

$$
D\mathcal C
=
T
\begin{pmatrix}
-\sin\theta_1&-\sin\theta_2&-\sin\theta_3\\
\cos\theta_1&\cos\theta_2&\cos\theta_3
\end{pmatrix}.
$$

The constrained angular Hessian is:

$$
H_\theta
=
\kappa_{\rm rel}
(D\mathcal C)^\dagger D\mathcal C.
$$

At the $C_3$ equilibrium:

$$
\operatorname{spec}H_\theta
=
\kappa_{\rm rel}T^2
\left\{
0,
\frac32,
\frac32
\right\}.
$$

The zero corresponds to global rotation. After projecting the relative sector:

$$
H_{\rm rel}
=
\frac32\kappa_{\rm rel}T^2I_2.
$$

The homogeneous radial mode derived from the official action has:

$$
K_\perp^{(r,0)}
=
\frac{3}{2\tau}I_3>0.
$$

Since the primitive flux class is preserved by physical fluctuations:

$$
J_{\theta r}=0.
$$

Hence, the Schur complement is:

$$
H_{\rm eff}
=
H_{\rm rel}
-
J_{\theta r}
\left(
K_\perp^{(r,0)}
\right)^{-1}
J_{\theta r}^{\dagger}
=
H_{\rm rel}.
$$

Therefore:

$$
\operatorname{spec}H_{\rm eff}
=
\kappa_{\rm rel}T^2
\left\{
\frac32,
\frac32
\right\}
>0.
$$

This establishes the collective stability of the junction. Inhomogeneous metric modes belong to the global spectral problem and are not used as the logical origin of the count.
