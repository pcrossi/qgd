# 14. Geometric Taxonomy, Effective Group, and Three Generations

This chapter addresses the question: if GDQ describes matter as geometry, how do the sectors that we operationally call color, weak isospin, hypercharge, and generations arise?

The goal is not to copy the Standard Model. The goal is to show what geometric structure must exist so that the known operational sector appears as a local effective reduction.

The thesis of the chapter is:

$$
\text{particles are geometric sectors of solitons/stomata;}
$$

$$
\text{gauge is the effective automorphism of internal modes;}
$$

$$
\text{generations are the additive index of three primitive stomata.}
$$

## Central result

The effective internal bundle is:

$$
E_{\rm int}
=
E_C\oplus E_W\oplus L_Y.
$$

The effective group is:

$$
G_{\rm eff}
=
\operatorname{Aut}_{\rm GDQ}(E_{\rm int}).
$$

In the local reduced sector:

$$
G_{\rm eff}^{\rm global}
=
\frac{
SU(3)_C\times SU(2)_L\times U(1)_Y
}{
\mathbb Z_6
}.
$$

The hypercharges of a generation are:

$$
\left(
Y_Q,
Y_{u^c},
Y_{d^c},
Y_L,
Y_{e^c}
\right)
=
\left(
\frac16,
-\frac23,
\frac13,
-\frac12,
1
\right).
$$

The selection of three stomata follows from:

$$
\mathcal S_{\rm GDQ}
\to
\text{Noether}
\to
\sum_{a=1}^{N}\mathbf T_a=0
\to
\text{junction C3}
\to
\operatorname{Ind}_{\rm APS}=3
\to
N_G=3.
$$
