# 14.7. Three stomata by Noether and Hopf

The selection of three stomata is not an experimental input. It arises from flux conservation and tension closure in the horizontal Hopf distribution.

Write:

$$
f=u+iv.
$$

The symmetry:

$$
v\mapsto v+\varepsilon
$$

generates the phase Noether current. Integrated over a small region enclosing the junction, it yields:

$$
\sum_{a=1}^{N}\mathbf T_a=0.
$$

The tensions belong to the horizontal distribution:

$$
\mathcal H=\ker\eta_H,
\qquad
\dim_{\mathbb R}\mathcal H=2.
$$

An elementary junction must be:

1. closed;
2. non-colinear;
3. isolated after removing the global rotation.

Non-colinear closure in dimension two requires:

$$
N\ge3.
$$

For $N>3$, the angular matrix leaves $N-3$ internal null modes. Isolation requires:

$$
N-3=0.
$$

Hence:

$$
N=3.
$$

The equilibrium is:

$$
(\theta_1,\theta_2,\theta_3)
=
\left(
\theta_0,
\theta_0+\frac{2\pi}{3},
\theta_0+\frac{4\pi}{3}
\right).
$$

Each co-oriented primitive stoma contributes a unitary local APS index. The local calculation is detailed in [[notes/aps_index_hopf_bismut|Local APS index, Hopf and Bismut]]. By additivity:

$$
\operatorname{Ind}_{\rm total}=3.
$$

Since the global gluing associates six units of charge per primitive sector:

$$
A=6\operatorname{Ind}_{\rm total}=18.
$$

Therefore:

$$
N_G=\frac A6=3.
$$

The note [[notes/global_product_and_three_stomata|Global product, non-circularity and three stomata]] explains why this conclusion does not come from the flat global product $T^5\times S^3$ nor from choosing a topological class based on the target.
