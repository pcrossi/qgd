# 14.4. The effective group as a GDQ automorphism

In GDQ, gauge is not a fundamental symmetry introduced externally. Gauge is the freedom to choose internal frames without changing the physical geometry.

Therefore:

$$
G_{\rm eff}
=
\operatorname{Aut}_{\rm GDQ}(E_{\rm int}).
$$

This group preserves:

1. internal Hermitian metric;
2. complex orientation;
3. global volume/phase;
4. spinorial structure;
5. torsion and gluing data.

For the color sector:

$$
E_C\simeq\mathbb C^3.
$$

Unitary frame changes yield $U(3)$. Preserving complex volume removes the common phase:

$$
\det U_C=1,
$$

hence:

$$
G_C=SU(3)_C.
$$

For the weak sector:

$$
E_W\simeq\mathbb C^2.
$$

Separating the abelian phase in $L_Y$, there remains:

$$
G_W=SU(2).
$$

The physical action is chiral:

$$
P_L
=
\frac12(1-\Gamma_{\rm GDQ}).
$$

Thus:

$$
SU(2)_L
\text{ acts on }
P_LE_{\rm int}.
$$

Finally, the hypercharge line yields:

$$
U(1)_Y.
$$

Locally, there appears:

$$
SU(3)_C\times SU(2)_L\times U(1)_Y.
$$

Globally, the gluing requires the $\mathbb Z_6$ quotient.
