# 14.1. Why taxonomy cannot be a free table

A geometric theory of matter cannot begin with a table of particles and then search for figures that resemble each entry. That would be taxonomy by analogy.

In GDQ, the correct question is the inverse:

$$
\text{what geometric sectors are allowed by the action, constraints, and boundaries?}
$$

Only then does one ask if these sectors, when reconstructed in the laboratory, possess the known quantum numbers.

The central idea adopted by GDQ is:

$$
\text{matter}
=
\text{soliton/defect/stoma of the geometry.}
$$

Therefore, we should not say that a particle exists because a geometric table named it. We must show the chain:

$$
\mathcal S_{\rm GDQ}
\to
\Phi_\ast
\to
\text{Noether}
\to
K_{\rm phys}
\to
\text{internal modes}
\to
\text{automorphisms}
\to
\text{charges}
\to
\text{index}.
$$

The chapter follows this chain.
