# 13.6. Sagnac and the simultaneity holonomy

In the Sagnac effect, the physical circuit rotates with angular velocity $\boldsymbol\Omega$. The local rotation velocity is:

$$
\mathbf v_{\rm rot}
=
\boldsymbol\Omega\times\mathbf r.
$$

The time difference between the two directions is:

$$
\Delta t_{\rm Sag}
=
\frac{2}{c^2}
\oint_\gamma
\left(
\boldsymbol\Omega\times\mathbf r
\right)\cdot d\mathbf r.
$$

By Stokes:

$$
\oint_\gamma
\left(
\boldsymbol\Omega\times\mathbf r
\right)\cdot d\mathbf r
=
2\boldsymbol\Omega\cdot\mathbf A.
$$

Thus:

$$
\Delta t_{\rm Sag}
=
\frac{4\boldsymbol\Omega\cdot\mathbf A}{c^2}.
$$

In the GDQ reading, the reduced clock-form is:

$$
\Theta_t
=
dt
-
\frac{1}{c^2}
\left(
\boldsymbol\Omega\times\mathbf r
\right)\cdot d\mathbf r.
$$

Sagnac measures the holonomy of this simultaneity form.
