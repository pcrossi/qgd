# 13. Aharonov-Bohm, Sagnac and physical holonomies

This chapter deals with effects where there is no ordinary local force in the packet path, but there is an observable phase shift or time shift. The common reading is paradoxical because it searches for a local force where the correct observable is global.

The thesis of the chapter is:

$$
\text{holonomies are local-in-charts and global-by-gluing effects.}
$$

In Aharonov--Bohm, the holonomy is gauge. In Sagnac, the holonomy is clock/simultaneity. Both are connection effects, but they are not the same physical mechanism.

## Central result

For ideal Aharonov--Bohm:

$$
\Delta\varphi_{\rm AB}
=
\frac{q\Phi}{\hbar c}.
$$

For Sagnac:

$$
\Delta t_{\rm Sag}
=
\frac{4\boldsymbol\Omega\cdot\mathbf A}{c^2}.
$$

For light:

$$
\Delta\varphi_{\rm Sag}^{\gamma}
=
\frac{8\pi}{\lambda c}\boldsymbol\Omega\cdot\mathbf A.
$$

For matter:

$$
\Delta\varphi_{\rm Sag}^{m}
=
\frac{4m}{\hbar}\boldsymbol\Omega\cdot\mathbf A.
$$

The common point is the integral of a connection along a circuit. The distinguishing point is which connection is being integrated.
