# 13.1. What is a physical holonomy

A connection can be locally trivial and globally observable. If a 1-form $\mathcal A$ is integrated along a closed circuit $\gamma$, the natural observable is:

$$
\operatorname{Hol}_\gamma(\mathcal A)
=
\exp\left(i\oint_\gamma \mathcal A\right).
$$

If the domain is simply connected and $\mathcal A=d\chi$ globally, then:

$$
\oint_\gamma \mathcal A=0.
$$

But if the domain is punctured or if the connection needs to be described by charts, it can occur that:

$$
d\mathcal A=0
\quad
\text{locally,}
\qquad
\oint_\gamma \mathcal A\ne0
\quad
\text{globally.}
$$

This is the core of holonomy effects. There is no ordinary local force in the path, but there is a global gluing of phase or clock.
