# 13.5. Real solenoids and interface impedance

In the ideal solenoid:

$$
A_{\rm eff}=A_{\rm harm},
\qquad
dA_{\rm harm}=0,
\qquad
\oint_\gamma A_{\rm harm}=\Phi.
$$

In a real solenoid, there are surfaces, losses, material and residual fields. The effective form can be written:

$$
A_{\rm eff}
=
A_{\rm harm}
+
\delta A_{\rm surf}.
$$

The surface correction must come from an interface response:

$$
\textsf R_{\rm sol}
=
K_{YY}
-
K_{YI}K_{II}^{-1}K_{IY}.
$$

This formula is not inserted as an external analogy. It is the Schur reduction of the physical Hessian of the official action around the background with the apparatus.

Schematically, if $\Phi=(g,J,H,f)$ represents the geometric fields of GDQ and $\Phi_\ast$ is the stationary background with the solenoid, the second variation defines:

$$
K_{\rm GDQ}
=
\left.
\frac{\delta^2\mathcal S_{\rm GDQ}}
{\delta\Phi\,\delta\Phi}
\right|_{\Phi_\ast}.
$$

Since there are gauge directions and charge/flux constraints, we do not use the entire $K_{\rm GDQ}$. First, we project to the physical sector:

$$
K_{\rm phys}
=
P_{\rm phys}^{\dagger}
K_{\rm GDQ}
P_{\rm phys}.
$$

The projector $P_{\rm phys}$ removes pure gauge modes and preserves the variations compatible with the global constraints of the problem. Then we separate the boundary variables $Y$ from the internal variables $I$:

$$
K_{\rm phys}
=
\begin{pmatrix}
K_{YY} & K_{YI}\\
K_{IY} & K_{II}
\end{pmatrix}.
$$

Variationally eliminating the internal degrees of freedom is equivalent to solving:

$$
K_{II}\delta\Phi_I
=
-
K_{IY}\delta\Phi_Y.
$$

Substituting back, the effective boundary energy becomes:

$$
\delta^2\mathcal S_{\rm eff}
=
\langle
\delta\Phi_Y,
\textsf R_{\rm sol}\delta\Phi_Y
\rangle_Y.
$$

Therefore, $\textsf R_{\rm sol}$ is the geometric impedance of the apparatus seen by the soliton. In the ideal case, it does not alter the harmonic component of the connection; in the real case, it generates $\delta A_{\rm surf}$.

Then:

$$
\Delta\varphi
=
\frac{q}{\hbar c}
\oint_\gamma
\left(
A_{\rm harm}
+
\delta A_{\rm surf}
\right).
$$

In the ideal limit:

$$
\oint_\gamma\delta A_{\rm surf}=0.
$$

Therefore, the ideal AB remains structurally closed. Real solenoid corrections are apparatus metrology.
