# 13.3. Local gauge and global gluing

The exterior of the solenoid can be covered by two open sets:

$$
M_{\rm ext}=U_N\cup U_S.
$$

In each chart, the connection can be pure:

$$
A_N=d\chi_N,
\qquad
A_S=d\chi_S.
$$

In the intersection:

$$
A_N-A_S=d(\chi_N-\chi_S).
$$

The transition function is:

$$
g_{NS}
=
\exp\left[
\frac{iq}{\hbar c}
(\chi_N-\chi_S)
\right].
$$

It stores the global data that does not appear in an isolated chart.

The role of Mayer--Vietoris here is to organize precisely this gluing. The sequence should not be read as an additional force; it tells how a form that is exact in each patch can carry a non-trivial cocycle when the patches are glued.

In each simply connected open set:

$$
[A_N]=0,
\qquad
[A_S]=0.
$$

But in the united domain:

$$
[A]\in H^1(M_{\rm ext})
$$

can be non-zero, because the information appears in the transition in $U_N\cap U_S$. Thus, the difference:

$$
\chi_N-\chi_S
$$

is not just a local choice without content. It determines the transition phase of the charged bundle:

$$
\psi_N
=
g_{NS}\psi_S.
$$

Upon traversing a loop that encloses the solenoid, the composition of the transitions returns to the same point with the factor:

$$
\exp\left[
\frac{iq}{\hbar c}
\oint_\gamma A
\right].
$$

This is the physical content of the holonomy: locally, each chart looks trivial; globally, the gluing is non-trivial.

A gauge transformation:

$$
A\to A+d\lambda
$$

alters the local phase, but in a closed loop:

$$
\oint_\gamma d\lambda=0
$$

for single-valued $\lambda$. Thus, the physical holonomy is preserved.

For large gauge transformations, the admissible condition is:

$$
\frac{q}{\hbar c}
\oint_\gamma d\lambda
\in
2\pi\mathbb Z.
$$

In this case, the phase can change by integer multiples of $2\pi$, but the observable holonomy remains the same.

This is the correct way to state that the potential is physical: the observable is not the local value of $A$, but its holonomy class.
