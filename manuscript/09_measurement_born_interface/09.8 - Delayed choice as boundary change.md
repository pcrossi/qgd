# 09.8. Delayed choice as boundary change

In delayed choice, the final arrangement of the apparatus can be defined after the packet has traversed part of the interferometer. The incorrect interpretation is to imagine a signal sent to the past. The GDQ interpretation is simpler: the final experiment defines which boundary value problem was realized prior to the record.

In the reduced sector, the observed density can be written as:

$$
\rho_{\rm obs}
=
\rho_1+\rho_2
+
2\sqrt{\rho_1\rho_2}\,
\mathcal C_{\rm det}
\cos\left(\frac{S_1-S_2}{\hbar}\right).
$$

The coherence factor is:

$$
\mathcal C_{\rm det}=e^{-\Gamma_{\rm det}}.
$$

With a reduced detector response:

$$
\Gamma_{\rm det}
=
\frac12
\langle
\Delta\Phi_\partial,
\text{R}_{\rm det}\Delta\Phi_\partial
\rangle.
$$

When the detector or recombiner is changed, we have the transition:

$$
\text{R}_{\rm old}
\longrightarrow
\text{R}_{\rm new}.
$$

This alters the final coherence, but does not rewrite causal history. The outcome arises from the global solution compatible with the boundaries actually imposed on the apparatus.
