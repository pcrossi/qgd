# 09.3. Operational probabilities in the reconstructed Hilbert space

Chapter 8 reconstructed the physical Hilbert space as an operational layer:

$$
\mathcal H_{\rm phys}
=
\overline{\mathcal D_+/(\mathcal N+\mathcal G)}.
$$

An ideal measurement does not measure an abstract word like "value". It separates mutually exclusive physical alternatives. In the reconstructed Hilbert space, these alternatives are represented by projectors:

$$
P_i=P_i^\dagger=P_i^2,
\qquad
P_iP_j=0\quad(i\ne j),
\qquad
\sum_iP_i=I.
$$

An operational probability rule must be positive, normalized, and additive over exclusive alternatives:

$$
\mu(P_i)\ge0,
\qquad
\mu(I)=1,
\qquad
\mu\left(\sum_iP_i\right)=\sum_i\mu(P_i).
$$

In the physical sector, with composition compatibility, the admissible form is:

$$
\mu(P)=\operatorname{Tr}(\varrho P).
$$

For a pure state:

$$
\varrho=|\psi\rangle\langle\psi|.
$$

Then:

$$
\mu(P_i)
=
\langle\psi|P_i|\psi\rangle.
$$

If $P_i=|i\rangle\langle i|$, we obtain:

$$
P(i|\psi)=|\langle i|\psi\rangle|^2.
$$

This is the operational Born rule. It does not replace GDQ with standard quantum mechanics. It states how the reconstructed operational layer of GDQ calculates readout frequencies when an apparatus defines the alternatives.

The technical proof is in
[[notes/operational_born_gleason_trace|Operational Born rule by measurement on projectors]].
It includes additivity, normalization, the transition to arbitrary bases, compatibility with composite systems, and the recovery of position probability as the integral of $\rho$ in the regular sector.
