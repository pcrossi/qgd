# 09.5A. Multiparametric calibration and invariant embedding

Knowing that the apparatus enters as a source and a boundary still does not answer a practical question: how to determine which apparatus was actually constructed? Geometry, temperature, current, alignment, losses, and preparation can simultaneously alter the recorded response. The theory needs to separate these instrumental data from the universal constants and the response of the object.

Consider controllable or calibratable physical parameters:

$$
\boldsymbol\lambda
=
(\lambda_1,\ldots,\lambda_p).
$$

They are not new fundamental parameters of GDQ. They are coordinates of a family of apparatuses or experimental conditions. Examples include current, active length, temperature, orientation, distance, material density, and contact impedance.

The complete chain is:

$$
J_{\rm app}^{\rm classico}
to
\Phi_*(\boldsymbol\lambda)
to
\text{Hess }\mathcal S_{\rm GDQ}(\boldsymbol\lambda)
to
\text{R}_{\rm app}(\boldsymbol\lambda)
to
\mathcal O(\boldsymbol\lambda)
to
\text{record}.
$$

Wait! Let me replace `to` with `\to` in the chain display equation:
```latex
J_{\rm app}^{\rm classico}
\to
\Phi_*(\boldsymbol\lambda)
\to
\text{Hess }\mathcal S_{\rm GDQ}(\boldsymbol\lambda)
\to
\text{R}_{\rm app}(\boldsymbol\lambda)
\to
\mathcal O(\boldsymbol\lambda)
\to
\text{record}.
```
Yes, let's use `\to` instead of `to` to follow scientific notation.

Here,

$$
K_{\rm phys}
=
P_{\rm phys}^{\dagger}
\operatorname{Hess}_{\Phi_*}\mathcal S_{\rm GDQ}
P_{\rm phys}
$$

is the Hessian after the removal of gauge modes, non-physical zero modes, and variations incompatible with charge, flux, normalization, and boundary. Separating accessible interface data, denoted by $q$, and internal degrees of freedom, denoted by $y$, we obtain:

$$
\text{R}_{\rm app}
=
K_{qq}
-
K_{qy}K_{yy}^{-1}K_{yq}.
$$

Therefore, the observed impedance is the Schur complement of the physical Hessian. It is not chosen to reproduce the result: it is calculated after fixing the background, source, domain, and boundary.

## Invariant embedding

When the apparatus can be constructed by continuous composition of parts, its response can be transported without solving the entire interior again. For a physical coordinate $\lambda$, the response satisfies a matrix Riccati equation:

$$
\frac{d\text{R}}{d\lambda}
=
A_{21}
+A_{22}\text{R}
-\text{R} A_{11}
-\text{R} A_{12}\text{R}.
$$

The variable $\lambda$ does not need to be a thickness. It can be any coordinate with a physical law of composition, such as accumulated current in a linear region, optical depth, active causal length, or continuous deformation between two apparatuses.

With multiple controls, a family of flows arises:

$$
\frac{\partial\text{R}}{\partial\lambda_i}
=
\mathcal F_i(\text{R},\boldsymbol\lambda).
$$

If the final response is independent of the order in which the controls are applied, the flows must be compatible. When compatibility fails, the curvature in the parameter space measures memory, hysteresis, or physical non-commutativity of the preparation.

## The point of contact between parameters

Each experiment defines a manifold of possible responses:

$$
\mathcal M_a
=
\left\{
\mathcal O_a(\boldsymbol\lambda):
\boldsymbol\lambda\in\Lambda
\right\}.
$$

Given a record $D_a$, its preimage defines a compatibility set in the parameter space:

$$
\mathcal C_a
=
\left\{
\boldsymbol\lambda\in\Lambda:
\mathcal O_a(\boldsymbol\lambda)=D_a
\right\}.
$$

The apparatus compatible with all records lies in the correct intersection:

$$
\boldsymbol\lambda_*
\in
\mathcal C_1\cap\cdots\cap\mathcal C_k.
$$

With noise, the point is determined by the weighted minimum:

$$
\widehat{\boldsymbol\lambda}
=
\operatorname*{arg\,min}_{\boldsymbol\lambda}
\sum_a
\left[
D_a-\mathcal O_a(\boldsymbol\lambda)
\right]^{\mathsf T}
\Sigma_a^{-1}
\left[
D_a-\mathcal O_a(\boldsymbol\lambda)
\right].
$$

Local identifiability is tested by the observational Jacobian $J$ and by the matrix:

$$
\mathcal I
=
J^{\mathsf T}\Sigma^{-1}J.
$$

If $\mathcal I$ has full rank, the parameters can be separated locally. A null mode indicates that different combinations produce the same record; in this case, no algorithm can recover unique parameters without a new observable or a new preparation.

## Rule of use

The calibration set determines $\widehat{\boldsymbol\lambda}$. Then it is frozen:

$$
D_{\rm cal}
\longrightarrow
\widehat{\boldsymbol\lambda}
\longrightarrow
\mathcal O_{\rm pred}(D_{\rm test}).
$$

Using the same record to choose and validate the parameters would be mere tuning. The separation between calibration and testing is part of the definition of the method.

## Generality

The construction does not depend exclusively on GDQ. It can be applied whenever a theory provides:

1. a functional or a linearizable system;
2. an admissible background;
3. a physical operator with defined domain and boundary;
4. external sources;
5. independent observables and calibration data.

In elasticity, $K_{\rm phys}$ is the stiffness; in electromagnetism, it comes from the Maxwell operator with material and boundary; in acoustics, optics, transport, and field theories, it is the corresponding second variation or linearized operator. The method does not make these theories equivalent. It provides a common language to go from theory and apparatus to a calibratable response.

Therefore, the appropriate name is:

> **Multiparametric Invariant Embedding Method for Calibration and Compatibility of Measurements.**

It is a general method for characterization of measurements. It does not constitute on its own a complete theory of the individual event: selection of outcomes, irreversibility, and probabilities require the constructions of the following sections.

The mathematical ingredients — Schur complement, Dirichlet-to-Neumann operator, Riccati equations, invariant embedding, and Fisher information — are known separately. The proposal of this chapter is their organization into a single chain of compatibility between theory, apparatus, frozen calibration, and independent testing. A claim of historical originality for this synthesis requires a specific bibliographical review.

## Initial validation with real data

The method was tested with the anti-Helmholtz coils and the interferometric visibility of $^{133}{\rm Cs}$ published by Fein et al. The response of the coils was characterized independently by a Hall probe and by geometry:

$$
C_{\rm coil}(I)
=
(10.3\ {\rm G\,m/A})I.
$$

Only the background uniform gradient was calibrated on half of the nominal series of $380\ {\rm m/s}$. The frozen parameter was then applied to the retained half and to the independent series of $270\ {\rm m/s}$:

| set | $N$ | RMSE | MAE | bias |
|---|---:|---:|---:|---:|
| calibration, $380\ {\rm m/s}$ | 15 | 0.022693 | 0.021161 | -0.010751 |
| internal test, $380\ {\rm m/s}$ | 14 | 0.022753 | 0.020167 | -0.003857 |
| independent validation, $270\ {\rm m/s}$ | 30 | 0.023745 | 0.019905 | -0.000433 |

The calibrated value was $0.35035948\ {\rm G/m}$, compared to $0.4\ {\rm G/m}$ reported by the authors in the fit of the complete set. There was no relevant degradation outside the calibration set.

This result initially validates the instrumental protocol. It is not an exclusive prediction of GDQ, because the magnetic response $m_Fg_F\mu_B$ was taken from the operational characterization of cesium. A fundamental prediction will require rederiving this channel from the official Hessian in the physical background of the atom, freezing it, and repeating the test.
