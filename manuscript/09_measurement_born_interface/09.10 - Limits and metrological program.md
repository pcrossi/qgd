# 09.10. Limits and metrological program

Chapter 9 closes the conceptual structure of measurement in GDQ, but must not promise a universal metrological calculation without apparatus data. A real measurement depends on materials, geometries, losses, response times, and boundaries.

What is closed:

1. $\rho$ is a conserved positive density;
2. Born is not just $\rho=R^2$;
3. Operational Born rule follows in the reconstructed physical Hilbert space;
4. The apparatus enters as a classical source, constraint, or boundary;
5. Decoherence explains diagonal records;
6. Unique outcome is treated by real basins of the apparatus and environment;
7. Delayed choice is a boundary change;
8. Entanglement is geometric non-factorization in the configuration space.

What remains for metrology:

1. calculate $\text{R}_{\rm app}$ for real apparatuses;
2. derive $\Gamma_{\rm det}$ from specific materials;
3. simulate dynamical basins of real detectors;
4. test Bell/no-signalling with multiparticle Hessian and concrete apparatuses;
5. classify non-Hermitian regimes when the apparatus is dissipative.

This list does not reopen the operational Born rule or the measurement structure. It defines the work necessary to compare GDQ with specific experiments at the level of engineering and metrology.
