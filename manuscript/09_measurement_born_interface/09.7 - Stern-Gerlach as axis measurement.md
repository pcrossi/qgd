# 09.7. Stern-Gerlach as axis measurement

Stern--Gerlach is the clean prototype of a measurement in GDQ. The object already possesses circulation/orientation. The apparatus does not create spin; it selects an axis.

If the local magnetic field defines:

$$
\mathbf n=\frac{\mathbf B}{|\mathbf B|},
$$

the two channels of the apparatus are:

$$
P_{\mathbf n}^{\pm}
=
\frac12
\left(I\pm\mathbf n\cdot\sigma\right).
$$

For a preparation with orientation $\mathbf a$, the operational rule gives:

$$
p_\pm(\mathbf n|\mathbf a)
=
\frac{1\pm\mathbf a\cdot\mathbf n}{2}.
$$

If $\theta$ is the angle between preparation and apparatus:

$$
p_+=\cos^2\frac\theta2,
\qquad
p_-=\sin^2\frac\theta2.
$$

The physical point is:

$$
\text{two channels do not mean equal weights.}
$$

The weights depend on the preparation and the direction chosen by the apparatus. The classical field acts on the boundary of the soliton and breaks the degeneracy between the two channels relative to $\mathbf n$.
