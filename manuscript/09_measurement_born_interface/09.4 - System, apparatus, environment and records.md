# 09.4. System, apparatus, environment and records

A real measurement is not just an algebraic projection. It involves a system, an apparatus, and an environment:

$$
S+A+E.
$$

The system contains the degree of freedom to be distinguished. The apparatus contains macroscopic pointer states. The environment carries the degrees of freedom that make these pointers stable and practically irreversible.

In the ideal reduced model:

$$
|s_i\rangle|A_0\rangle|E_0\rangle
\longmapsto
|s_i\rangle|A_i\rangle|E_i\rangle.
$$

For:

$$
|\psi\rangle=\sum_i c_i|s_i\rangle,
$$

the total evolution produces:

$$
|\Psi_{SAE}\rangle
=
\sum_i c_i|s_i\rangle|A_i\rangle|E_i\rangle.
$$

If the records and environments are distinguishable,

$$
\langle A_j|A_i\rangle\simeq\delta_{ij},
\qquad
\langle E_j|E_i\rangle\simeq\delta_{ij},
$$

then the reduced state of $S+A$ loses the interference terms:

$$
\rho_{SA}
\simeq
\sum_i |c_i|^2
|s_i,A_i\rangle\langle s_i,A_i|.
$$

This expression explains why we observe a diagonal distribution of records. It also explains repeatability: measuring the same pointer again returns the same macroscopic sector.

But it is still not, on its own, an ontological selection of a single branch. In GDQ, the individual outcome is treated as a dynamical selection of a real basin in the microgeometry of the apparatus and environment.

The complete technical construction does not use an imported measurement Hamiltonian as a new ontology. The effective measurement operator is the physical Hessian of the official action on the background with the source and boundary of the apparatus:

$$
\mathcal H_{\rm meas}
=
P^{\rm phys}
\operatorname{Hess}_{\Phi_\ast}
\mathcal S_{\rm GDQ}^{S+A+E}
P^{\rm phys}.
$$

The records are spectral sectors/basins of this reduced operator. The asymptotic proof is in
[[notes/asymptotic_theorem_gdq_records|Asymptotic theorem of GDQ records]].
