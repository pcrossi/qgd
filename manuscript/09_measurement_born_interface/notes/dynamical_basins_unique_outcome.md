# Dynamical basins and unique outcome

## Statement

The Born rule provides the operational distribution of records. An individual event requires a real dynamics of the apparatus and the environment that selects a macroscopic basin:

$$
\Phi_{A+E}(t)\to\mathcal B_i.
$$

## Status

Conditional theorem/effective reduction. The conclusion requires:

1. stable macroscopic basins;
2. regular microscopic noise;
3. basin boundaries of measure zero;
4. compatibility of input weights with the Born measure;
5. sufficient isolation of the final records.

## Argument

After the $S+A+E$ coupling, the reduced distribution becomes diagonal:

$$
\rho_{SA}
\simeq
\sum_i |c_i|^2
|s_i,A_i\rangle\langle s_i,A_i|.
$$

This diagonalization provides record probabilities, but does not select an individual branch. For a real apparatus, each record corresponds to an attracting region:

$$
\mathcal B_i
=
\{\Phi_{A+E}:\Phi_{A+E}(t\to\infty)\to R_i\}.
$$

If the boundaries between basins have measure zero, then almost every initial microconfiguration ends in a single basin. The frequency observed over many repetitions is:

$$
p_i
=
\mu_{\rm micro}(\mathcal B_i).
$$

Born compatibility requires:

$$
\mu_{\rm micro}(\mathcal B_i)=|c_i|^2.
$$

When this compatibility is satisfied by the apparatus, the theory produces a unique outcome and the Born distribution.

## What remains conditional

For real apparatuses, it is necessary to calculate the Hessian, the interface response, the dissipation, and the basins. Without this calculation, the unique outcome remains a real dynamical hypothesis, not a new postulate of the official action.
