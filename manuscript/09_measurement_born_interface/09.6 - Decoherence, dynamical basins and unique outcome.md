# 09.6. Decoherence, dynamical basins and unique outcome

Decoherence explains why interference between macroscopic records disappears. It does not select on its own which record actually occurs in an individual measurement.

In GDQ, the individual selection is formulated as a dynamical basin problem. The apparatus and the environment possess many microconfigurations. Each macroscopic outcome corresponds to a stable basin:

$$
\mathcal B_i
\subset
\text{microconfigurations of }A+E.
$$

A preparation with coefficients $c_i$ defines operational weights:

$$
p_i=|c_i|^2.
$$

But an individual event corresponds to a real trajectory of the microgeometry that enters one of the basins:

$$
\Phi_{A+E}(t)\longrightarrow\mathcal B_i.
$$

This distinction is important:

$$
\text{distribution of records}
\ne
\text{individual event mechanism}.
$$

The chapter assumes the conservative formulation: Born provides the operational distribution; the unique outcome requires the dynamical hypothesis that the real basins of the apparatus and environment select a record. This hypothesis is physical and testable by detector models. It does not alter the official action.
