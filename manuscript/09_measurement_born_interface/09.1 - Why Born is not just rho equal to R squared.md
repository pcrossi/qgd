# 09.1. Why Born is not just rho equal to R squared

The first point is simple, but must be made explicit. In the regular sector of GDQ, we can write:

$$
\Psi=\sqrt\rho\,e^{iS_R/\hbar}.
$$

Then:

$$
|\Psi|^2=\rho.
$$

This is necessary for any probabilistic interpretation. However, it is not the complete Born rule. It is only the local identification of the geometric density with the square of the amplitude in the position representation.

The complete Born rule must answer stronger questions:

1. why mutually exclusive alternatives sum probabilities;
2. why an experimental basis is selected;
3. how to calculate probabilities in bases other than position;
4. how to compose systems;
5. how the apparatus produces stable records.

In GDQ, the local density comes first. The operational rule comes later, when the physical sector is reconstructed as a Hilbert space and when the apparatus defines which alternatives are actually measured.

## The error to avoid

We must not write:

$$
\rho=R^2
\quad\Rightarrow\quad
\text{Born is proven.}
$$

The correct way is:

$$
\rho=|\Psi|^2
\quad
\text{in the regular position sector,}
$$

and:

$$
\mu(P)=\operatorname{Tr}(\varrho P)
$$

for operational alternatives represented by projectors in the physical Hilbert space.

The first formula states what the local geometric density is. The second states how an apparatus assigns probabilities to possible outcomes.
