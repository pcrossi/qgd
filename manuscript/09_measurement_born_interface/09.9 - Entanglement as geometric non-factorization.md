# 09.9. Entanglement as geometric non-factorization

Entanglement must not be described as an instantaneous signal between two laboratories. In GDQ, the multiparticle state is a geometric solution in the configuration space.

For two systems:

$$
Q_2=M_{\rm loc}\times M_{\rm loc}.
$$

Separability means:

$$
\rho_{AB}(x_A,x_B)
=
\rho_A(x_A)\rho_B(x_B),
$$

and:

$$
S_{AB}(x_A,x_B)
=
S_A(x_A)+S_B(x_B).
$$

Entanglement means that at least one of these factorizations fails:

$$
\rho_{AB}\ne\rho_A\rho_B
\quad
\text{or}
\quad
S_{AB}\ne S_A+S_B.
$$

The language of Mayer--Vietoris is useful for describing the phase gluing between open sets:

$$
Q_2=U_A\cup U_B.
$$

On the intersection, the local phases must satisfy a compatibility condition:

$$
\theta_A|_{U_A\cap U_B}
-
\theta_B|_{U_A\cap U_B}
=
d\chi.
$$

If the global class prevents factorization, we have geometric correlation. This does not authorize asserting zero physical distance between laboratories. The future operational requirement is to prove, for real apparatuses, no-signalling:

$$
P(a|x,y)=P(a|x),
\qquad
P(b|x,y)=P(b|y).
$$

In the reconstructed projective sector, the reduced target that GDQ must reproduce for two spinorial channels is:

$$
P(s,t|\boldsymbol a,\boldsymbol b)
=
\frac14
\left(
1
-
st\,\boldsymbol a\cdot\boldsymbol b
\right),
\qquad
s,t\in\{-1,+1\}.
$$

Hence:

$$
E(\boldsymbol a,\boldsymbol b)
=
-
\boldsymbol a\cdot\boldsymbol b,
$$

but:

$$
\sum_tP(s,t|\boldsymbol a,\boldsymbol b)
=
\frac12,
\qquad
\sum_sP(s,t|\boldsymbol a,\boldsymbol b)
=
\frac12.
$$

In other words: the correlation depends on both axes, but the local marginal does not carry the distant choice.
