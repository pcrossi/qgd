# 23.4. Physical walls and impedance

A real wall is not Dirichlet imposed by decree. It possesses internal degrees of freedom. Upon eliminating them variationally, the well mode sees a boundary impedance.

If $Y$ is the interface and $I$ is the material interior of the wall, the partitioned physical Hessian is:

$$
K
=
\begin{pmatrix}
K_{YY} & K_{YI}\\
K_{IY} & K_{II}
\end{pmatrix}.
$$

Eliminating the internal modes:

$$
\mathsf R_{\rm wall}
=
K_{YY}
-
K_{YI}K_{II}^{-1}K_{IY}.
$$

For a reduced homogeneous wall of height $V_0$, thickness $d$, and energy $E<V_0$, the impedance is:

$$
\lambda(E)
=
\sqrt{V_0-E}\,
\coth\left(d\sqrt{V_0-E}\right).
$$

The ideal condition $R=0$ appears in the limit of infinite impedance. For finite impedance, the energy shifts because the mode physically penetrates the wall.

The numerical test in this chapter compares:

1. Robin/DtN spectrum;
2. direct diagonalization of the finite barrier;
3. ideal infinite well.
