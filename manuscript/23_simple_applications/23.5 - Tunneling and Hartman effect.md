# 23.5. Tunneling and Hartman effect

Consider a one-dimensional barrier:

$$
\Omega_L=[0,L],
\qquad
V_0>E.
$$

Inside the barrier:

$$
\psi(x)=\psi_0e^{-\kappa x},
\qquad
\kappa=\frac{\sqrt{2m(V_0-E)}}{\hbar}.
$$

Therefore:

$$
\rho(x)=\rho_0e^{-2\kappa x}.
$$

In the stationary evanescent sector, with frozen transverse coordinates and longitudinal gauge:

$$
g_{xx}(x)=g_0\frac{\rho(x)}{\rho_0}
=
g_0e^{-2\kappa x}.
$$

This relation is not a universal metric identity. It is a reduced solution for the evanescent channel.

The proper distance inside the barrier is:

$$
D_{\rm prop}(L)
=
\int_0^L\sqrt{g_{xx}(x)}\,dx
=
\frac{\sqrt{g_0}}{\kappa}
\left(1-e^{-\kappa L}\right).
$$

Therefore:

$$
\lim_{L\to\infty}D_{\rm prop}(L)
=
\frac{\sqrt{g_0}}{\kappa}.
$$

The Hartman effect is the saturation of this reduced proper distance. It does not imply superluminal front velocity.

For comparison with literature, one also uses:

$$
\tau_W(E)=
\hbar\frac{\partial}{\partial E}\arg T(E).
$$

Real wavepackets require integrating the spectrum:

$$
\Psi_T(x,t)
=
\int T(E)A(E)e^{i(kx-\omega t)}\,dE.
$$

The peak advance by spectral filtering is not causal information transport above $c$.
