# 27.2 — Universal GDQ calculation pipeline

The universal pipeline is:

$$
\mathcal S_{\rm GDQ}
\to
\Phi_*
\to
C_a[\Phi]=0
\to
P_{\rm phys}
\to
K_{\rm phys}
\to
J_{\rm app}
\to
\delta\Phi
\to
\mathsf R_{\rm app}
\to
\mathcal O_{\rm obs}.
$$

Here:

- $\Phi_*$ is the stationary background;
- $C_a[\Phi]=0$ are constraints on charge, flux, normalization, gauge, or boundary;
- $P_{\rm phys}$ is the projector onto the physical sector;
- $K_{\rm phys}$ is the physical Hessian;
- $J_{\rm app}$ is the source, apparatus, or probe;
- $\delta\Phi$ is the linear response;
- $\mathsf R_{\rm app}$ is the interface impedance;
- $\mathcal O_{\rm obs}$ is the observable.

The physical Hessian is:

$$
K_{\rm phys}
=
P_{\rm phys}
\operatorname{Hess}_{\Phi_*}\mathcal S_{\rm GDQ}
P_{\rm phys}.
$$

In the linear regime:

$$
\delta\Phi
=
K_{\rm phys}^{-1}J_{\rm app}.
$$

When internal degrees of freedom are eliminated:

$$
\mathsf R_{\rm app}
=
K_{\partial\partial}
-
K_{\partial I}K_{II}^{-1}K_{I\partial}.
$$

This expression reappears in physical walls, detectors, slits, protons, nuclei, horizons, and cosmological boundaries. Because of this, it must be implemented as a common block and not reinvented case-by-case.

The minimal algebraic block is always the same. First, one constructs the matrix of linearized constraints:

$$
D C(\Phi_*)\,\delta\Phi=0.
$$

Then, one builds the orthogonal projector, under the quadratic metric used by the Hessian, to remove forbidden variations:

$$
P_{\rm phys}
=
I
-
G^{-1}D C^\dagger
\left(D C\,G^{-1}D C^\dagger\right)^{-1}
D C.
$$

Next, the physical Hessian is compressed:

$$
K_{\rm phys}
=
P_{\rm phys}^\dagger K P_{\rm phys}.
$$

Finally, if an internal sector $I$ is not directly observed, it is eliminated by Schur complement:

$$
K_{\rm eff}
=
K_{\partial\partial}
-
K_{\partial I}K_{II}^{-1}K_{I\partial}.
$$

This is the exact same structural calculation used for wall impedance, magnetic response, detector, form factor, horizon, and nuclear channel. What changes from one problem to another is the background, the domain, the constraints, and the source; not the variational skeleton.
