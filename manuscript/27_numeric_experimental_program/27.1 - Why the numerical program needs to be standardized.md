# 27.1 — Why the numerical program needs to be standardized

A broad geometric theory can generate many scripts. Without a protocol, scripts become ambiguous evidence: one might be a fixture, another a benchmark, another an adjustment, another a blind prediction. The reader needs to know the difference.

GDQ requires that each calculation declares:

1. the background used;
2. the operator evaluated;
3. the constraints imposed;
4. the boundary;
5. the observable extracted;
6. whether experimental data was introduced prior to the comparison.

The goal is not to preserve all historical attempts. The manuscript must preserve the final or reduced versions that document the correct construction. Failed paths belong to the work history, not to the positive narrative.

The operational principle is:

$$
\boxed{
\text{reproducibility before interpretation.}
}
$$
