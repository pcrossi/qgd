# 25.2 — Black holes as solitons with horizon

In the GDQ reading, a regular black hole is a geometric soliton of density, torsion, and curvature that possesses a horizon.

It is not introduced as an arbitrary external metric. The correct chain is:

$$
\mathcal S_{\rm GDQ}
\to
\Phi_{\rm BH,*}
\to
T_{\mu\nu}^{\rm GDQ}
\to
g_{\mu\nu}^{\rm eff}
\to
\text{horizons}
\to
K_{\rm BH}^{\rm phys}.
$$

The minimal effective ansatz used to organize the radial reduction is:

$$
ds^2
=
-e^{2\Phi(r)}A(r)c^2dt^2
+
A(r)^{-1}dr^2
+
r^2d\Omega^2.
$$

With:

$$
A(r)
=
1-\frac{2Gm(r)}{c^2r}.
$$

The anti-singular mechanism is simple. If the central effective density is regular:

$$
\epsilon_{\rm GDQ}(r)
=
\epsilon_0+O(r^2),
$$

then:

$$
m(r)
=
\frac{4\pi\epsilon_0}{3c^2}r^3+O(r^5).
$$

Therefore:

$$
A(r)
=
1-\frac{\Lambda_{\rm core}}{3}r^2+O(r^4).
$$

The center ceases to be a singular Schwarzschild metric and becomes a regular de Sitter-type core, sustained by density, Bohm pressure, and torsion. The physically relevant condition is not just the presence of a horizon; it is having a horizon without curvature divergence.

In the tested reduction, the core was not imposed by a phenomenological mass profile. It was first obtained as a reduced radial saddle for $u(r)=\sqrt\rho$, and only afterwards reconstructed as an effective metric. The solved equation was:

$$
u'=v,
$$

$$
v'
=
2(\phi+\lambda_Tu^2-\mu)u
-\frac{2}{r}v,
$$

$$
\phi'=\frac{M(r)}{r^2},
\qquad
M'=r^2u^2.
$$

The regularity and normalization conditions were:

$$
u'(0)=0,
\qquad
M(0)=0,
\qquad
u(R)=0,
\qquad
M(R)=1,
\qquad
\phi(R)=-\frac1R.
$$

The reduced eigenvalue found was:

$$
\mu=-1.067957044153\times10^{-1}.
$$

With this, the core presented:

$$
M(r)\sim r^{3.00002651}.
$$

For effective compactness $\eta=8$, two horizons emerge:

$$
r_{H,1}=4.222352820613,
\qquad
r_{H,2}=15.957122727991.
$$

The reduced central densities and pressures were:

$$
\epsilon_{\rm core}
=
9.934478711421\times10^{-3},
$$

$$
p_{r,\rm core}
=
-9.934478711373\times10^{-3},
$$

$$
p_{t,\rm core}
=
-9.934159730822\times10^{-3}.
$$

Thus, NEC/WEC are saturated in the core and SEC is violated. This is exactly the signature expected to prevent the classical singularity without introducing exotic matter external to GDQ.
