# 25.1 — A single action in different boundary regimes

GDQ does not add one sector for atoms, another for nuclei, another for galaxies, and another for cosmology. The physical action remains the same:

$$
\mathcal S_{\rm GDQ}
=
\int_\gamma
\left[
\int_{\mathcal M_{\mathbb C}}
\frac{\hbar}{\Lambda_C^2}
\mathcal L_{\rm GDQ}
\mathcal U\sqrt{\det g}\,d^{2n}z
\right]
\frac{d\tau}{\tau}.
$$

What changes is the boundary regime. In an atom, the relevant boundary can be the effective surface of the proton. In a black hole, the relevant boundary includes horizons. In cosmology, the boundary is global: horizon, observable volume, charge normalization, and average vacuum tension.

The chain used in this chapter is:

$$
\mathcal S_{\rm GDQ}
\to
\Phi_*^{\rm global}
\to
K_{\rm global}^{\rm phys}
\to
\text{boundary}
\to
\text{observable}.
$$

With:

$$
\text{Hessian boundary correction: } K_{\rm global}^{\rm phys} = P_{\rm phys} \operatorname{Hess}_{\Phi_*^{\rm global}}\mathcal S_{\rm GDQ} P_{\rm phys}.
$$

The physical projector removes coordinate variations, gauge modes, modes incompatible with charge/flux, and fluctuations that do not preserve the causal class of the problem.

The central phrase of the chapter is:

$$
\boxed{
\text{GDQ projects the same geometry onto different boundary regimes.}
}
$$

Therefore, the results below must not be read as new astrophysical actions. They are reductions or boundary evaluations of the official action.
