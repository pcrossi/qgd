# Note — Electroweak scale and proton radius

The candidate geometric electroweak scale is:

$$
v_{\rm GDQ}
=
M_p\frac{6\pi^5}{7}.
$$

It is the global dimensional normalization of the electroweak mode. The dimensionless mode comes from the potential:

$$
\mathcal V(\beta)
=
\frac{1}{2}a_2\beta^2
+
\frac{1}{4}a_4\beta^4,
$$

with:

$$
\beta_\ast^2
=
-\frac{a_2}{a_4}.
$$

For:

$$
a_2=-0.253196676,
\qquad
a_4=2133.554507,
$$

we obtain:

$$
\beta_\ast=0.0108937431.
$$

The dimensional connection is:

$$
v=\sqrt{Z_\beta}\,\beta_\ast.
$$

It must be distinguished from the auxiliary formula:

$$
v_K
=
\frac{M_e}{\alpha}
\left(
1-\frac{3}{4\pi^2}
\right)^{-1/2},
$$

which only produces a scale of tens of MeV.

At the current reduced level:

$$
v_{\rm GDQ}
=
246.111195996\,{\rm GeV},
$$

with an error of $-0.044048\%$ against the operational scale extracted from $G_F$.

The structural proton radius is:

$$
r_p^{\rm surf}
=
\frac{1}{8}
\left(
1+\frac{\alpha}{4}
\right)
\epsilon_{\rm eff}
\frac{3\Lambda_C}{2}.
$$

This expression comes from the decomposition:

$$
r_p^{\rm surf}
=
C_r\epsilon_{\rm eff}R_B,
$$

with:

$$
C_r
=
\frac{1}{8}
\left(
1+\frac{\alpha}{4}
\right),
\qquad
R_B
=
\frac{3}{2} \Lambda_C.
$$

For:

$$
\alpha^{-1}=137.035999084,
\qquad
\epsilon_{\rm eff}=0.011591040463,
\qquad
\Lambda_C=386.159268\,{\rm fm},
$$

we have:

$$
C_r=0.125228042267790,
\qquad
R_B=579.238902\,{\rm fm},
$$

and:

$$
\boxed{
r_p^{\rm surf}
=
0.840778765432\,{\rm fm}.
}
$$

## Form factor and observed radius

The charge radius observed by scattering is defined by the slope of the form factor:

$$
F_p(q^2)
=
1-\frac{q^2r_p^2}{6}
+O(q^4).
$$

The finite size in $ns$ states enters through:

$$
\Delta E_{\rm fs}(ns)
=
\frac{2\pi}{3}
Z\alpha\hbar c\,
r_p^2
|\psi_{ns}(0)|^2.
$$

Since:

$$
|\psi_{ns}(0)|^2
=
\frac{(Z\alpha\mu c/\hbar)^3}{\pi n^3},
$$

it follows:

$$
\Delta E_{\rm fs}(ns)
\propto
\mu^3r_p^2.
$$

This is the structural reason why muonic hydrogen is much more sensitive to the proton radius than electronic hydrogen.

The radius measured by a probe is:

$$
r_p^{\rm eff}[\ell]
=
r_p^{\rm surf}
+
\delta r_p[\ell],
$$

with:

$$
\delta r_p[\ell]
=
-
\left(H_p^{\rm surf}\right)^{-1}J_{p,\ell}.
$$

Thus, differences between electronic and muonic hydrogen belong to the probe--proton interface problem.

In the contact limit of $s$ states:

$$
\frac{\delta r_p[e]}{\delta r_p[\mu]}
=
\left(
\frac{\mu_{ep}}{\mu_{\mu p}}
\right)^3
=
1.555489846615637\times10^{-7}.
$$

The fine metrological closure requires directly calculating $H_p^{\rm surf}$, $J_{p,e}$, and $J_{p,\mu}$ from the official action, without adjusting these blocks using an experimental radius.
