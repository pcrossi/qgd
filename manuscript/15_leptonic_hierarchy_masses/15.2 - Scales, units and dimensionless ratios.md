# 15.2. Scales, units and dimensionless ratios

A normalized geometric operator provides dimensionless eigenvalues:

$$
\widehat L\phi_n
=
\widehat\lambda_n\phi_n.
$$

To convert this to physical energy, a scale is needed:

$$
M_n c^2
=
E_0\sqrt{\widehat\lambda_n}.
$$

With:

$$
E_0
=
\frac{\hbar c}{\ell_0}.
$$

Thus:

$$
\frac{M_i}{M_j}
=
\sqrt{
\frac{\widehat\lambda_i}{\widehat\lambda_j}
}.
$$

This is the natural object of the theory. The number $0.511\,\mathrm{MeV}$ depends on how the laboratory defines the unit of energy. Therefore, the chapter must speak of dimensionless ratios.

Using $M_e$ as the metrological standard is acceptable when the prediction is:

$$
M_\mu/M_e,
\qquad
M_\tau/M_e.
$$

It would be circular only if $M_\mu$ or $M_\tau$ were used in the construction.

There is also an independent metrological bridge, constructed in the baryonic sector: the endpoint of free beta decay satisfies

$$
Q_\beta=(\delta_B-1)M_ec^2.
$$

Thus, once $\delta_B$ is derived, the experimental data $Q_\beta$ determines an electronic scale:

$$
M_ec^2=\frac{Q_\beta}{\delta_B-1}.
$$

This bridge does not replace the general rule that the theory predicts dimensionless ratios; it shows how a dimensional scale can be inherited from a physical metrological boundary.
