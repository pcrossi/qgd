# 15.8. 8D Hessian and inheritance by Schur

The three-dimensional reduction is not a naive application of Perelman to a general 8D manifold. The argument is sectorial.

In the factored sector:

$$
M_8=B_3\times K_5.
$$

The factor $B_3$ sustains the material curvature/tension. The factor $K_5$ classifies charge, phase, spin, and holonomy.

The second variation of the official action on the stationary leptonic background $\Phi_\ell$ defines the Hessian:

$$
K_{\rm GDQ}^{\ell}
=
\left.
\delta^2\mathcal S_{\rm GDQ}
\right|_{\Phi_\ell}.
$$

This Hessian still contains redundant variations: infinitesimal diffeomorphisms, gauge modes of the complex structure, variations that violate the normalization of $\mathcal U$, and variations that change the Cauchy charge of the defect. Therefore, the physical operator is the projected one:

$$
K_{\rm phys}^{\ell}
=
P_{\rm phys}^{\dagger}
K_{\rm GDQ}^{\ell}
P_{\rm phys}.
$$

Here $P_{\rm phys}$ is the projector onto fluctuations that preserve:

1. the normalization of the measure;
2. the charge/circulation of the stoma;
3. the Noether flux;
4. the topological class of the defect;
5. the admissible boundary conditions.

When $K_5$ is flat and there are no active mixed modes, the physical Hessian separates:

$$
H_8
=
\begin{pmatrix}
H_B & J\\
J^\dagger & H_\perp
\end{pmatrix}.
$$

The effective operator in the material sector is:

To see where it comes from, we separate a physical fluctuation as:

$$
\delta\Phi_{\rm phys}
=
\xi_B\oplus\eta_\perp.
$$

Here $\xi_B$ alters the observable material tension and $\eta_\perp$ represents internal transverse fluctuations. The quadratic form is:

$$
Q[\xi_B,\eta_\perp]
=
\langle\xi_B,H_B\xi_B\rangle
+2\operatorname{Re}\langle\xi_B,J\eta_\perp\rangle
+\langle\eta_\perp,H_\perp\eta_\perp\rangle.
$$

If $H_\perp$ is positive in the transverse physical subspace, the variational elimination of $\eta_\perp$ yields:

$$
H_\perp\eta_\perp
+J^\dagger\xi_B
=0,
$$

therefore:

$$
\eta_\perp
=
-H_\perp^{-1}J^\dagger\xi_B.
$$

Substituting this stationary solution into $Q$, we obtain:

$$
H_B^{\rm eff}
=
H_B-JH_\perp^{-1}J^\dagger.
$$

If $H_\perp$ is coercive and the mixture is subcritical, the critical index of the 8D sector is inherited from the 3D sector.

On the product background:

$$
J=0.
$$

Thus:

$$
H_B^{\rm eff}=H_B.
$$

So:

$$
R_\ell^{(8)}=R_\ell^{(0)}.
$$

This is the product 8D closure of the leptonic hierarchy.

The physical meaning is simple: in the stationary product, the internal degrees classify the particle but do not drain stiffness from the material tension. Therefore, the hierarchy calculated on the three-dimensional support is transported to the 8D bulk without correction. In warped/mixed backgrounds, this conclusion is no longer automatic and must be verified by the size of the mixing block $J$.
