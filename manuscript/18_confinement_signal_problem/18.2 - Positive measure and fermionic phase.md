# 18.2. Positive measure and fermionic phase

The decomposition:

$$
f
=
-\ln\rho
+
i\frac{S_R}{\hbar}
$$

separates two functions:

1. $\rho$ defines the sampling measure;
2. $S_R$ defines the fermionic monodromy.

This allows writing benchmarks with positive weight:

$$
\rho_{\rm MC}
\propto
e^{-\beta E_{\rm GDQ}}.
$$

Antiferromagnetic correlation, for example, does not need to arise from a negative weight. It can arise from staggered circulation and holonomy.

The closed point is structural:

$$
\boxed{
\text{GDQ reformulates the signal as a phase over a positive measure.}
}
$$

The non-closed point is general algorithmic:

$$
\boxed{
\text{this does not prove polynomial complexity for all fermionic systems.}
}
$$
