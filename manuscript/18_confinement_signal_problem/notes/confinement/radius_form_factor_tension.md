# Effective radius, form factor, and tension

This note separates three levels:

1. primitive cap with $r_\perp=0.86\,{\rm fm}$;
2. canonical surface radius;
3. compressed probe radius.

None of these radii is adjusted using the hadronic tension.

## 1. Canonical Surface Radius

The consolidated geometric chain provides:

$$
r_p
=
C_r\epsilon_{\rm eff}R_B.
$$

With:

$$
\epsilon_{\rm eff}
=
\frac{5\alpha}{\pi}
-
\left(
\frac49\alpha^2-\frac\pi2\alpha^3
\right),
$$

$$
C_r
=
\frac18
\left(
1+\frac\alpha4
\right),
$$

and:

$$
R_B=\frac32\Lambda_C.
$$

Using:

$$
\epsilon_{\rm eff}=0.011591040463,
\qquad
\Lambda_C=386.159268\,{\rm fm},
$$

we have:

$$
R_B=579.238902000000\,{\rm fm},
$$

and:

$$
r_p=0.840778765450\,{\rm fm}.
$$

## 2. Confinement Form Factor

The primitive cap uses:

$$
r_\perp=0.86\,{\rm fm}.
$$

The form factor associated with the canonical radius is:

$$
F_{\rm shape}
=
\left(
\frac{r_\perp}{r_p}
\right)^2
=
1.046245090518.
$$

Then:

$$
\sigma_{\rm GDQ}
=
F_{\rm shape}
\pi\frac{\hbar c}{r_\perp^2}
=
\pi\frac{\hbar c}{r_p^2}.
$$

Numerically:

$$
\sigma_{\rm GDQ}
=
0.876946044304\,{\rm GeV/fm}
=
0.173045114896\,{\rm GeV}^2.
$$

Hence:

$$
\sqrt{\sigma_{\rm GDQ}}
=
0.415986916737\,{\rm GeV}.
$$

Subsequent comparison with $\sigma_{\rm had}\simeq0.89\,{\rm GeV/fm}$:

$$
-1.466737\%.
$$

## 3. Compressed Probe Radius

The effective compressed radius:

$$
r_{\rm eff}=0.8354\,{\rm fm}
$$

generates:

$$
F_{\rm shape,eff}
=
\left(
\frac{0.86}{0.8354}
\right)^2
=
1.059761067152,
$$

and:

$$
\sigma_{\rm eff}
=
0.888274921594\,{\rm GeV/fm}.
$$

The subsequent deviation is:

$$
-0.193829\%.
$$

## 4. Status

The canonical radius closes the tension scale at the structural level. The compressed radius closes it almost metrologically, but must be treated as a probe boundary until re-derived in the same transverse background of the official action.
