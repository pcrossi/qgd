# 18.4. Reduced benchmark of fermionic systems

The reduced benchmark of the signal problem used a cold lattice $L=4$ and a positive measure. The core results were:

| Quantity | Value |
|---|---:|
| exact configurations | $65536$ |
| exact $C_s(1)$ | $-0.1698717343244$ |
| MC $C_s(1)$ | $-0.16836$ |
| acceptance | $0.75515$ |
| observed scaling | $\tau_{\rm corr}\sim N^{0.934}$ |

The antiferromagnetic signal correctly appears. The comparison with external data is partial: the cold order of magnitude is compatible, but the thermal map and correlation length require the full device/bath.

Therefore, the correct reading is:

$$
\boxed{
\text{positive and useful benchmark;}
\qquad
\text{not a general proof of polynomial algorithm.}
}
$$

The preserved reduced physical benchmark also compares the Schur curve with experimental/digitized cold atom points. The strongest result is the intermediate point:

| $k_BT/t$ | Experimental $C_s(1)$ | GDQ--Schur $C_s(1)$ | deviation |
|---:|---:|---:|---:|
| $0.45$ | $-0.210\pm0.020$ | $-0.210714$ | $-0.036\sigma$ |

On the complete curve, the signal remains correct, but there are residuals in the cold limit and at high temperature. This preserves the conservative conclusion: the reduced sector is an operational validation of the positive measure and fermionic holonomy, not a universal metrological solution.
