# 18.3. Holonomy knots and surgical interfaces

The reduced computational path replaces continuous surgery with coupled domains:

$$
M^\ast
=
\bigcup_a U_a.
$$

The interfaces are:

$$
\Sigma_{ab}
=
U_a\cap U_b.
$$

In each interface, a transmission/reflection matrix is used:

$$
\mathsf S_{ab}
=
\begin{pmatrix}
\mathsf R_a & \mathsf T_{ba}\\
\mathsf T_{ab} & \mathsf R_b
\end{pmatrix}.
$$

In the closed sector:

$$
\mathsf S_{ab}^{\dagger}\mathsf S_{ab}=I.
$$

In the open sector:

$$
\mathsf S_{ab}^{\dagger}\mathsf S_{ab}\le I.
$$

In the reduced benchmark, $\mathsf S$ was not inserted as an external quantum operator. The path used was:

$$
K_{\rm GDQ}
\to
P_{\rm phys}K_{\rm GDQ}P_{\rm phys}
\to
\Lambda_{\rm DtN}
\to
\mathsf Z_{\Sigma}
\to
\mathsf S_{\Sigma}.
$$

Here, $K_{\rm GDQ}$ is the second-variation Hessian of the official action in the interface background, $P_{\rm phys}$ removes gauge modes and null constraint modes, $\Lambda_{\rm DtN}$ is the Dirichlet-to-Neumann operator induced on the boundary, and $\mathsf Z_{\Sigma}$ is the reduced Hermitian impedance of the interface. The matrix $\mathsf S$ was then obtained by Cayley transform. The unitarity error remained in machine order:

$$
\max\|\mathsf S^\dagger\mathsf S-I\|
\simeq
2.61\times10^{-16}.
$$
