# 24.3 — Surface Hessian, Schur and alpha projector

The composite nucleus possesses internal degrees of freedom and surface degrees of freedom. To calculate the alpha channel, we divide the physical Hessian into blocks:

$$
K^{\rm phys}
=
\begin{pmatrix}
K_{II} & K_{I\partial}\\
K_{\partial I} & K_{\partial\partial}
\end{pmatrix}.
$$

The block $I$ represents eliminated internal modes. The block $\partial$ represents modes accessible to the alpha–nucleus boundary. The correct variational elimination is the Schur complement:

$$
K_{\partial}^{\rm phys}
=
K_{\partial\partial}
-
K_{\partial I}K_{II}^{-1}K_{I\partial}.
$$

This operator is the physical impedance of the channel. It contains the proper rigidity of the surface and the feedback from the internal modes.

The alpha cluster is not selected by the lowest abstract eigenvalue. It is selected by charge, circulation, and overlap with the alpha configuration. The correct projector is the Riesz spectral projector:

$$
P_\alpha
=
\frac{1}{2\pi i}
\oint_{\mathcal C_\alpha}
\left(z-K_{\partial}^{\rm phys}\right)^{-1}
\,dz.
$$

The contour $\mathcal C_\alpha$ encloses the spectral band that possesses the primitive alpha overlap. If $P_{\rm daughter}$ is the projector onto the rigid subspace of the daughter nucleus, the escaping channel is:

$$
P_\perp
=
P_\alpha(1-P_{\rm daughter}).
$$

The surface energy that corrects the rate is:

$$
E_{\partial}^{\rm GDQ}
=
\left\langle
P_\perp\Phi_\alpha,
K_{\partial}^{\rm phys}
P_\perp\Phi_\alpha
\right\rangle_{\partial}.
$$

Thus:

$$
\Gamma_{\rm GDQ}
=
\nu_{\rm GDQ}
\exp\left(-E_{\partial}^{\rm GDQ}\right)
\exp\left(-W_{\rm rad}^{\rm GDQ}\right).
$$

This construction is important because it prevents a common confusion. If we directly adjust $\Delta W$ against the observed half-life, we get only reverse engineering. If we build $K_{\partial}^{\rm phys}$ and $P_\alpha$ before the comparison, the calculation becomes an evaluation of the theory.

The reduced version used in this chapter preserves exactly this Schur–Riesz structure, but with low-dimensional blocks constructed from surface curvature, shell rigidity, and Coulomb contact. The complete nuclear Hessian is left as a subsequent metrological step.

In the preserved verification, the reduced construction executes the chain:

$$
\text{channel data}
\to
K_{II},K_{I\partial},K_{\partial\partial}
\to
K_\partial^{\rm phys}
\to
P_\alpha
\to
E_\partial^{\rm GDQ}
\to
T_{1/2}.
$$
