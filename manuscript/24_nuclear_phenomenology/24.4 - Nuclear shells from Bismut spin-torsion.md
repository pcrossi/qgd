# 24.4 — Nuclear shells from Bismut spin-torsion

The alpha proof of concept only becomes physically coherent if the surface rigidity recognizes shell closures. A manual list of magic numbers does not serve as a derivation. What must appear is an angular operator.

Without torsion, the three-dimensional angular oscillator generates:

$$
2,8,20,40,70,112,\ldots
$$

This sequence is insufficient for heavy nuclei because it does not produce:

$$
28,50,82,126.
$$

In GDQ, the nuclear surface carries a Bismut connection. The reduced angular Hessian has the form:

$$
K_{\rm ang}^{B}
=
K_{\rm osc}
+
K_{L^2}
-
K_B\,\mathbf L\cdot\mathbf S.
$$

The term $\mathbf L\cdot\mathbf S$ is not imported as a Pauli operator. It is the angular reduction of the Bismut torsion on the surface of the nuclear soliton. It splits the sublevels:

$$
j=l+\frac12,
\qquad
j=l-\frac12.
$$

Each sublevel contributes with capacity:

$$
2j+1.
$$

By ordering the sublevels using the reduced angular Hessian and summing the capacities, we obtain:

$$
2,8,20,28,50,82,126.
$$

Physically, this means that shell closure is a hardening of the surface. A doubly magic daughter nucleus is not just energetically special; it has a higher boundary impedance for certain cluster channels.

This construction explains why the case $\mathrm{Po}$-$212\to\mathrm{Pb}$-$208+\alpha$ requires a determinant mobility treatment: the daughter $\mathrm{Pb}$-$208$ is doubly magic. The surface rigidity should not be read as a universal constant, but as spectral data of the block $K_{\partial}^{\rm phys}$.
