# 24.5 — Reduced alpha comparison with accepted half-lives

The alpha comparison preserved in this chapter is classified as a reduced proof of concept. It does not use the half-life to construct the operators, but still uses a reduced surface Hessian rather than the complete nuclear Hessian.

The compared observable is:

$$
\log_{10}T_{1/2}.
$$

The benchmark uses six channels:

| Channel | $\log_{10}T_{1/2}^{\rm accepted}$ | $\log_{10}T_{1/2}^{\rm GDQ,red}$ | residue |
|---|---:|---:|---:|
| U-238 | $17.149217$ | $17.224558$ | $0.075341$ |
| U-234 | $12.889155$ | $12.792212$ | $-0.096943$ |
| U-232 | $9.337323$ | $9.298479$ | $-0.038844$ |
| Th-232 | $17.646780$ | $17.708693$ | $0.061913$ |
| Ra-226 | $10.703224$ | $10.624607$ | $-0.078617$ |
| Po-212 | $-6.524329$ | $-6.556893$ | $-0.032564$ |

The RMS error in decades is:

$$
\text{RMS}
=
0.067894.
$$

Against the Gamow base with a reduced internal frequency, the diagnostic improvement is:

$$
77.619\%.
$$

This agreement is strong for a reduced chain, but the status should not be inflated. Metrological closure still requires replacing:

1. reduced blocks with the complete $K_N^{\rm phys}$;
2. reduced shell rigidity with actual eigenvalues of the nuclear Hessian;
3. reduced determinant mobility with the spectral determinant of the surface block;
4. the small dataset with a broad NUBASE/AME/ENSDF comparison.

The physical significance of the result is more important than the isolated number: the improvement appears when the alpha is treated as a boundary channel with Schur–Riesz, rather than a particle crossing a rigid universal barrier.

The script [[scripts/reduced_alpha_decay.py]] reproduces the final table. The script [[scripts/alpha_pipeline_reduced_schur_riesz.py]] reconstructs the reduced chain that generates these numbers from the surface Hessian blocks, the Schur complement, the Riesz projector, and the determinant mobility.
