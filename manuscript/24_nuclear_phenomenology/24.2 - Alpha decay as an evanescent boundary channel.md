# 24.2 — Alpha decay as an evanescent boundary channel

Alpha decay is the first natural nuclear test of GDQ because it combines three elements that the theory already possesses:

1. a composite object with a physical boundary;
2. a charged cluster channel;
3. a radial evanescent region.

The process is:

$$
(A,Z)
\longrightarrow
(A-4,Z-2)+\alpha.
$$

In the operational model, the half-life is written as:

$$
T_{1/2}
=
\frac{\ln 2}{\nu_{\rm GDQ}}
\exp(W_{\rm GDQ}).
$$

The radial exponent is:

$$
W_{\rm GDQ}
=
\frac{2}{\hbar}
\int_{r_1}^{r_2}
\sqrt{2\mu\left(V_C(r)-Q_\alpha\right)}
\sqrt{g_{rr}^{\rm eff}(r)}
\,dr.
$$

The points $r_1$ and $r_2$ are the turning points of the alpha channel. The reduced mass is $\mu$, the external Coulomb potential is $V_C$, and $Q_\alpha$ is the released energy.

In standard language, this calculation resembles Gamow. In GDQ, however, the new term is not an adjusted potential. The new term is the effective radial metric $g_{rr}^{\rm eff}$ produced by eliminating the internal degrees of freedom:

$$
g_{rr}^{\rm eff}
\quad
\text{comes from}
\quad
K_{\rm channel}^{\rm phys}.
$$

In the reduced approximation, we use a radial form compatible with an evanescent channel:

$$
g_{rr}^{\rm eff}(r)
=
1+\delta g_{rr}(r),
$$

with $\delta g_{rr}$ controlled by the surface rigidity. This form is suitable for a consistency test, but should not be confused with the complete nuclear Hessian.

The physical meaning is direct: the alpha does not cross a barrier imposed from the outside. It escapes through a boundary channel whose impedance is determined by the geometric structure of the daughter nucleus and by the circulation of the alpha cluster.

The full calculation requires:

$$
\Phi_{N,*}
\to
K_N^{\rm phys}
\to
K_{\partial}^{\rm phys}
\to
P_\alpha
\to
\Gamma_\alpha.
$$
