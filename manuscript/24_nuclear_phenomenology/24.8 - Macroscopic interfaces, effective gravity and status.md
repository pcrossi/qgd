# 24.8 — Macroscopic interfaces, effective gravity and status

The calculations in this chapter use three different levels of description. They must not be mixed.

| Level | Object | Use in this chapter |
|---|---|---|
| Fundamental GDQ | $\mathcal S_{\rm GDQ}$, $\Phi_*$, physical Hessian | origin of operators |
| Asymptotic reduction | free channels, projectors, propagators | Klein--Nishina and known limits |
| Macroscopic interface | boundary, apparatus, medium, effective gravity | normalization and refraction |

When the medium or macroscopic gravity enter, the effective form is:

$$
G_{\mu\nu}
+
\Lambda g_{\mu\nu}
=
\frac{8\pi G}{c^4}
T_{\mu\nu}^{\rm GDQ}
+
\Delta_{\mu\nu}^{\rm tor}.
$$

In the averaged macroscopic limit, with unpolarized torsion:

$$
\Delta_{\mu\nu}^{\rm tor}
\to
0.
$$

And the leading PPN coefficients are:

$$
\gamma=1,
\qquad
\beta=1.
$$

This result is sufficient to use gravity and media as effective interfaces in this chapter. It does not replace the task of calculating fine metrological corrections when the problem demands it.

The final status of the blocks is:

| Block | Status |
|---|---|
| Alpha decay | closed as a reduced GDQ proof of concept |
| Spin--torsion shells | preserved reduced derivation; complete nuclear Hessian is future work |
| Klein--Nishina | closed asymptotic reduction; complete 8D vertices are future work |
| Neutrinos | closed neutral ontology; reduced candidate masses |
| Effective gravity | structurally closed for leading macroscopic use |
| Integrated cosmology | future program for a single solver |

The chapter, therefore, does not close all nuclear metrology. It closes a more basic stage: it shows how GDQ organizes nuclear channels, asymptotic photons, and neutral neutrinos without changing its ontology.
