# 24.1 — What nuclear phenomenology means in GDQ

Nuclear phenomenology, in this manuscript, does not mean replacing GDQ with an empirical nuclear potential. The goal is more restricted and more verifiable: to start from the official action, project the admissible material background, and obtain the observable channels as responses of the physical Hessian.

The chain that will be used throughout the chapter is:

$$
\mathcal S_{\rm GDQ}
\to
\Phi_*
\to
K^{\rm phys}
\to
\text{channel operator}
\to
\text{boundary}
\to
\text{rate or cross section}.
$$

Here $\Phi_*=(g,J,H,f,\mathcal U)_*$ is the stationary background of the physical object, and:

$$
K^{\rm phys}
=
P_{\rm phys}
\operatorname{Hess}_{\Phi_*}\mathcal S_{\rm GDQ}
P_{\rm phys}.
$$

The projector $P_{\rm phys}$ removes gauge modes, pure coordinate modes, and variations incompatible with charge, flux, and normalization constraints.

Three different situations appear in this chapter:

| Phenomenon | GDQ Reading | Status |
|---|---|---|
| Alpha decay | radial evanescent channel with surface impedance | strong reduced proof of concept |
| Klein–Nishina | asymptotic scattering of the electronic Hessian | structurally closed reduction |
| Neutrinos | neutral torsional modes without localized stoma | closed ontology; reduced metrology |

The common point is that the observable does not originate from an external quantum operator. The observable originates from a probe or boundary condition applied to an existing GDQ geometry.

Physically, this changes the interpretation. The nucleus is not a point with potentials coupled for convenience. It is a composite soliton, with internal surface channels. The photon is not inserted as an abstract point particle, but appears as a massless transverse channel of the Hessian. The neutrino is not postulated as a separate fundamental field: it is the projected neutral torsional sector.

The numerical comparisons in this chapter are preserved with explicit classification. Where there is still an effective reduction, this will be stated in the text. Where the comparison only validates a known limit, this will also be stated.
