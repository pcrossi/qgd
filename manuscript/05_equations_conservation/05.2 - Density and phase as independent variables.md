# Density and phase as independent variables

The field $f$ is complex, but this does not mean that its real and imaginary parts play the same role. The constitutive decomposition of QGD is

$$
\rho=e^{-(f+\bar f)/2},
\qquad
S_R=\frac{\hbar}{2i}(f-\bar f).
$$

$\rho$ is real and positive. $S_R$ is real and has the dimension of action. In this section we will show that these two quantities can be used as independent coordinates in the field space.

## 1. Inversion of the change of variables

From the first definition,

$$
f+\bar f=-2\ln\rho.
$$

From the second,

$$
f-\bar f=\frac{2i}{\hbar}S_R.
$$

Adding and subtracting the two relationships,

$$
f=-\ln\rho+\frac{i}{\hbar}S_R,
\qquad
\bar f=-\ln\rho-\frac{i}{\hbar}S_R.
$$

Therefore, for $\rho>0$, the transformation is invertible. The positivity is not a detail: at points where $\rho=0$, $\ln\rho$ becomes singular and the domain must be treated by appropriate sectors, nodes, or boundary conditions.

## 2. Independence of the variations

Varying the inverse expressions,

$$
\delta f
=-\frac{\delta\rho}{\rho}
+\frac{i}{\hbar}\delta S_R,
$$

and

$$
\delta\bar f
=-\frac{\delta\rho}{\rho}
-\frac{i}{\hbar}\delta S_R.
$$

Conversely,

$$
\frac{\delta\rho}{\rho}
=-\frac12(\delta f+\delta\bar f),
$$

and

$$
\delta S_R
=\frac{\hbar}{2i}(\delta f-\delta\bar f).
$$

Thus, varying $f$ and $\bar f$ in a way compatible with conjugation is equivalent to independently varying $\rho$ and $S_R$. A purely real variation of $f$ changes the density; a purely imaginary variation changes the phase.

## 3. How the measure responds

The official measure is

$$
\mathcal U
=\frac{\rho}{(4\pi z_\tau)^n}.
$$

If $z_\tau$ is kept fixed,

$$
\delta\mathcal U
=\frac{\delta\rho}{(4\pi z_\tau)^n}
=\mathcal U\frac{\delta\rho}{\rho}.
$$

Therefore,

$$
\delta_{S_R}\mathcal U=0,
\qquad
\delta_\rho\mathcal U
=\mathcal U\frac{\delta\rho}{\rho}.
$$

This asymmetry explains why the phase equation has the simple form of a conservation law, while the density equation also receives the response of the measure itself.

## 4. Separation of the kinetic term

Differentiating $f$ and $\bar f$,

$$
\partial_t f
=-\partial_\mu\ln\rho
+\frac{i}{\hbar}\partial_\mu S_R,
$$

$$
\partial_{\bar\nu}\bar f
=-\partial_{\bar\nu}\ln\rho
-\frac{i}{\hbar}\partial_{\bar\nu}S_R.
$$

The product contains four parts:

$$
\begin{aligned}
\partial_\mu f\partial_{\bar\nu}\bar f
={}&
\partial_\mu\ln\rho\partial_{\bar\nu}\ln\rho
+\frac{1}{\hbar^2}
\partial_\mu S_R\partial_{\bar\nu}S_R
\\
&+\frac{i}{\hbar}
\partial_\mu\ln\rho\partial_{\bar\nu}S_R
-\frac{i}{\hbar}
\partial_\mu S_R\partial_{\bar\nu}\ln\rho.
\end{aligned}
$$

Upon contracting with a Hermitian metric and taking the real physical form, the two terms in the second line are antisymmetric conjugates and do not contribute to the real part. We obtain

$$
\operatorname{Re}\left(
g^{\mu\bar\nu}
\partial_\mu f\partial_{\bar\nu}\bar f
\right)
=g^{\mu\bar\nu}
\partial_\mu\ln\rho\partial_{\bar\nu}\ln\rho
+\frac{1}{\hbar^2}
g^{\mu\bar\nu}
\partial_\mu S_R\partial_{\bar\nu}S_R.
$$

The first part measures the geometric cost of deforming the amplitude. The second measures the cost of the phase circulation.

## 5. The real form of the integrand

Since

$$
\frac{f+\bar f}{2}=-\ln\rho,
$$

the real part of the internal density can be organized as

$$
\mathcal L_{\rho,S}
=\tau\left[
\mathcal R
+|\nabla\ln\rho|_g^2
+\frac{1}{\hbar^2}|\nabla S_R|_g^2
\right]
-\ln\rho-n.
$$

Therefore, with fixed $g$ and $z_\tau$,

$$
\operatorname{Re}\mathcal S_{\rm GDQ}
=\int_\gamma\int_M
C\mathcal U\mathcal L_{\rho,S}\,dV_g\frac{d\tau}{\tau}.
$$

This is just the official action rewritten; no Madelung term has been added. In particular, the canonical term $\rho\,\partial_tS_R$ has not yet appeared automatically. This term belongs to the dynamic reconstruction of the physical chart and should not be confused with the gradient in the Riemannian bulk.

## 6. Wave function notation

We can condense the two variables into

$$
\Psi=\sqrt\rho\,e^{iS_R/\hbar}.
$$

This writing is useful because

$$
|\Psi|^2=\rho.
$$

But $\Psi$ was not introduced as a new fundamental field. It is a representation of the two components already contained in $f$. The logical order is

$$
f
\longleftrightarrow
(\rho,S_R)
\longleftrightarrow
\Psi,
$$

and not the independent postulation of $\Psi$.

In the next section we will vary $S_R$. Since the measure does not change and the action depends only on $\nabla S_R$, a conserved current will emerge.
