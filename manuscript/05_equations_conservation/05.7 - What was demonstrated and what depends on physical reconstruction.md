# What was demonstrated and what depends on physical reconstruction

We have reached the end of the first variational pass through the official action. It is useful now to separate the results by logical strength. This separation prevents a correct equation in the bulk from being presented prematurely as a laboratory equation.

## 1. Direct results of the official action

Keeping fixed the causal boundary, the topology, $z_\tau$, and the declared Hermitian class, we demonstrated that the change

$$
f
\longleftrightarrow
(\rho,S_R)
$$

is invertible for $\rho>0$, with

$$
f=-\ln\rho+\frac{i}{\hbar}S_R.
$$

We also demonstrated that $\mathcal U$ depends on $\rho$ but not on $S_R$. This difference produces two distinct variational equations.

### Phase current

The invariance under

$$
S_R\mapsto S_R+S_0
$$

produces the current

$$
\widehat J_S^\mu
=\frac{2\tau}{\hbar\Lambda_C^2}
\mathcal U
g^{\mu\bar\nu}\partial_{\bar\nu}S_R,
$$

and the on-shell equation

$$
\nabla_\mu\widehat J_S^\mu=0.
$$

We did not just cite Noether: we derived the off-shell identity

$$
\nabla_aJ_\xi^a
=-\mathcal E_A\Delta_\xi\Phi^A,
$$

and showed that conservation follows when $\mathcal E_A=0$.

### Density equation

Varying the amplitude term and the measure simultaneously, we obtain

$$
\tau\left[
\mathcal R
+\frac{1}{\hbar^2}|\nabla S_R|_g^2
-4\frac{\Delta_g\sqrt\rho}{\sqrt\rho}
\right]
-\ln\rho-n-1
=\lambda(\tau).
$$

The ratio $\Delta_g\sqrt\rho/\sqrt\rho$ was not inserted by analogy with quantum mechanics. It arose from the weighted integration by parts. $\lambda(\tau)$ is the multiplier that preserves $\int_M\mathcal U\,dV_g=1$; it is not a phenomenological potential.

### Weighted metric equation

In the class of metric variations with fixed $f$, $\bar f$, and $z_\tau$, the bulk equation is

$$
\begin{aligned}
0={}&\tau\mathcal U
\left(
\mathcal R_{AB}+P_{AB}^{(f)}
\right)
\\
&+\tau\left(
g_{AB}\Delta_g\mathcal U
-\nabla_A\nabla_B\mathcal U
\right)
-\frac12\mathcal U
\left(\mathcal L_0-\lambda(\tau)\right)g_{AB}.
\end{aligned}
$$

The derivatives of $\mathcal U$ show that this equation is not simply Einstein's equation by another name.

### Boundaries

Each integration by parts left a normal momentum. Thus, the Dirichlet, Neumann, Robin, and Dirichlet-to-Neumann conditions belong to the variational principle. They are not details chosen after the solution.

## 2. Results identified in the physical reduction

We also demonstrated an independent differential identity:

$$
\frac{\delta}{\delta\rho}
\left[
\frac{\hbar^2}{8m}
\int\frac{|\nabla\rho|^2}{\rho}
\right]
=-\frac{\hbar^2}{2m}
\frac{\Delta\sqrt\rho}{\sqrt\rho}.
$$

Therefore, if the physical reduction normalizes the amplitude energy like the Fisher energy above, the derived operator in the bulk becomes exactly the Bohm potential.

Likewise, if the reduction produces the canonical pair

$$
\rho\,\partial_tS_R,
$$

the variations of $S_R$ and $\rho$ yield, respectively,

$$
\partial_t\rho+\nabla\cdot(\rho v)=0,
$$

and

$$
\partial_tS_R
+\frac{|\nabla S_R|^2}{2m}
+V
-\frac{\hbar^2}{2m}
\frac{\Delta\sqrt\rho}{\sqrt\rho}
=0.
$$

These two equations form the Madelung representation. The spatial structure of amplitude and phase is already present in the official action; the complete temporal identification depends on the causal reconstruction.

The direct evaluation of the pre-symplectic potential shows why this condition is not automatic. The official action provides

$$
\Pi_{S_R}
=n_\mu\widehat J_S^\mu
=\frac{2\tau}{\hbar\Lambda_C^2}
\mathcal U\,
n_\mu g^{\mu\bar\nu}\partial_{\bar\nu}S_R,
$$

and not directly $\Pi_{S_R}=\rho$. The exact condition is derived in [[../notes/equations/Audit of the canonical term rho d_t S_R|Audit of the canonical term rho d_t S_R]].

The complete Hamiltonian test further shows that $C=\Pi_{S_R}-\sqrt h\rho$ is not a primary constraint of the action: the amplitude sector has an independent temporal momentum, and $\dot C$ does not vanish identically. In the co-moving stationary background, $C=0$ can define a preserved leaf; this does not establish the identity for general dynamics.

## 3. Quantum mechanics as a physical sector of QGD

The previous result has a broader significance than a normalization difficulty. The official action describes a Cauchy space with data

$$
\left(\rho,p_\rho,S_R,\Pi_{S_R}\right),
$$

while the usual hydrodynamic representation uses only

$$
(\rho,S_R).
$$

In the Madelung sector, the physical polarization satisfies

$$
p_\rho=0,
\qquad
\Pi_{S_R}=\sqrt h\rho.
$$

The first condition eliminates the independent kinetic oscillation of the amplitude. The second identifies the charge transported by the phase with the geometric density measured on the leaf. In this sector, the state form is

$$
\Omega_{\rm state}
=\int_\Sigma\delta\rho\wedge\delta S_R\,d\Sigma,
$$

and the effective action takes the canonical Madelung form.

This does not modify $\mathcal S_{\rm GDQ}$. The fundamental theory continues to admit the complete space of solutions; one selects a submanifold to represent ordinary quantum matter. Physically, solutions outside it may contain oscillations proper to the density, fast modes of the bulk, or out-of-equilibrium geometric states. Their mathematical existence does not yet authorize identifying them with new particles or observed phenomena.

Therefore, the proposed structural relationship is

$$
\boxed{
\text{hydrodynamic quantum mechanics}
\subset
\text{QGD dynamics}.
}
$$

This principle is generalizable: different observable regimes can correspond to different stable sectors or polarizations of the same solution space. What is demonstrated here is the natural canonical geometry of the sector $(\rho,S_R)$ and its stationary realization by Routh reduction. The universal dynamic selection of this polarization and the classification of the other sectors remain a subsequent program.

This separation also clarifies the role of NESS. A macroscopic steady state can conserve Noether currents and, at the same time, present entropy production in the projected description. There is no contradiction: charge is conserved in the closed dynamics, while irreversibility appears after degrees of the bulk or the apparatus cease to be monitored. Monotonicity in $\tau$ does not replace this construction in $t$.

## 4. What remains conditional

This chapter, by itself, did not demonstrate:

- that a coordinate of the Riemannian bulk is already physical time;
- that the normal component of the internal current is exactly $\rho$ in the laboratory;
- that the pullback produces the canonical term $\rho\,\partial_tS_R$ with the correct normalization;
- that the action is real for any causal boundary;
- that a specific stationary background is stable;
- that a continuous cosmological charge conserves its normalization upon reaching the laboratory.

These are not corrections to be added to the action. They are conditions of a mathematical bridge between the global space, the interface, and the physical chart.

## 5. Why the Hessian is still necessary

The equations above characterize stationary points. A stationary point can be a minimum, a maximum, or a saddle. Therefore, the admissibility chain continues with

$$
\text{bulk equations}
\longrightarrow
\text{background}
\longrightarrow
\mathbb H_*
\longrightarrow
\text{physical spectrum}.
$$

Gauge modes must be removed, constraints must be imposed, and the domain of the operator must include the boundary conditions. Only then can a positive gap be interpreted as the stability of a sector.

## 6. The function of the next chapter

The next chapter will build the bridge between Einstein's cosmological space $T^5\times S^3$ and the local bulk $\mathbb R^4\times T^4$. Its task will not be to re-derive the equations of this section, but to show when currents, normalizations, spectral projectors, and the physical clock are preserved by the transport.

This order keeps two questions separate:

1. which laws follow from the official action?
2. how are these laws observed in the laboratory chart?

Chapter 5 answered the first at the bulk level and delimited precisely what the second still needs to demonstrate.
