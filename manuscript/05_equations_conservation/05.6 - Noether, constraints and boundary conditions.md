# Noether, constraints and boundary conditions

So far, we have found a conserved current by directly varying the phase. Now we will show why this current is not an algebraic accident. It is the Noether current of the phase-shift symmetry. We will first present the general proof and then apply the result to the official QGD action.

## 1. The precise statement

Consider collective fields $\Phi^A$ and a Lagrangian density $\mathscr L$ depending on the fields and their first derivatives. We write

$$
\mathcal S[\Phi]
=\int_M \mathscr L(\Phi,\nabla\Phi)\,dV.
$$

A continuous transformation with a constant parameter $\varepsilon$ is written as

$$
\delta_\varepsilon\Phi^A
=\varepsilon\,\Delta_\xi\Phi^A.
$$

The index $\xi$ identifies the symmetry: phase shift, translation, rotation, or internal isometry. We say that it is a symmetry of the action when the density varies, at most, by a divergence:

$$
\delta_\varepsilon\mathscr L
=\varepsilon\,\nabla_a B_\xi^a.
$$

The case $B_\xi^a=0$ is a strict invariance. Allowing a divergence is essential because its integral depends only on the boundary and does not modify the bulk equations under admissible data.

## 2. First variation without hiding the integration by parts

The variation of the density is

$$
\delta\mathscr L
=\frac{\partial\mathscr L}{\partial\Phi^A}\delta\Phi^A
+\frac{\partial\mathscr L}{\partial(\nabla_a\Phi^A)}
\nabla_a\delta\Phi^A.
$$

We apply the product rule to the second term:

$$
\frac{\partial\mathscr L}{\partial(\nabla_a\Phi^A)}
\nabla_a\delta\Phi^A
=\nabla_a\left(
\frac{\partial\mathscr L}{\partial(\nabla_a\Phi^A)}
\delta\Phi^A
\right)
-\nabla_a\left(
\frac{\partial\mathscr L}{\partial(\nabla_a\Phi^A)}
\right)\delta\Phi^A.
$$

Therefore,

$$
\delta\mathscr L
=\mathcal E_A\,\delta\Phi^A+\nabla_a\Theta^a(\Phi,\delta\Phi),
$$

where

$$
\mathcal E_A
=\frac{\partial\mathscr L}{\partial\Phi^A}
-\nabla_a\left(
\frac{\partial\mathscr L}{\partial(\nabla_a\Phi^A)}
\right)
$$

are the Euler–Lagrange expressions, and

$$
\Theta^a(\Phi,\delta\Phi)
=\frac{\partial\mathscr L}{\partial(\nabla_a\Phi^A)}
\delta\Phi^A
$$

is the boundary potential of the first variation. For Lagrangians with curvature, $\Theta^a$ also contains the terms produced by the connection variation; the structural identity remains the same.

## 3. The Noether proof

We substitute the symmetry transformation into the previous identity. On one hand,

$$
\delta_\varepsilon\mathscr L
=\varepsilon\,\nabla_aB_\xi^a.
$$

On the other hand,

$$
\delta_\varepsilon\mathscr L
=\varepsilon\,\mathcal E_A\Delta_\xi\Phi^A
+\varepsilon\,\nabla_a
\Theta^a(\Phi,\Delta_\xi\Phi).
$$

Equating the two expressions and removing the constant parameter,

$$
\nabla_a\left[
\Theta^a(\Phi,\Delta_\xi\Phi)-B_\xi^a
\right]
=-\mathcal E_A\Delta_\xi\Phi^A.
$$

We define the Noether current by

$$
J_\xi^a
=\Theta^a(\Phi,\Delta_\xi\Phi)-B_\xi^a.
$$

The Noether identity follows, which is valid even off-shell:

$$
\nabla_aJ_\xi^a
=-\mathcal E_A\Delta_\xi\Phi^A.
$$

When the background satisfies the Euler–Lagrange equations, $\mathcal E_A=0$, we obtain

$$
\boxed{\nabla_aJ_\xi^a=0.}
$$

This is the logical point that must be preserved: conservation does not come merely from the appearance of a formula. It comes simultaneously from the invariance of the action and from the fact that the configuration is on-shell.

## 4. Localized variation: why the current appears

There is a particularly illuminating way to repeat the argument. A symmetry is defined for a constant $\varepsilon$. To discover its current, we temporarily promote the parameter to a test function $\varepsilon(x)$ of compact support. This promotion does not create a new gauge symmetry; it is only a mathematical probe.

Calculated via the Euler–Lagrange equations, the variation of compact support is

$$
\delta_{\varepsilon(x)}\mathcal S
=\int_M
\varepsilon\,\mathcal E_A\Delta_\xi\Phi^A\,dV.
$$

Calculated directly through the quasi-symmetric transformation, the same variation is the term that measures the non-constancy of the parameter:

$$
\delta_{\varepsilon(x)}\mathcal S
=\int_M(\nabla_a\varepsilon)J_\xi^a\,dV
=-\int_M\varepsilon\,\nabla_aJ_\xi^a\,dV,
$$

since the compact support eliminates the boundary. Equating the two ways of calculating the same variation and using the arbitrariness of $\varepsilon(x)$, we recover

$$
\nabla_aJ_\xi^a=-\mathcal E_A\Delta_\xi\Phi^A.
$$

This procedure explains why the coefficient of $\nabla_a\varepsilon$ is precisely the current.

## 5. Application to the QGD phase

In the decomposition

$$
f=-\ln\rho+\frac{i}{\hbar}S_R,
\qquad
\bar f=-\ln\rho-\frac{i}{\hbar}S_R,
$$

the action depends on $S_R$ only through its derivatives. Thus, the transformation

$$
S_R\longmapsto S_R+\varepsilon
$$

with constant $\varepsilon$ leaves $f+\bar f$, $\rho$, and $\mathcal U$ unchanged. In the real sector, the phase-dependent part can be isolated as

$$
\mathcal S_{S}
=\int_\gamma\int_{\mathcal M_{\mathbb C}}
\frac{\hbar}{\Lambda_C^2}
\frac{\tau\mathcal U}{\hbar^2}
g^{\mu\bar\nu}
\partial_\mu S_R\partial_{\bar\nu}S_R
\sqrt{\det g}\,d^{2n}z\,\frac{d\tau}{\tau}.
$$

Now we localize the parameter:

$$
\delta S_R=\varepsilon(z,\bar z,\tau).
$$

Since $\delta\mathcal U=0$ in this variation, we have

$$
\delta\mathcal S_S
=\int_\gamma\int_{\mathcal M_{\mathbb C}}
\frac{\hbar}{\Lambda_C^2}
\frac{\tau\mathcal U}{\hbar^2}
g^{\mu\bar\nu}
\left(
\partial_\mu\varepsilon\,\partial_{\bar\nu}S_R
+\partial_\mu S_R\,\partial_{\bar\nu}\varepsilon
\right)
\sqrt{\det g}\,d^{2n}z\,\frac{d\tau}{\tau}.
$$

In real form, the two terms are conjugates and sum to twice the real part. The coefficient of $\nabla\varepsilon$ defines the current. Suppressing only the common global constant prefactor and keeping the geometric density, we can write it as

$$
J_S^{\mu}
=\frac{2\tau}{\hbar^2}
\mathcal U g^{\mu\bar\nu}\partial_{\bar\nu}S_R.
$$

If we wish to include the action normalization in the current itself, we use

$$
\widehat J_S^{\mu}
=\frac{2\tau}{\hbar\Lambda_C^2}
\mathcal U g^{\mu\bar\nu}\partial_{\bar\nu}S_R.
$$

The two conventions differ by a global constant and express the same local law. Integrating by parts,

$$
\delta\mathcal S_S
=-\int_\gamma\int_{\mathcal M_{\mathbb C}}
\varepsilon\,\nabla_\mu\widehat J_S^\mu\,dV
+\int_\gamma\int_{\partial\mathcal M_{\mathbb C}}
\varepsilon\,\widehat J_S^\mu n_\mu\,d\Sigma.
$$

For variations of compact support, the boundary term vanishes. Since $\varepsilon$ is arbitrary, the phase equation is

$$
\boxed{\nabla_\mu\widehat J_S^\mu=0.}
$$

Therefore, the equation obtained in the previous section is exactly Noether's theorem applied to the global phase symmetry of the official action.

## 6. From local current to conserved charge

Let $\Omega$ be a region bounded by two oriented sections $\Sigma_1$ and $\Sigma_2$ and a lateral wall $\mathcal B$. By Stokes' theorem,

$$
0=\int_\Omega\nabla_aJ_\xi^a\,dV
=\int_{\Sigma_2}\star J_\xi
-\int_{\Sigma_1}\star J_\xi
+\int_{\mathcal B}\star J_\xi.
$$

Defining

$$
Q_\xi[\Sigma]=\int_\Sigma\star J_\xi,
$$

we obtain

$$
Q_\xi[\Sigma_2]-Q_\xi[\Sigma_1]
=-\int_{\mathcal B}\star J_\xi.
$$

The charge between the two sections is equal only when the lateral flux vanishes:

$$
\left.\star J_\xi\right|_{\mathcal B}=0
\quad\Longrightarrow\quad
Q_\xi[\Sigma_2]=Q_\xi[\Sigma_1].
$$

This clarifies the role of the boundary. A locally conserved current does not guarantee, by itself, that the charge of an open region remains constant; the charge can escape through the wall. In the global–local bridge, the no-leakage condition is precisely what allows transporting the normalization between sections.

## 7. Which symmetries produce which laws

The same reasoning can be applied to other symmetries, provided they are real symmetries of the domain, the measure, the background, and the boundary data:

- global shift of $S_R$: phase current and probability flux;
- translations: energy and momentum currents, when the background admits the corresponding translations;
- isotropy: angular momentum current and conservation of axial circulation;
- isometries of the internal space: geometric charge currents;
- admissible reparameterizations: identities between equations and constraints.

These associations do not authorize automatically identifying each current with the laboratory observable of the same name. This identification requires the pullback to the physical leaf and the normalization treated in the next chapter.

Discrete symmetries also require care. Since they do not have a continuous infinitesimal parameter, they do not generate a current through Noether's first theorem. They can, however, preserve a lattice of charges, permute sectors, and organize equivalent chambers. This distinction will be used in the cosmological average.

## 8. Symmetries and boundary terms

The same integration by parts that produces the bulk equations leaves on the boundary

$$
\delta\mathcal S\big|_{\partial M}
=\int_{\partial M}\Pi_A\,\delta\Phi^A.
$$

$\Pi_A$ is the normal momentum conjugate to the trace of $\Phi^A$. For the phase, for example, it is proportional to the normal flux:

$$
\Pi_{S_R}=n_\mu\widehat J_S^\mu.
$$

There are three legitimate ways to make the variational problem well-posed:

1. fix the field on the boundary, $\delta\Phi^A|_{\partial M}=0$;
2. leave the field free and impose $\Pi_A=0$;
3. include an explicitly declared interface action, yielding

$$
\Pi_A+\frac{\delta\mathcal S_{\rm int}}{\delta\Phi^A}=0.
$$

The third possibility includes Robin conditions and Dirichlet-to-Neumann operators. In it, the interface is not an ornament: it relates the value of the field to the normal flux produced by the bulk. Its coefficients must come from the reduced action, the response of the eliminated domain, or external data from the apparatus; they cannot be chosen afterward to reproduce a spectrum.

If an external classical source acts on the boundary, it changes the charge balance:

$$
n_aJ^a_{\rm object}+n_aJ^a_{\rm apparatus}=0.
$$

The flux lost by the object is received by the apparatus. This is the variational form of the classical–quantum interaction that will be developed later, without promoting the apparatus to a new fundamental field of QGD.

## 9. What was effectively proved

We demonstrated in this chapter that:

1. a continuous symmetry of the action produces a Noether identity;
2. on-shell, this identity becomes a local conservation law;
3. the independence of the action with respect to the absolute value of $S_R$ produces the phase current of QGD;
4. the conservation of the integrated charge requires, in addition to the local equation, the absence of lateral leakage or an explicit balance with an interface;
5. the boundary conditions arise from the same first variation that produces the bulk equations.

We have not yet asserted here that every internal current is already a laboratory observable. This step requires the physical reconstruction and the bridge between the global space and the local chart.
