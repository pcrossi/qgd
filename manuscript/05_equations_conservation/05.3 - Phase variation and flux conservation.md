# Phase variation and flux conservation

Now we will keep $g$, $\rho$, $z_\tau$, and the boundary fixed, varying only $S_R$. This is the simplest variation of the action, but its interpretation requires care: it directly produces a current in the complex bulk; the continuity equation in laboratory time still requires physical reconstruction.

## 1. The symmetry before the equation

In the real form of the action, $S_R$ appears via

$$
K_S
=\frac{1}{\hbar^2}
g^{\mu\bar\nu}
\partial_\mu S_R\partial_{\bar\nu}S_R.
$$

There is no dependence on the absolute value of $S_R$. Therefore,

$$
S_R\longmapsto S_R+S_0,
$$

with constant $S_0$, leaves the action unchanged. In terms of $f$,

$$
f\longmapsto f+\frac{iS_0}{\hbar},
\qquad
\bar f\longmapsto\bar f-\frac{iS_0}{\hbar}.
$$

The sum $f+\bar f$ does not change. Consequently,

$$
\delta_{S_R}\rho=0,
\qquad
\delta_{S_R}\mathcal U=0.
$$

This is the structural reason why the phase variation produces a conservation law: the action possesses a global continuous symmetry.

## 2. Isolation of the phase-dependent term

The part of the action that depends on $S_R$ is

$$
\mathcal S_S
=\int_\gamma\int_M
\frac{\hbar}{\Lambda_C^2}
\frac{\tau\mathcal U}{\hbar^2}
g^{\mu\bar\nu}
\partial_\mu S_R\partial_{\bar\nu}S_R
dV_g\frac{d\tau}{\tau}.
$$

Make an arbitrary variation in the interior,

$$
S_R\longmapsto S_R+\varepsilon\eta,
\qquad
\delta S_R=\eta.
$$

Differentiating at $\varepsilon=0$ and taking the real part of the Hermitian contraction,

$$
\delta\mathcal S_S
= \int_\gamma\int_M
\frac{2\tau}{\hbar\Lambda_C^2}
\mathcal U
g^{\mu\bar\nu}
\partial_\mu\eta
\partial_{\bar\nu}S_R
dV_g\frac{d\tau}{\tau}.
$$

The factor of two comes from the sum of the two conjugate parts. No variation of the measure appears, since $\rho$ was kept fixed.

## 3. Integration by parts

We define

$$
A^\mu
=\frac{2\tau}{\hbar\Lambda_C^2}
\mathcal U
g^{\mu\bar\nu}\partial_{\bar\nu}S_R.
$$

Using

$$
\nabla_\mu(\eta A^\mu)
=A^\mu\partial_\mu\eta
+\eta\nabla_\mu A^\mu,
$$

we obtain

$$
\begin{aligned}
\delta\mathcal S_S
={}&-\int_\gamma\int_M
\eta\,\nabla_\mu A^\mu
dV_g\frac{d\tau}{\tau}
\\
&+\int_\gamma\int_{\partial M}
\eta\,n_\mu A^\mu
d\Sigma\frac{d\tau}{\tau}.
\end{aligned}
$$

For $\eta$ compactly supported in the bulk, the boundary term vanishes. Since $\eta$ is arbitrary,

$$
\nabla_\mu A^\mu=0.
$$

We call $A^\mu$ the current normalized by the action:

$$
\widehat J_S^\mu
=\frac{2\tau}{\hbar\Lambda_C^2}
\mathcal U
g^{\mu\bar\nu}\partial_{\bar\nu}S_R.
$$

Therefore,

$$
\boxed{
\nabla_\mu\widehat J_S^\mu=0.
}
$$

We can remove any global constant prefactor from the definition. The reduced current

$$
J_S^\mu
=\frac{2\tau}{\hbar^2}
\mathcal U
g^{\mu\bar\nu}\partial_{\bar\nu}S_R
$$

satisfies the same equation. The chosen convention must, however, be maintained when comparing the normalization of charges.

## 4. What happens at the boundary

If the variation does not vanish at the boundary, there remains

$$
\delta\mathcal S_S\big|_{\partial M}
=\int_\gamma\int_{\partial M}
\eta\,n_\mu\widehat J_S^\mu
d\Sigma\frac{d\tau}{\tau}.
$$

The normal momentum conjugate to the phase is, therefore,

$$
\Pi_{S_R}=n_\mu\widehat J_S^\mu.
$$

We can fix $S_R$ at the boundary, impose $\Pi_{S_R}=0$, or balance this flux with an interface. These alternatives describe distinct physical systems. In particular, an external apparatus can receive the flux without violating the conservation of the combined system.

## 5. From local conservation to integrated conservation

Consider a region $\Omega$ whose boundary is formed by two sections $\Sigma_1$ and $\Sigma_2$ and a lateral wall $\mathcal B$. By Stokes' theorem,

$$
0=\int_\Omega\nabla_\mu J_S^\mu\,dV_g
=\int_{\Sigma_2}\star J_S
-\int_{\Sigma_1}\star J_S
+\int_{\mathcal B}\star J_S.
$$

Defining

$$
Q_S[\Sigma]=\int_\Sigma\star J_S,
$$

we find

$$
Q_S[\Sigma_2]-Q_S[\Sigma_1]
=-\int_{\mathcal B}\star J_S.
$$

If there is no lateral leakage,

$$
\int_{\mathcal B}\star J_S=0,
$$

then

$$
Q_S[\Sigma_2]=Q_S[\Sigma_1].
$$

Therefore, the differential equation provides local conservation; the conservation of the integrated charge additionally requires a closed domain or a no-leakage condition.

## 6. Why we do not write the continuity equation immediately

It is tempting to identify the equation above with

$$
\partial_t\rho+\nabla\cdot(\rho v)=0.
$$

This identification is not automatic. The official action is formulated in the Hermitian/Riemannian bulk and along the causal boundary $\gamma$. To obtain the laboratory equation, we need to:

1. select a clock-form;
2. identify leaves of simultaneity;
3. decompose the current into a normal component and tangential components;
4. show that the normal component is the physical density $\rho$;
5. identify the spatial part with $\rho v$;
6. demonstrate that there is no normalization factor lost in transport.

Furthermore, the reduced action must contain the canonical term

$$
\rho\,\partial_tS_R.
$$

Without it, the variation of a purely spatial energy would produce only

$$
\nabla\cdot(\rho v)=0,
$$

which is the stationary condition. Thus, the direct result of this section is the conservation of the phase current in the bulk. Temporal continuity will be a conditional corollary of the global–local bridge and the canonical reconstruction.

## 7. Relation to Noether

The calculation above derived the current directly. In Section 05.6 we will show that it is exactly the Noether current of the symmetry $S_R\mapsto S_R+S_0$. There we will also distinguish the off-shell identity from the on-shell conservation.
