# 05. Equations of motion and conservation laws

An action only acquires physical content when we ask what happens when its fields vary. This short sentence contains several operations that often appear ready-made in books: choosing which data can vary, differentiating the integrand, integrating by parts, separating bulk and boundary, and finally, using the arbitrariness of the variations. In this chapter, we will open up these operations. The objective is not to present a list of final equations, but to show where each of them comes from and what hypotheses allow us to interpret them physically.

In the previous chapter, we identified the fundamental functional, its structural data, and the role of the Hessian. Now we will follow the reverse path: we will start from the action and extract the equations that an admissible background must satisfy. When a transition depends on a reduction not yet constructed, it will be marked as conditional, rather than being silently incorporated into the derivation.

The most delicate point is that the complex field $f$ gathers two different pieces of information. Its real part determines the density; its imaginary part determines the phase. Varying them separately reveals two complementary laws: flux conservation and dynamic equilibrium. The metric variation adds the response of the geometry itself. The Noether proof will be done explicitly: we will start with the first variation, locate the symmetry parameter, identify the current, and show under what conditions its integral defines a truly conserved charge.
