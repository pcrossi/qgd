# Metric variation and geometric response

In QGD, the metric is not the motionless stage upon which matter moves. It is one of the fields that render the action stationary. Varying it is more laborious than varying $\rho$ or $S_R$, because the metric appears in the curvature, in the contractions, in the volume, and in the very definition of the connection.

In this section, we will perform the bulk variation within a fixed Hermitian class. The independent variation of the complex structure or the Bismut connection requires an extended class and will be indicated separately.

## 1. Metric variable convention

We will use the inverse metric $g^{AB}$ as the independent variable. Then

$$
\delta g_{AB}
=-g_{AC}g_{BD}\delta g^{CD},
$$

and

$$
\delta dV_g
=-\frac12g_{AB}\delta g^{AB}\,dV_g.
$$

Choosing $g_{AB}$ as the variable would produce the equivalent equation with lowered indices and reorganized signs. Mixing the two conventions during the same calculation is a common source of errors.

We will keep $f$, $\bar f$, and $z_\tau$ fixed during this variation. By the constitutive definition,

$$
\delta_g\mathcal U=0.
$$

This means that $\mathcal U$ is kept as a scalar function while the volume varies. If the integral normalization of $\mathcal U$ is imposed during the variation, it must enter as a constraint with a multiplier; it will not be incorporated implicitly.

## 2. Volume variation

Write again

$$
\mathcal L_0
=\tau(\mathcal R+K_f)+V_f,
$$

where, before imposing the integral normalization of the measure,

$$
K_f
=g^{AB}\operatorname{Re}
(\partial_Af\partial_B\bar f),
$$

and

$$
V_f=\frac{f+\bar f}{2}-n.
$$

The volume contributes

$$
\delta_g\left(
C\mathcal U\mathcal L_0dV_g
\right)_{\rm volume}
=-\frac12C\mathcal U\mathcal L_0
g_{AB}\delta g^{AB}\,dV_g.
$$

This term responds isotropically to the variation of the local volume.

## 3. Variation of the gradient energy

Since $f$ is fixed,

$$
\delta_gK_f
=P_{AB}^{(f)}\delta g^{AB},
$$

with

$$
P_{AB}^{(f)}
=\operatorname{Re}
(\partial_Af\partial_B\bar f).
$$

In the real writing, only the symmetric part of $P_{AB}^{(f)}$ is contracted with $\delta g^{AB}$. The contribution to the first variation is

$$
\delta_g\mathcal S_{\rm grad}
=\int_\gamma\int_M
C\tau\mathcal U
P_{AB}^{(f)}\delta g^{AB}
dV_g\frac{d\tau}{\tau}.
$$

This part is the stress produced by the amplitude and phase gradients. After the Madelung decomposition, it contains both $\partial_A\ln\rho\partial_B\ln\rho$ and $\partial_AS_R\partial_BS_R/\hbar^2$.

## 4. Variation of the weighted curvature

The local identity for the scalar curvature is

$$
\delta\mathcal R
=\mathcal R_{AB}\delta g^{AB}
+g_{AB}\nabla^2\delta g^{AB}
-\nabla_A\nabla_B\delta g^{AB},
$$

in the adopted convention, before integrating by parts. The last two terms are divergences only when the weight is constant. Since the action contains $\mathcal U$, the integrations by parts also differentiate this weight.

The weighted result is

$$
\begin{aligned}
\delta\int_M\mathcal U\mathcal R\,dV_g
={}&\int_M
\left[
\mathcal U\left(
\mathcal R_{AB}-\frac12\mathcal Rg_{AB}
\right)
\\
&\qquad
+g_{AB}\Delta_g\mathcal U
-\nabla_A\nabla_B\mathcal U
\right]
\delta g^{AB}\,dV_g
+B_{\mathcal R}.
\end{aligned}
$$

$B_{\mathcal R}$ gathers the boundary concomitant. This formula shows why the equation is not simply Einstein's equation multiplied by $\mathcal U$: the derivatives of the weight appear.

## 5. Metric bulk equation

Summing curvature, gradient, and volume, we obtain

$$
\delta_g\mathcal S_{\rm GDQ}
=\int_\gamma\int_M
C\,\mathcal E_{AB}^{(g)}
\delta g^{AB}\,dV_g\frac{d\tau}{\tau}
+B_g,
$$

where

$$
\begin{aligned}
\mathcal E_{AB}^{(g)}
={}&\tau\mathcal U
\left(
\mathcal R_{AB}+P_{AB}^{(f)}
\right)
\\
&+\tau\left(
g_{AB}\Delta_g\mathcal U
-\nabla_A\nabla_B\mathcal U
\right)
-\frac12\mathcal U\mathcal L_0g_{AB}.
\end{aligned}
$$

For compactly supported variations in the bulk, in the unrestricted problem,

$$
\boxed{\mathcal E_{AB}^{(g)}=0.}
$$

Dividing by $\mathcal U>0$, an equivalent form is

$$
\begin{aligned}
0={}&\tau\left(
\mathcal R_{AB}+P_{AB}^{(f)}
\right)
\\
&+\tau\left[
g_{AB}\frac{\Delta_g\mathcal U}{\mathcal U}
-\frac{\nabla_A\nabla_B\mathcal U}{\mathcal U}
\right]
-\frac12\mathcal L_0g_{AB}.
\end{aligned}
$$

In the normalized physical problem, we use the same multiplier $\lambda(\tau)$ from the density variation. Since

$$
\delta_g\int_M\mathcal U\,dV_g
=-\frac12\int_M
\mathcal U g_{AB}\delta g^{AB}\,dV_g,
$$

the normalized metric equation is obtained by replacing the last term with

$$
-\frac12\mathcal U
\left(\mathcal L_0-\lambda(\tau)\right)g_{AB}.
$$

Explicitly,

$$
\boxed{
\begin{aligned}
0={}&\tau\mathcal U
\left(
\mathcal R_{AB}+P_{AB}^{(f)}
\right)
\\
&+\tau\left(
g_{AB}\Delta_g\mathcal U
-\nabla_A\nabla_B\mathcal U
\right)
\\
&-\frac12\mathcal U
\left(
\mathcal L_0-\lambda(\tau)
\right)g_{AB}.
\end{aligned}
}
$$

This is the bulk equation in the declared class of variations. If only Hermitian metrics are admissible, one must take the Hermitian projection of this equation; the prohibited components do not correspond to independent variations.

## 6. Stress tensor obtained by variation

The effective stress tensor is not introduced by analogy with another theory. It is defined by the metric variation itself. Separating the non-purely geometric part of the action into $S_{\rm mat}$, we adopt the convention

$$
T_{AB}^{\rm eff}
=-\frac{2}{\sqrt g}
\frac{\delta S_{\rm mat}}{\delta g^{AB}}.
$$

In the gradient sector of $f$, the local contribution is proportional to

$$
P_{AB}^{(f)}
-\frac12K_fg_{AB}.
$$

With the common prefactor $C=\hbar/\Lambda_C^2$, this means, in the convention used in this section,

$$
T_{AB}^{(f)}
=-2C\tau\mathcal U
\left(
P_{AB}^{(f)}
-\frac12K_fg_{AB}
\right).
$$

The scalar sector $V_f=(f+\bar f)/2-n$ contributes only through the volume variation:

$$
T_{AB}^{(V)}
=C\mathcal U V_fg_{AB}.
$$

If the measure normalization is imposed, the multiplier $\lambda(\tau)$ changes the isotropic part exactly as in the normalized metric equation. The important point is that $T_{AB}^{\rm eff}$ is an object derived from the action, not an imported source.

## 7. Rewriting the derivatives of the weight

Since

$$
\ln\mathcal U
=-\frac{f+\bar f}{2}
-n\ln(4\pi z_\tau),
$$

and $z_\tau$ is constant in the internal directions of this variation,

$$
\nabla_A\ln\mathcal U
=-\frac12\nabla_A(f+\bar f).
$$

For any positive function,

$$
\frac{\nabla_A\nabla_B\mathcal U}{\mathcal U}
=\nabla_A\nabla_B\ln\mathcal U
+\nabla_A\ln\mathcal U\nabla_B\ln\mathcal U,
$$

and

$$
\frac{\Delta_g\mathcal U}{\mathcal U}
= \Delta_g\ln\mathcal U
+|\nabla\ln\mathcal U|_g^2.
$$

Therefore, the weighted block can be written entirely in terms of the real part of $f$. It is in this precise sense that the density field participates in the metric response.

## 8. Type of equation

There are three different questions that should not be mixed.

First, the stationary bulk equation

$$
\mathcal E_{AB}^{(g)}=0
$$

is a second-order geometric equation in the Riemannian/Hermitian bulk. Its principal part has the usual diffeomorphism degeneracy. After fixing the geometric gauge, for example by a DeTurck-type choice, the stationary sector is elliptic.

Second, if we use the variational equation as a generator of geometric relaxation in $\tau$, we obtain schematically

$$
\partial_\tau g_{AB}
=-2\mathcal E_{AB}^{\rm geom}.
$$

After the same DeTurck correction, this flow is parabolic. It is a flow of scale, smoothing, and geometric relaxation; it is not the observer's physical time.

Third, the physical causal evolution appears only after the reconstruction of the effective Lorentzian layer $(N,h)$. In this projection, the propagating physical operators have a hyperbolic principal symbol in relation to $h$.

Therefore:

$$
\text{stationary bulk: elliptic},
\qquad
\text{flow in }\tau\text{: parabolic},
\qquad
\text{physical evolution in }t\text{: hyperbolic}.
$$

This distinction is part of QGD: $\tau$ is neither a physical fifth dimension nor causal time.

## 9. The role of Bismut torsion

The official action contains $\mathcal R$ and gradients calculated in the adopted Hermitian structure. If the connection used to define these objects is the Bismut connection, its torsion is determined by $(g,J)$ within the chosen class. When varying $g$ or $J$, the torsion can vary inductively.

This does not authorize adding an arbitrary torsion tensor to the equation. There are two distinct situations:

1. if $J$ and the connection are kept fixed, the above metric variation holds in the declared sector;
2. if $(g,J,H)$ vary compatibly, one must calculate the connection derivative and project the equation onto the tangent space of the admissible Hermitian–Bismut structures.

The second calculation is necessary for complete torsional backgrounds, but it is an explicit expansion of the variational class, not a new fundamental term.

When the scalar curvature is written using the Bismut connection, the condensed notation is

$$
\mathcal R
\longrightarrow
\mathcal R_B.
$$

In usual conventions, the torsional scalar curvature contains a quadratic contribution of the type

$$
\mathcal R_B
=\mathcal R_{\rm LC}
-c_H|H|^2
+\operatorname{divergence},
$$

where $c_H$ depends on the chosen normalization for the 3-form $H$. Thus, the torsion enters through the connection and the induced quadratic sector, not by an arbitrary tensor added later.

## 10. Bianchi identity and on-shell conservation

The action is invariant under admissible diffeomorphisms of the bulk. If $X$ is the generator field of an infinitesimal diffeomorphism, the Noether variation has the form

$$
0=\delta_X\mathcal S_{\rm GDQ}
=\int_M
\left(
\mathcal E_g^{AB}\mathcal L_Xg_{AB}
+\mathcal E_f\mathcal L_Xf
+\mathcal E_{\bar f}\mathcal L_X\bar f
+\mathcal E_H\mathcal L_XH
\right)dV_g
+B_X.
$$

After integrating by parts, and for variations that do not leave unaccounted boundary flux, one obtains the differential identity

$$
\nabla^A\mathcal E_{AB}
+\mathcal E_f\nabla_Bf
+\mathcal E_{\bar f}\nabla_B\bar f
+\mathcal E_H\cdot\iota_BH
=0.
$$

Therefore, when the field equations also hold,

$$
\mathcal E_f=0,
\qquad
\mathcal E_{\bar f}=0,
\qquad
\mathcal E_H=0,
$$

the metric equation satisfies

$$
\nabla^A\mathcal E_{AB}=0.
$$

In the language of effective stress, this is on-shell covariant conservation:

$$
\nabla^AT_{AB}^{\rm eff}=0.
$$

If the physical connection of the sector is Bismut, the covariant derivative and the Bianchi identity must be read in the corresponding compatible connection. Off-shell, the divergence of $T_{AB}^{\rm eff}$ does not need to vanish in isolation; it is compensated by the unsatisfied equations of $f$, $\bar f$, and the torsional sector.

## 11. The boundary term of curvature

On a closed manifold, $B_g$ vanishes. On a manifold with boundary, the variation of $\mathcal R$ contains normal derivatives of $\delta g$. Fixing only $\delta g|_{\partial M}=0$ might not be sufficient to vanish them.

There are then two rigorous routes:

- work with compactly supported variations to derive only the bulk equation;
- construct the boundary functional compatible with the weighted action and the interface data.
Sincerely, we will not silently insert the Gibbons–Hawking–York term from general relativity, because the measure of QGD is weighted and the interface problem must be derived in its own class. The Dirichlet-to-Neumann operator used in the stomata is precisely a way to encode the response of the eliminated domain after that problem has been well-posed.

## 12. Stationarity is not stability

A solution

$$
(g_*,f_*;J_*,H_*)
$$

must satisfy simultaneously the metric, density, and phase equations, the measure normalization, and the boundary data. This guarantees only that the first variation vanishes.

To test stability, we write a perturbation $\eta$ and expand

$$
\operatorname{Re}\mathcal S[\Phi_*+\varepsilon\eta]
=\operatorname{Re}\mathcal S[\Phi_*]
+\frac{\varepsilon^2}{2}
\langle\eta,\mathbb H_*\eta\rangle
+O(\varepsilon^3).
$$

Before interpreting the sign, we remove gauge directions and impose the constraints. The physical Hessian is

$$
\mathbb H_*
=P_{\rm phys}
\operatorname{Hess}
\operatorname{Re}\mathcal S_{\rm GDQ}\big|_*
P_{\rm phys}.
$$

Only the spectrum of this operator, with specified domain and boundary, can decide whether the background is stable, metastable, or has an instability direction.
