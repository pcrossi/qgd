# 10.3. Why scalar circulation is not enough

A scalar circulation can show that there is phase monodromy. It can explain why one turn around a defect produces a sign. But it does not build on its own:

1. the spin bundle;
2. the Clifford algebra;
3. the representation of $\mathrm{Spin}(3,1)$;
4. the $2\pi\mapsto -1$ transformation as a representation of the double covering;
5. the CAR algebra;
6. the graded locality;
7. the Pauli exclusion principle.

Therefore, the correct statement is:

$$
\text{circulation and torsion give the physical geometry of spin,}
$$

but:

$$
\text{half-integer spin requires a spinor structure.}
$$

This distinction prevents GDQ from turning into an incomplete hydrodynamic analogy. The advantage of GDQ is different: it gives a geometric interpretation to the spinor structure that appears operationally.
