# 10.9. Pauli exclusion as a node and geometric barrier

From the CAR:

$$
\{a_i^\dagger,a_j^\dagger\}=0.
$$

Taking $i=j$:

$$
2(a_i^\dagger)^2=0.
$$

Thus:

$$
(a_i^\dagger)^2=0.
$$

Therefore, two identical fermions cannot occupy the same state.

In wavefunction language:

$$
\Psi(x,x)=0.
$$

In GDQ language, this node has a geometric manifestation. If:

$$
R=\sqrt\rho\to0,
$$

the Bohm potential:

$$
Q
=
-\frac{\hbar^2}{2m}
\frac{\nabla^2R}{R}
$$

becomes singular around the node, functioning as a geometric barrier against the coincidence of the two solitons.

The correct logical order is:

$$
\text{CAR}
\to
\text{Pauli}
\to
\text{node}
\to
\text{geometric barrier}.
$$

We must not invert this order and declare that the Bohm barrier on its own proves the CAR. It is the geometric manifestation of an algebra already fixed in the effective spinor sector.
