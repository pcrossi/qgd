# 10.1. What spin means in GDQ

The physical idea of spin in GDQ is geometric. A material soliton is not a point: it has internal structure, boundary, phase circulation, and torsion. What operational physics calls spin appears, in this reading, as the signature of a stable internal orientation of the defect.

In the hydrodynamic sector:

$$
\mathbf v=\frac{\nabla S_R}{m}.
$$

In a simply connected region without defects, this velocity is irrotational:

$$
\nabla\times\mathbf v=0.
$$

But when there is a removed core, stoma, knot, or multivalued phase sector, the circulation can be non-trivial:

$$
\oint_\gamma dS_R\ne0.
$$

This circulation should not be read as a spinning rigid ball. It is a phase/torsion holonomy of the geometric space that carries the soliton.

The chapter preserves this interpretation, but adds the correct mathematical requirement: spin $1/2$ needs a spinor structure. The circulation explains the geometric manifestation; the spin bundle realizes the half-integer representation.
