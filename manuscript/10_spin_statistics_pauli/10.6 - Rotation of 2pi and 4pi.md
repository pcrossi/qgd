# 10.6. Rotation of 2pi and 4pi

In the spatial subgroup, the spinor rotation by angle $\theta$ around $\mathbf n$ is:

$$
U(\theta)
=
\exp\left(
-i\frac{\theta}{2}\mathbf n\cdot\boldsymbol\sigma
\right).
$$

For $\theta=2\pi$:

$$
U(2\pi)
=
\exp(-i\pi\,\mathbf n\cdot\boldsymbol\sigma)
=
-I.
$$

For $\theta=4\pi$:

$$
U(4\pi)
=
\exp(-i2\pi\,\mathbf n\cdot\boldsymbol\sigma)
=
I.
$$

Thus:

$$
2\pi\mapsto -1,
\qquad
4\pi\mapsto +1.
$$

This property is not a choice of sign. It comes from the fact that $SU(2)$ is the double covering of $SO(3)$. The Hopf geometry provides the visual image:

$$
S^1\hookrightarrow S^3\to S^2,
$$

with $S^3\simeq SU(2)$ and $S^2\simeq\mathbb{CP}^1$ as the space of physical directions.
