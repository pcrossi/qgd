# Pauli, CAR and Bohm barrier

## Statement

In the fermionic CAR sector:

$$
(a_i^\dagger)^2=0.
$$

Thus, two identical fermions do not occupy the same state. In GDQ, this appears geometrically as an amplitude node and a Bohm barrier.

## Algebraic proof

The CAR relations impose:

$$
\{a_i^\dagger,a_j^\dagger\}=0.
$$

For $i=j$:

$$
a_i^\dagger a_i^\dagger+a_i^\dagger a_i^\dagger=0.
$$

Thus:

$$
2(a_i^\dagger)^2=0.
$$

Therefore:

$$
(a_i^\dagger)^2=0.
$$

## Wavefunction

Antisymmetry implies:

$$
\Psi(x_1,x_2)=-\Psi(x_2,x_1).
$$

At $x_1=x_2$:

$$
\Psi(x,x)=-\Psi(x,x).
$$

Thus:

$$
\Psi(x,x)=0.
$$

## Geometric manifestation

If:

$$
R=\sqrt\rho,
$$

then at the node:

$$
R\to0.
$$

The Bohm potential:

$$
Q
=
-\frac{\hbar^2}{2m}\frac{\nabla^2R}{R}
$$

becomes singular when the denominator vanishes in an uncancelled way. This singularity geometrically manifests exclusion.

## Scope

Pauli follows from CAR. The Bohm barrier does not replace the proof; it is the geometric reading of the algebraic impossibility.
