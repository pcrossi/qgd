# 10.2. Circulation, defects and torsion

A topological defect removes from the domain a core where the phase does not need to be globally simple. Around this core, the integral of the phase 1-form can be topologically protected:

$$
\oint_\gamma dS_R
=
\text{phase period}.
$$

In the complex reading, a normal neighborhood of the stoma can be described by a punctured disk $D^\ast$ with coordinate $z$. A local spinor section can behave as:

$$
s(z)=z^{1/2}s_0(z).
$$

The associated logarithmic connection is:

$$
\Omega_S=d\log s
=
\frac12\frac{dz}{z}+d\log s_0.
$$

Then:

$$
\operatorname{Res}_{z=0}\Omega_S=\frac12.
$$

By the residue theorem:

$$
\frac{1}{2\pi i}\oint_\gamma\Omega_S=\frac12.
$$

In physical phase:

$$
\oint_\gamma dS_R=\frac h2=\pi\hbar.
$$

Thus:

$$
\exp\left(\frac{i}{\hbar}\oint_\gamma dS_R\right)=-1.
$$

This is the half-monodromy that appears as spin $1/2$.

The Bismut/Cartan torsion provides the geometric language of orientation:

$$
B_{\mu\nu\lambda}
\sim
\kappa\,S_{\mu\nu\lambda}.
$$

This relation should be read as a spin coupling/interpretation, not as a replacement of the spinor construction.
