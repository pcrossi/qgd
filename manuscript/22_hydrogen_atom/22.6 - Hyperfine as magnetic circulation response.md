# 22.6. Hyperfine as magnetic circulation response

Hyperfine structure is the coupling between the magnetic circulation of the electron and the magnetic circulation of the proton.

In operational language:

$$
H_{\rm hfs}
=
\frac{8\pi}{3}
\boldsymbol\mu_e\cdot\boldsymbol\mu_p
\delta^{(3)}(\mathbf r)
+H_{\rm dip}
+H_{\rm recoil}
+H_{\rm ff}.
$$

In GDQ language:

$$
\mathsf R_{\rm mag}^{p,e}
=
\mathsf R_{\rm contact}
+\mathsf R_{\rm dip}
+\mathsf R_{\rm ff}
+\mathsf R_{\rm recoil}.
$$

## 1. Leading Comparison

Using the experimental magnetic moment of the proton as an external input, the leading Fermi frequency is:

$$
\nu_F
=
1.418840092598624\times10^9\,{\rm Hz}.
$$

The accepted 21 cm line is:

$$
\nu_{\rm obs}
=
1.420405751768\times10^9\,{\rm Hz}.
$$

Relative error:

$$
-1.102262\times10^{-3}.
$$

With the leading magnetic channel:

$$
a_e^{(1)}
=
\frac{\alpha}{2\pi},
$$

it becomes:

$$
\nu
=
1.420487947291580\times10^9\,{\rm Hz}.
$$

Relative error:

$$
5.786764\times10^{-5}.
$$

With the shell Zemach factor:

$$
\nu
=
1.420427795242300\times10^9\,{\rm Hz}.
$$

Relative error:

$$
1.551914\times10^{-5}.
$$

With fine kinematic recoil:

$$
\nu
=
1.420427774656176\times10^9\,{\rm Hz}.
$$

Relative error:

$$
1.550465\times10^{-5}.
$$

## 2. Refinement by Collective Magnetic Impedance

The previous calculation still treats the surface magnetic response incompletely: it uses the shell Zemach factor but does not insert the collective impedance of the proton into the magnetic form factor.

In GDQ, the correct surface correction is not a number evaluated only at the atomic scale. It must enter into $G_M(q)$ inside the Zemach integral, because this integral also samples hadronic scales.

We write:

$$
\frac{G_M(q)}{\mu_p}
=
j_0(qr_p)
+\beta_{\rm GDQ}\mathcal I_\Sigma(q),
$$

with:

$$
\beta_{\rm GDQ}
=
3(1+\kappa_p).
$$

The factor $3$ comes from the three coherent channels of the proton. The factor $1+\kappa_p$ is the total geometric magnetic moment of the proton in nuclear magneton units.

With:

$$
\kappa_p
=
1.792828941528952,
$$

we have:

$$
\beta_{\rm GDQ}
=
8.378486824586854.
$$

The effective Zemach radius obtained by direct integration of the form factors is:

$$
r_Z^{\rm Schur}
=
1.311146929275\,{\rm fm}.
$$

Using $a_e=\alpha/(2\pi)$, we obtain:

$$
\nu_{\rm hfs}
=
1.420408212335650\times10^9\,{\rm Hz},
$$

with difference:

$$
+2460.567651\,{\rm Hz}.
$$

Using external metrological $a_e$ only as a benchmark ruler:

$$
\nu_{\rm hfs}
=
1.420405718790905\times10^9\,{\rm Hz}.
$$

Compared to:

$$
\nu_{\rm obs}
=
1.420405751768000\times10^9\,{\rm Hz},
$$

the difference is:

$$
-32.977095\,{\rm Hz},
$$

or:

$$
-2.321667236238\times10^{-8}.
$$

This is the leading metrological result obtained in the reduced chain. It does not use the 21 cm line to fix $\beta_{\rm GDQ}$. The residue of tens of Hz remains associated with terms not yet included: complete hyperfine recoil, fine polarizability, and metrological conditions of the physical system.

## 3. Interpretation

The error fell from $10^{-3}$ to $10^{-5}$ with the leading geometric channels, and to $10^{-8}$ when the collective magnetic impedance is inserted into the form factor.

The remaining residue requires the higher magnetic Hessian of the proton:

$$
\Delta\nu_{\rm mag}^{\rm req}
\simeq
\mathcal O(10^1)\,{\rm Hz}.
$$

This number is diagnostic of the missing scale, not a fitted parameter.
