# 22.7. Form factor, Zemach and muonic hydrogen

The finite size of the proton enters through form factors and boundary conditions.

The Zemach radius is:

$$
r_Z
=
-\frac4\pi
\int_0^\infty
\frac{dq}{q^2}
\left[
\frac{G_E(q)G_M(q)}{G_M(0)}
-1
\right].
$$

For coincident surface shells:

$$
G_E(q)
=
j_0(qr_p),
\qquad
\frac{G_M(q)}{\mu_p}
=
j_0(qr_p).
$$

Direct integration yields:

$$
r_Z
=
1.121038354001\,{\rm fm}.
$$

In agreement with:

$$
r_Z
=
\frac43r_p
=
1.121038353933\,{\rm fm}.
$$

## 1. Muonic Hydrogen

The finite-size shift scales as:

$$
\Delta E_{\rm fs}
\propto
\mu^3 r_p^2.
$$

For electronic hydrogen:

$$
\Delta E_{\rm fs}^{H}(2s)
=
5.715065961503\times10^{-10}\,{\rm eV}.
$$

For muonic hydrogen:

$$
\Delta E_{\rm fs}^{\mu H}(2s)
=
3.674126176\,{\rm meV}.
$$

The amplification is:

$$
6.428842992\times10^6.
$$

## 2. Radius Backreaction

The linear response of the protonic surface is:

$$
\delta r_p[\ell]
=
-
(H_p^{\rm surf})^{-1}J_\ell.
$$

Since:

$$
J_\ell
=
\mu_{\ell p}^3,
$$

we have:

$$
\frac{\delta r_p[e]}{\delta r_p[\mu]}
=
\left(
\frac{\mu_{ep}}{\mu_{\mu p}}
\right)^3
=
1.555489846616\times10^{-7}.
$$

Therefore, the electron also backreacts on the proton radius, but much less than the muon.

To make the scale explicit, if the muonic probe contracts the effective radius by diagnostic values of order $10^{-2}\,{\rm fm}$, the same variational response predicts:

| Assumed muonic contraction | Estimated electronic contraction |
|---:|---:|
| $-0.010000\,{\rm fm}$ | $-1.555489846616\times10^{-9}\,{\rm fm}$ |
| $-0.030000\,{\rm fm}$ | $-4.666469539847\times10^{-9}\,{\rm fm}$ |
| $-0.034000\,{\rm fm}$ | $-5.288665478493\times10^{-9}\,{\rm fm}$ |

This table is not a calibration of the radius. It only shows the consequence of the $\mu^3$ law for the contact source. The absolute prediction of $\delta r_p[\mu]$ requires evaluating $H_p^{\rm surf}$ and $J_\mu$ via the complete protonic Hessian.
