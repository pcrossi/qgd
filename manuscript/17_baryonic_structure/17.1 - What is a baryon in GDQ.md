# 17.1. What is a baryon in GDQ

In GDQ, a baryon is a composite geometric defect. It possesses three internal throats, or stomata, glued together by a common Ricci–Bismut structure. These throats are not independent point-like particles. They are channels of flux, torsion, and phase within the same soliton.

The reduced structure is:

$$
\mathfrak G_B
=
\{
\mathcal F_a,\Psi_{ab},\mathcal A_{ab},B_{ab},g_B,f_B
\}_{a,b=1}^{3}.
$$

Here:

1. $\mathcal F_a$ are the effective chambers;
2. $\Psi_{ab}$ encodes the gluing between throats;
3. $\mathcal A_{ab}$ records holonomies and phases;
4. $B_{ab}$ is the Bismut torsional sector;
5. $g_B$ and $f_B$ are the baryonic metric-dilatonic background.

The physical meaning is direct: proton and neutron are two stationary orientations of the same trimodal geometric class.
