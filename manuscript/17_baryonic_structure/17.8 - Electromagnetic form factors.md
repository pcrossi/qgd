# 17.8. Electromagnetic form factors

The Sachs form factors are transforms of the geometric densities:

$$
G_E^B(q^2)
=
\int
\rho_E^B(r)
j_0(qr)
d\mu,
$$

$$
G_M^B(q^2)
=
\int
\rho_M^B(r)
j_0(qr)
d\mu.
$$

The mandatory normalizations are:

$$
G_E^p(0)=1,
\qquad
G_E^n(0)=0,
$$

$$
G_M^p(0)=\mu_p,
\qquad
G_M^n(0)=\mu_n.
$$

In reduced closure, the proton can be represented by an electrical shell:

$$
G_E^p(q^2)=j_0(qr_p).
$$

The neutron is globally neutral but can have polarized local density. The minimum structure uses two shells:

$$
\rho_E^n d\mu
=
A_n[
\delta(\chi-\chi_+)
-
\delta(\chi-\chi_-)
]d\chi.
$$

In the extended double glue:

$$
\langle r_n^2\rangle_{\rm ext}
=
-2|\mu_n|\alpha_{\rm tor}^{(2)}r_p^2,
$$

with:

$$
\alpha_{\rm tor}^{(2)}
=
2\alpha\ln(2\pi^2).
$$

This gives a reduced value close to the observed squared electrical radius of the neutron.

The corresponding smooth profile is obtained by $H_n(\chi)$, a surface heat flow solution. The complete curve at intermediate $q$ also requires the probe's response, modeled by the Schur complement of the collective surface impedance.
