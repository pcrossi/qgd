# 17.4. Proton and neutron as torsional orientations

The proton corresponds to the charged alignment:

$$
(\mathcal T_1,\mathcal T_2,\mathcal T_3)
=
(\tau,\tau,\tau).
$$

The neutron corresponds to a stationary antiparallel orientation:

$$
(\mathcal T_1,\mathcal T_2,\mathcal T_3)
=
(\tau,\tau,-2\tau).
$$

Then:

$$
\sum_{a=1}^{3}\mathcal T_a=0.
$$

This is boundary torsional current conservation:

$$
\delta_\vartheta\mathcal S_{\rm GDQ}=0
\quad\Longrightarrow\quad
dJ_{\rm tor}=0.
$$

The neutron mass excess comes from the antiparallel shear:

$$
\delta_B
=
\ln(2\pi^2)\frac{3\sqrt2}{5}.
$$

Therefore:

$$
\frac{M_n}{M_e}
=
\frac{M_p}{M_e}
+
\delta_B.
$$

Numerically:

$$
\delta_B
\simeq
2.5308259219.
$$
