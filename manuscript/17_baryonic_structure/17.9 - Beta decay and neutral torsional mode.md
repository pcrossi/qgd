# 17.9. Beta decay and neutral torsional mode

Free beta decay is:

$$
n\to p+e^-+\bar\nu_e.
$$

The kinematic endpoint is:

$$
Q_\beta
=
M_n-M_p-m_e.
$$

It is not a fixed antineutrino energy. It is the total kinetic energy available. Event by event:

$$
M_nc^2-M_pc^2
=
E_e+E_{\bar\nu}+E_{\rm recoil}.
$$

In the limit of negligible recoil:

$$
E_{\bar\nu}
=
\Delta M-E_e,
\qquad
m_e\le E_e\le\Delta M.
$$

Therefore the spectrum is continuous. The correct statement is not that the antineutrino always carries $Q_\beta$, but that $Q_\beta$ is the maximum endpoint available for the kinetic sum of the light products.

Since the baryonic construction yields $M_n-M_p=\delta_B M_e$, the endpoint also allows us to metrologically determine the electronic scale:

$$
M_ec^2
=
\frac{Q_\beta}{\delta_B-1}.
$$

This relation does not use $M_e$ to fit $\delta_B$; it uses the beta endpoint as a metrological boundary condition.

In GDQ, the electron is a localized charged defect and the antineutrino is a propagating neutral torsional mode:

$$
\psi_{\bar\nu}
\in
\ker D_{0,-3/2}^{(0)}.
$$

The effective amplitude comes from the fourth physical variation of the official action:

$$
\mathcal V_{\rm eff}^{(4)}
=
\mathcal S_{\rm GDQ}^{(4)}
-
\mathcal S_{\rm GDQ}^{(3)}K_\perp^{-1}
\mathcal S_{\rm GDQ}^{(3)}
+
\text{permutations}.
$$

In the unpolarized sector:

$$
\frac12\sum_{\rm spins}|\mathcal M_0|^2
=
2|C_S|^2+6|C_T|^2.
$$

For the total rate, the contracted physical quantity is:

$$
\mathcal J_3^2
:=
2|C_S|^2+6|C_T|^2.
$$

Angular correlations depend on $C_S$ and $C_T$ separately. The total lifetime depends only on this contracted norm.
