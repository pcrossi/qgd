# 17.7. Radii and magnetic moments

The charge radius comes from the geometric density. Under surface reduction:

$$
r_p
=
C_r\epsilon_{\rm eff}R_B,
$$

with:

$$
C_r
=
\frac18
\left(
1+\frac{\alpha}{4}
\right),
\qquad
R_B=\frac32\Lambda_C.
$$

Thus:

$$
r_p
=
\frac18
\left(
1+\frac{\alpha}{4}
\right)
\epsilon_{\rm eff}
\left(
\frac32\Lambda_C
\right).
$$

With $\epsilon_{\rm eff}=0.011591040463$ and $\Lambda_C=386.159268\,{\rm fm}$:

$$
r_p
\simeq
0.8407787654\,{\rm fm}.
$$

The magnetic moment comes from the geometric current:

$$
\vec\mu_B
=
\frac12
\int_{\Sigma_B^\circ}
\vec r\times\vec J_B\,d^3x.
$$

In the current surface reduction:

$$
\mu_p
=
1
+
\frac35\ln(2\pi^2)
\left(
1+\frac{\alpha}{4}
\right),
$$

$$
\mu_n
=
-
\frac34\delta_B
\left(
1+\alpha\frac{3\sqrt2}{4}
\right).
$$

Numerically:

$$
\mu_p
\simeq
2.7928289415\,\mu_N,
$$

$$
\mu_n
\simeq
-1.9128109072\,\mu_N.
$$
