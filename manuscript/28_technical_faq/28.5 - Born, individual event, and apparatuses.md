# 28.5 — Born, individual event, and apparatuses

Born's rule should not be treated as a postulate manually inserted into GDQ.

The geometric density is:

$$
\rho=e^{-(f+\bar f)/2}.
$$

It is positive and obeys a continuity equation in the physical sector. After the reconstruction of the operational Hilbert space, the probability of a projector $P_i$ is read as:

$$
P(i)=\operatorname{Tr}(\varrho P_i).
$$

This closes Born's rule at the operational level.

But Born by projectors is not a complete theory of the individual event. A real event requires an apparatus, dissipation, boundary, registration, and channel selection. The GDQ measurement chain is:

$$
J_{\rm app}^{\rm classical}
\to
\delta\Phi_{\rm app}
\to
\mathsf R_{\rm app}
\to
\Gamma_{\rm det}
\to
\text{registration}.
$$

Thus, GDQ separates two assertions:

1. operational probabilities follow from $\rho$ and physical reconstruction;
2. the individual event requires classical--quantum interface dynamics.

This separation avoids a common confusion. Projectors tell how to compute frequencies in an ensemble. They do not by themselves explain why a macroscopic detector registers one channel and not another.
