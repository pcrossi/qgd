# 28.1 — What this FAQ is for

A new theory is not evaluated solely by the calculations it presents. It also needs to respond to language objections: what is an axiom, what is a reduction, what is an external comparison, what is numerical evidence, and what is still a research program.

QDQ uses familiar objects from mathematics and physics: phase, density, connections, Hessians, projectors, geometric flows, boundaries, measurements, and observables. The reading risk is to confuse these names with the theories where they usually appear.

Therefore, this chapter adopts a simple rule:

$$
\boxed{
\text{same word does not mean same ontology.}
}
$$

When QDQ recovers a Dirac equation, a Yang--Mills sector, a Born rule, or a Pauli formula, this does not mean these objects were postulated as foundations. It means they appear as operational reductions of a geometric chain.

The chain that must be preserved is:

$$
\mathcal S_{\rm GDQ}
\to
\Phi_*
\to
P_{\rm phys}
\to
K_{\rm phys}
\to
J_{\rm app}
\to
\mathsf R_{\rm app}
\to
\mathcal O_{\rm obs}.
$$

Here:

- $\mathcal S_{\rm GDQ}$ is the official action;
- $\Phi_*$ is the admissible background;
- $P_{\rm phys}$ removes gauge modes and redundancies;
- $K_{\rm phys}$ is the Hessian in the physical sector;
- $J_{\rm app}$ is a classical source or apparatus data;
- $\mathsf R_{\rm app}$ is the impedance/response induced at the interface;
- $\mathcal O_{\rm obs}$ is the observable comparable with the experiment.

The FAQ should be read like this: if an objection questions a link in the chain, it is technical and must be answered by derivation. If it replaces the chain with another theory, it is a confusion of language.
