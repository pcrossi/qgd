# 28.3 — QDQ and the operational Standard Model

QDQ should not be rewritten as the Standard Model with geometric names.

The correct order is:

$$
\text{GDQ}
\to
\text{internal geometry}
\to
\text{effective connections}
\to
\text{operational sectors}.
$$

When objects like $SU(3)$, $SU(2)$, $U(1)$, the Dirac equation, Pauli, Yang--Mills, or Born appear, they must be read as effective reductions or operational representations. They do not replace the QDQ ontology.

The example of the color sector is the most direct. QDQ constructs an effective internal connection and shows that, in the color/quark domain, the reduced dynamics has the operational form of Yang--Mills. This allows writing:

$$
\text{GDQ internal sector}
\simeq
\text{effective Yang--Mills sector},
$$

in the declared domain.

But sector equivalence does not authorize inverting the logic and writing:

$$
\text{fundamental Yang--Mills}
\to
\text{GDQ}.
$$

This would be replacing the theory with its reduction.

Physically, the difference is simple. In the operational Standard Model, gauge fields are fundamental entities. In QDQ, they are effective modes of connection, holonomy, torsion, and boundary of the complex geometry. The operational calculation may coincide; the fundamental interpretation does not.
