# 28.9 — Conditional results and how not to reopen problems

A conditional result is not a weak result. It is a statement with explicit hypotheses.

In GDQ, many results have the form:

$$
\text{if } H_1,\ldots,H_k \text{ hold, then } R \text{ follows from the official action.}
$$

This is different from adjustment. What cannot be done is to erase the hypotheses.

Examples of correct reading:

| Topic | Conservative status |
|---|---|
| leptonic hierarchy | conditional theorem in the intrinsic/product domain; warped/mixed backgrounds are an extension |
| three generations | conditional on the class of three centers, torsional equilibrium, and APS/Bismut gluing |
| sign problem | structurally closed in the geometric mechanism and reduced benchmarks; universal algorithm is future |
| absolute constants | closed by metrological calibration; the fundamental target is dimensionless ratios |
| Navier--Stokes | removed from the core; can remain as a future application |
| Bell/no-signalling | geometric entanglement formulated; real apparatuses require operational proof |

The criterion to not reopen a problem is to ask: does the new objection attack an already declared hypothesis or reveal an internal contradiction?

If it only asks for finer metrology, it does not reopen the structural result. If it shows that a hypothesis used is incompatible with the official action, then it reopens.
