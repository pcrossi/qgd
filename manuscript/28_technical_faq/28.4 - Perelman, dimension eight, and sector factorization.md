# 28.4 — Perelman, dimension eight, and sector factorization

GDQ does not naively apply Perelman's three-dimensional geometrization to an arbitrary manifold of dimension eight.

The official local bulk is:

$$
M=\mathbb R^4\times T^4,
$$

with real dimension eight. The cosmological/spectral space used in certain global calculations is:

$$
T^5\times S^3.
$$

Perelman's input is sectorial. The correct idea is not:

$$
\text{3D theorem}
\Rightarrow
\text{general 8D theorem}.
$$

The correct idea is:

$$
\text{flat frozen sector}
\oplus
\text{controlled curved sector}
\Rightarrow
\text{sectorial reduction of the flow}.
$$

When the toroidal factor is flat and decoupled in the product background, its Ricci curvature does not drive Ricci singularities. The dynamics of neckpinch, surgery, and geometric censorship remain concentrated in the curved sector. This factorization justifies using Perelman's arguments as an auxiliary sectorial structure.

The limit of this assertion must also be preserved. In warped/mixed backgrounds, with $\nabla_K f\ne0$, cross torsion, or non-product metric, the factorization can fail. In these cases, Perelman should not be used as a universal shortcut. The full Hessian of the mixed background must be diagonalized.

Conclusion:

$$
\boxed{
\text{Perelman is a sectorial auxiliary; the physical action remains the GDQ action.}
}
$$
