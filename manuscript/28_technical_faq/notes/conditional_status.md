# Conditional status

A conditional theorem has the form:

$$
(H_1\wedge H_2\wedge\cdots\wedge H_k)
\Rightarrow
R.
$$

It fails only if:

1. some hypothesis $H_i$ is false in the declared domain;
2. the mathematical implication is wrong;
3. the result $R$ contradicts another canonical part of the theory.

It does not fail just because a more general domain exists that is not yet calculated.

Example: if a result was proven in the product background, the existence of a future warped background does not invalidate it. It only prevents promoting it to a universal theorem.
