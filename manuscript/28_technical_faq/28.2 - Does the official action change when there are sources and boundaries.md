# 28.2 — Does the official action change when there are sources and boundaries?

No. The official action of QDQ is not altered for each problem.

The central physical action is:

$$
\mathcal{S}_{\mathrm{GDQ}} = \int_{\gamma}
\left[ \int_{\mathcal{M}_{\mathbb C}}
\frac{\hbar}{\Lambda_C^2}
\left[
\tau\left(\mathcal R+
g^{\mu\bar\nu}\partial_\mu f\partial_{\bar\nu}\bar f\right)
+\frac{f+\bar f}{2}-n
\right]
\mathcal U\sqrt{\det g}\,d^{2n}z
\right]\frac{d\tau}{\tau}.
$$

What changes from one problem to another is not the fundamental action. The following change:

1. the admissible background;
2. the domain;
3. the boundary conditions;
4. the conserved constraints;
5. the applied classical source;
6. the physical projector;
7. the extracted observable.

This distinction is essential. A Stern--Gerlach magnetic field, an Aharonov--Bohm solenoid, a Casimir plate, or a cosmological cavity are not new ontological terms of the action. They are external problem data: boundaries, sources, or constraints.

Formally, if $J_{\rm app}$ represents a classical probe, it enters as a controlled variation of the boundary condition or as a coupling term used to calculate the linear response:

$$
\delta\Phi_{\rm app}
=
-K_{\rm phys}^{-1}J_{\rm app}.
$$

The observable is then a contraction of this response with the measured channel:

$$
\mathcal O_{\rm obs}
=
\langle W_{\rm obs},\delta\Phi_{\rm app}\rangle.
$$

This is not a change of the action. It is the normal variational use of a fundamental action in the presence of boundary data.
