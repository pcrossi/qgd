# 28.8 — Numbers, parameters, units, and metrology

Good numbers do not replace a derivation.

The reading rule is:

$$
\boxed{
\text{numerical agreement reinforces a chain; it does not create the chain.}
}
$$

A numerical result is strong when:

1. the equation was derived beforehand;
2. the domain has been declared;
3. the boundary has been fixed;
4. the parameters were frozen before the comparison;
5. the mesh or tolerance has been refined;
6. the experimental target was not used to choose internal coefficients.

Otherwise, the result must be classified as a benchmark, phenomenological compatibility, controlled reverse engineering, or proof of concept.

It is also necessary to separate units from ratios. No physical theory predicts the number "MeV", "meter", or "kilogram" without a metrological convention. What a fundamental theory can predict are dimensionless relations:

$$
\frac{m_\mu}{m_e},
\qquad
\frac{m_\tau}{m_e},
\qquad
\alpha,
\qquad
\frac{r_p}{\ell_C},
\qquad
\frac{v_{\rm EW}}{M_p}.
$$

Once a reference scale is chosen, the other scales can be expressed as geometric ratios.
