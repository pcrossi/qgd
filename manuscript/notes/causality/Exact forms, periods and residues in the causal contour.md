# Exact forms, periods and residues in the causal contour

If $F$ is globally defined in a neighborhood of $\gamma$, then

$$
\oint_\gamma dF
=F(\gamma(1))-F(\gamma(0))
=0.
$$

If a form $\omega$ is only closed,

$$
d\omega=0,
$$

its period can be non-zero when the class

$$
[\omega]\in H^1(M;\mathbb C)
$$

is non-trivial. If $\omega$ is meromorphic,

$$
\oint_\gamma\omega
=2\pi i\sum_p\operatorname{Res}_p\omega.
$$

Thus, there are three logically distinct cases:

$$
\omega=dF
\quad\Longrightarrow\quad
\oint_\gamma\omega=0,
$$

$$
d\omega=0,
\quad
[\omega]\ne0
\quad\Longrightarrow\quad
\oint_\gamma\omega
\text{ can be a non-zero period},
$$

and

$$
\omega\text{ meromorphic}
\quad\Longrightarrow\quad
\oint_\gamma\omega
\text{ measures internal residues}.
$$

A quantization condition further requires that the normalized period represents an integral class; the residue theorem alone does not fix the physical unit $\hbar$ or the physical integer.
