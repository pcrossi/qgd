# Robertson-Schrödinger by Hermitian positivity

Heisenberg's inequality is the simplest case. In an operational sector with a positive Hermitian product, the general inequality comes from the positivity of the form:

$$
h(u,u)h(v,v)\ge |h(u,v)|^2.
$$

In geometric language, one can decompose:

$$
h=g-i\omega,
$$

where $g$ is the symmetric part and $\omega$ is the symplectic part.

Then:

$$
|h(u,v)|^2
=
g(u,v)^2+\omega(u,v)^2.
$$

Applied to the vectors associated with the fluctuations of observables, this identity leads to the Robertson--Schrödinger form:

$$
\sigma_A^2\sigma_B^2
\ge
\operatorname{Cov}(A,B)^2
+
\frac14
\left|
\langle[A,B]\rangle
\right|^2.
$$

In GDQ, the correct reading is:

- the symmetric metric part records covariance;
- the symplectic part records incompatibility;
- the inequality comes from the Hermitian positivity of the reconstructed sector;
- the Fubini--Study geometry belongs to the reconstructed Hilbert space, not to the primary bulk.

Here too, the word "Kähler" must be used with care. The general geometry of GDQ is Hermitian with Bismut torsion; the strict Kähler case is a special sector without torsion.
