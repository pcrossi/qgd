# Uncertainty by Cauchy-Schwarz in the Madelung fluid

In the regular Madelung/Nelson sector, the osmotic velocity is:

$$
u=\nu\nabla\ln\rho,
\qquad
\nu=\frac{\hbar}{2m}.
$$

Thus:

$$
mu
=
\frac{\hbar}{2}\nabla\ln\rho.
$$

Consider one dimension, with $\rho$ normalized, a regular domain, and zero flux at the boundary. Then:

$$
\int \rho\,d x=1.
$$

Define:

$$
\Delta x=x-\langle x\rangle.
$$

The central pairing is:

$$
\int
\rho\,\Delta x\,(mu)\,dx
=
\frac{\hbar}{2}
\int
\Delta x\,\partial_x\rho\,dx.
$$

By integration by parts:

$$
\int
\Delta x\,\partial_x\rho\,dx
=
-\int\rho\,dx
=
-1.
$$

Thus:

$$
\left|
\int
\rho\,\Delta x\,(mu)\,dx
\right|
=
\frac{\hbar}{2}.
$$

Applying Cauchy-Schwarz:

$$
\left(
\int\rho\,(\Delta x)^2\,dx
\right)
\left(
\int\rho\,(mu)^2\,dx
\right)
\ge
\frac{\hbar^2}{4}.
$$

Therefore:

$$
\Delta x\,\Delta p
\ge
\frac{\hbar}{2}.
$$

This proof is geometric in the GDQ sense because it uses the reconstructed density and the osmotic velocity of the Madelung sector. It does not require postulating Heisenberg's inequality as an independent axiom.

Necessary hypotheses:

- $\rho\ge0$ normalized;
- sufficiently smooth $\rho$;
- regular $R=\sqrt\rho$;
- zero flux or decay at the boundary;
- Madelung/Nelson sector with $\nu=\hbar/(2m)$;
- absence of nodal singularities in the domain used.
