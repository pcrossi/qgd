# Unitary evolution in physical time

The problem of this section is to separate two evolutions that are usually mixed up. The parameter $\tau$ belongs to the geometric/diffusive flow. The time $t$ is the physical time reconstructed on the operational leaf. The question of unitariety is about $t$, not about the Euclidean dampening in $\tau$.

If the sectorial reconstruction produces a self-adjoint physical Hamiltonian:

$$
H=H^\dagger,
$$

then the evolution in physical time is:

$$
U(t)=e^{-itH/\hbar}.
$$

By the spectral theorem, $U(t)$ is unitary:

$$
U(t)^\dagger U(t)=I.
$$

Thus, for physical states:

$$
\frac{d}{dt}
\langle \Psi(t),\Phi(t)\rangle
=0.
$$

In differential form, if

$$
i\hbar\frac{d\Psi}{dt}=H\Psi,
$$

then

$$
\frac{d}{dt}\langle\Psi,\Phi\rangle
=
\frac{i}{\hbar}\langle H\Psi,\Phi\rangle
-
\frac{i}{\hbar}\langle\Psi,H\Phi\rangle
=0,
$$

because $H$ is self-adjoint. Taking $\Phi=\Psi$:

$$
\frac{d}{dt}\|\Psi(t)\|^2=0.
$$

This resolves a frequent confusion. The geometric flow in $\tau$ can have a dissipative, entropic, or parabolic character in certain sectors. This does not mean loss of norm in physical time $t$.

The correct distinction is:

- $\tau$ parametrizes geometric flow;
- $t$ is the reconstructed physical time;
- unitariety belongs to the evolution in $t$ in the physical Hilbert space;
- dissipation in $\tau$ is not, by itself, probabilistic dissipation in the laboratory.

The result is conditional. It requires the effective sector to satisfy the necessary hypotheses to reconstruct $H$ as a self-adjoint and positive operator when appropriate.

Unstable states are not exceptions to the rule. They appear when observing only a partial channel. The closed system can have

$$
H_{\rm total}=H_{\rm total}^\dagger,
$$

but the elimination of unobserved channels can produce an effective generator

$$
H_{\rm eff}
=
H_{PP}
+
\Delta H
-
\frac{i}{2}\Gamma,
\qquad
\Gamma\ge0.
$$

In this case the norm of channel $P$ can decay, but the total norm remains preserved. The loss is projection, not a violation of fundamental unitariety.

GDQ does not prove unitary evolution by a formal rotation:

$$
t=-i\tau.
$$

The current formulation separates the complex causal variable:

$$
z_\tau=\tau+i\nu_0t,
\qquad
\nu_0=\frac{\hbar}{2m_0},
$$

with:

$$
\tau=\nu_0t.
$$
