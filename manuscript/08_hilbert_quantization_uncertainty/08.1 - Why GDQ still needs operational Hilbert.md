# Why GDQ still needs operational Hilbert

GDQ does not take Hilbert space as a primary entity. The primary layer is geometric: fields, measurement, action, constraints, boundaries, and physical sectors.

But the theory also needs to recover the operational language of quantum physics. An experiment measures spectral lines, probabilities, transitions, interference, decays, selection rules, and time evolution. To describe this with precision, it is necessary to reconstruct a structure with:

- positive inner product;
- operators with domains;
- spectrum;
- projectors;
- system composition;
- unitary evolution in physical time;
- operational probability rule.

Therefore, there are two errors that the chapter must avoid.

The first error is to say that GDQ is just quantum mechanics written differently. This would erase the fundamental geometry of the theory.

The second error is to deny the operational Hilbert space. This would render the theory unable to speak of spectrum and measurement in a way comparable to experimental physics.

A correct position is:

$$
\boxed{
\text{Hilbert is reconstructed, not postulated as primary ontology.}
}
$$

This reconstruction depends on the sector. There is no single naive $L^2$ containing all GDQ phenomena without domains, quotients, knots, holonomies, and boundary conditions.

In the Madelung regular sector, the operational space might seem familiar:

$$
\Psi=\sqrt\rho\,e^{iS_R/\hbar}.
$$

But this form is a sectorial reconstruction. The fundamental object remains the geometry:

$$
\rho=e^{-(f+\bar f)/2},
\qquad
S_R=\frac{\hbar}{2i}(f-\bar f).
$$
