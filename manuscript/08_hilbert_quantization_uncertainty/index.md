# 08. Physical Hilbert Space, Quantization, and Uncertainty

GDQ does not start by postulating an abstract Hilbert space. It starts with geometry, measurement, action, constraints, boundaries, and physical sectors. Even so, the operational Hilbert layer needs to emerge: without it, there is no spectrum, positive inner product, self-adjoint operators, unitary evolution, Born rule, or controlled treatment of uncertainty.

The goal of this chapter is to explain this reconstruction without inverting the ontology of the theory. The Hilbert space is an operational layer reconstructed from geometry; it is not the fundamental stage of GDQ.

## Central Result

The operational chain in this chapter is:

$$
\text{Geometric GDQ}
\to
\text{regular sectors}
\to
\text{reflection positivity}
\to
\mathcal H_{\rm phys}
\to
\text{self-adjoint operators}
\to
\text{unitary in }t
\to
\text{S^1-valued phase}
\to
\text{Wallstrom resolved}
\to
\text{uncertainty by positivity}.
$$

The physical Hilbert space is:

$$
\mathcal H_{\rm phys}
=
\overline{
\mathcal D_+/
(\mathcal N+\mathcal G)
}.
$$

The inner product is:

$$
\langle [F],[G]\rangle_{\mathcal H}
=
\langle \Theta F\,G\rangle_E.
$$

In the single-particle regular sector, this structure reduces to the familiar operational space:

$$
L^2(N,E,d\Sigma_h).
$$

This reduction is useful, but should not be confused with the ontological foundation of GDQ.

In logical terms, standard quantum mechanics appears here as a projective-operational sector of GDQ. The chain is:

$$
\mathcal S_{\rm GDQ}
\to
\text{Madelung regular sector}
\to
\mathcal H_{\rm phys}
\to
\text{spectral projectors}.
$$

Therefore:

$$
\boxed{
\text{Projective QM}
\subset
\text{GDQ}.
}
$$

This inclusion does not mean that GDQ abandons quantum mechanics. It means that QM is recovered when the geometry is observed in the regular, Hilbertian, and projective domain. Outside this domain — for example, in dynamic boundaries, variable domains, non-Hermitian effective Hessians, classical-quantum interfaces, and apparatus responses — GDQ preserves geometric degrees of freedom that the usual projective formulation does not describe as fundamental.

## Status of the Result

The chapter structurally closes three points:

1. operational reconstruction of the physical Hilbert space, conditioned on sectorial positivity;
2. Wallstrom resolution by the circular phase/U(1) line bundle;
3. uncertainty inequalities in the reduced regular sector.

The complete Lorentzian reconstruction for all sectors remains conditional, as it requires the appropriate positivity, domain, cluster, and reflection hypotheses. Entropic extensions, GUP, and metrological torsional corrections are a future program.
