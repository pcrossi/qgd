# Inner product, physical quotient, and domains

The physical inner product is:

$$
\langle [F],[G]\rangle_{\mathcal H}
=
\langle \Theta F\,G\rangle_E.
$$

The brackets indicate equivalence classes in the quotient:

$$
[F]\in
\mathcal D_+/(\mathcal N+\mathcal G).
$$

This is necessary because not every degree of freedom in a geometric description represents a physical excitation. The raw Hessian contains constraints, gauge directions, zero modes, and redundancies.

The physical space only emerges after:

1. imposing constraints;
2. removing zero norms;
3. quotienting redundancies;
4. completing the regular domain in the positive norm.

In a single-particle regular sector, the construction reduces to:

$$
L^2(N,E,d\Sigma_h).
$$

Here $N$ is the reconstructed spatial leaf, $E$ is the effective bundle of the sector, and $d\Sigma_h$ is the induced measure.

This reduction is not universal. It fails or needs to be refined when there are:

- nodes of $\rho$;
- multivalued phases;
- spinorial sectors;
- holonomies;
- physical boundaries;
- unlocalized massless modes;
- singular solitonic states.

Therefore, physical operators require domains. The correct statement is not "any formal expression defines an observable", but:

$$
\boxed{
\text{observables are densely defined operators on the physical sector.}
}
$$

When the physical Hessian provides a self-adjoint operator, the spectrum is operationally meaningful. When it provides only a symmetric quadratic form without a closed domain, mathematical work is still missing.
