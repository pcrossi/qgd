# Knots, holonomy, and spinorial sectors

The nodal set:

$$
Z_\rho=\{x:\rho(x)=0\}
$$

is not a technical detail. On $Z_\rho$, the amplitude vanishes and the phase ceases to be a regular coordinate. Therefore, the circular phase must be defined on:

$$
M^\ast=M\setminus Z_\rho.
$$

In each chart $U_a\subset M^\ast$, one can write:

$$
\Psi_a=\sqrt\rho\,e^{i\chi_a}s_a.
$$

On intersections:

$$
s_a=g_{ab}s_b,
\qquad
g_{ab}:U_a\cap U_b\to U(1).
$$

The curvature of the connection determines the first Chern class:

$$
c_1(L)
=
\left[\frac{F_A}{2\pi}\right]
\in
\left.H^2(M^\ast,\mathbb Z\right).
$$

This is the geometric form of circulation quantization.

The technical proof of this step is recorded in:

[[notes/wallstrom_u1_line_bundle|Proof of circulation quantization by U(1) bundle]]

Spinorial sectors require additional care. The $S^1$-valued phase resolves the integrality of the scalar sector, but does not replace the spin construction. Spin involves internal circulation, Hopf structure, antiperiodic sectors, and its own global conditions.

Therefore:

$$
\boxed{
\text{Resolved scalar Wallstrom is not a complete proof of all spin sectors.}
}
$$

This separation avoids confusing:

- scalar phase circulation;
- $U(1)$ holonomy;
- spinorial antiperiodicity;
- fermionic statistics;
- spin response to an apparatus.
