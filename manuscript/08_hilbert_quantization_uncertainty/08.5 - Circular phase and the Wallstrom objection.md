# Circular phase and the Wallstrom objection

The Wallstrom objection arises when attempting to reconstruct quantum mechanics solely from local hydrodynamic equations.

The local equations formally allow:

$$
\oint_C\nabla S_R\cdot dx
=
\alpha h,
\qquad
\alpha\in\mathbb R.
$$

But quantum physics requires:

$$
\oint_C\nabla S_R\cdot dx
=
Nh,
\qquad
N\in\mathbb Z.
$$

The error would be trying to obtain this integrality solely from a Poisson summation. The sum:

$$
\sum_{m\in\mathbb Z}e^{im\epsilon}
=
2\pi
\sum_{n\in\mathbb Z}\delta(\epsilon-2\pi n)
$$

is correct as a distributional identity, but already presupposes $m\in\mathbb Z$. It is harmonic analysis on the circle; it is not the origin of the circle.

In GDQ, the correct origin is global:

$$
e^{iS_R/\hbar}:M^\ast\to S^1,
$$

where:

$$
M^\ast=M\setminus Z_\rho,
\qquad
Z_\rho=\{x:\rho(x)=0\}.
$$

More precisely, the reconstructed state is a section of a Hermitian line bundle:

$$
\Psi\in\Gamma(L),
\qquad
L\to M^\ast.
$$

Writing:

$$
\chi=\frac{S_R}{\hbar},
$$

the phase is an angular coordinate. Therefore:

$$
\frac1{2\pi}
\oint_C d\chi
\in\mathbb Z.
$$

Equivalently:

$$
\oint_C dS_R=Nh.
$$

The integrality is topological. States with non-integer circulation are not admissible global sections of the physical sector.
