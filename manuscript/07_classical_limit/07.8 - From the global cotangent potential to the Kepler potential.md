# From the global cotangent potential to the Kepler potential

## 1. Why the global potential should not be $1/r$

The potential $1/r$ is the radial harmonic solution of flat three-dimensional space. In a closed cosmological space, the global structure and compensation conditions are different. The bridge of Chapter 6 allows us to compare the two regimes without identifying their spaces.

Consider a slice $S^3$ of radius $R_E$ and the angular distance

$$
\chi=\frac{r}{R_E},
\qquad
0<\chi<\pi.
$$

For a radial function $V(\chi)$, the Laplacian is

$$
\Delta_{S^3}V
=\frac{1}{R_E^2\sin^2\chi}
\frac{d}{d\chi}
\left(\sin^2\chi\frac{dV}{d\chi}\right).
$$

## 2. Radial solution away from sources

In the source-free region, $\Delta_{S^3}V=0$. Therefore,

$$
\frac{d}{d\chi}
\left(\sin^2\chi\frac{dV}{d\chi}\right)=0,
$$

and there exists a constant $C$ such that

$$
\sin^2\chi\frac{dV}{d\chi}=C.
$$

Since $\int\csc^2\chi\,d\chi=-\cot\chi$, we obtain

$$
V(\chi)=V_0-C\cot\chi.
$$

Fixing the physical normalization of the source by a constant $\kappa$,

$$
\boxed{
V_E(r)
=V_0-\frac{\kappa}{R_E}
\cot\left(\frac{r}{R_E}\right).
}
$$

The compact space requires a compensated total charge. The antipodal configuration $(q,-q)$ recorded in the global--local bridge is the minimal realization of this condition; the cotangent potential has opposite singularities at the poles.

## 3. Local limit

For $r\ll R_E$,

$$
\cot x
=\frac1x-\frac{x}{3}-\frac{x^3}{45}+O(x^5).
$$

Substituting $x=r/R_E$,

$$
\frac1{R_E}\cot\left(\frac r{R_E}\right)
=\frac1r-\frac{r}{3R_E^2}
-\frac{r^3}{45R_E^4}
+O\left(\frac{r^5}{R_E^6}\right).
$$

Therefore,

$$
\boxed{
V_E(r)
=V_0-\frac{\kappa}{r}
+\frac{\kappa r}{3R_E^2}
+O\left(\frac{r^3}{R_E^4}\right).
}
$$

The Kepler potential is not imposed on the global space. It is the first term of the local expansion of the cotangent Green's function in Einstein's Universe.

## 4. Force and curvature correction

The radial force is

$$
F_r=-\frac{dV_E}{dr}
=-\frac{\kappa}{R_E^2}
\csc^2\left(\frac r{R_E}\right).
$$

In the local limit,

$$
F_r
=-\frac{\kappa}{r^2}
-\frac{\kappa}{3R_E^2}
+O\left(\frac{r^2}{R_E^4}\right).
$$

## 5. Status of the normalization

The cotangent form and its limit $1/r$ follow from geometry and global conditions. The constant $\kappa$ is not determined by the harmonic equation in isolation. Its identification with an electrical, gravitational, or other coupling requires the norm of the mode and the response of the official Hessian.
