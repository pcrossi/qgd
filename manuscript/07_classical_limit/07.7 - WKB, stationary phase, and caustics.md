# WKB, stationary phase, and caustics

## 1. The same reduction in wave language

In the reconstructed sector, write

$$
\Psi=R\,e^{iS_R/\hbar}.
$$

Substituting this form into an effective Schrödinger equation,

$$
i\hbar\partial_t\Psi
=-\frac{\hbar^2}{2m}\Delta\Psi+V\Psi,
$$

and separating the real and imaginary parts, we recover exactly

$$
\partial_tS_R
+\frac{|\nabla S_R|^2}{2m}
+V
-\frac{\hbar^2}{2m}\frac{\Delta R}{R}=0
$$

and

$$
\partial_t(R^2)
+\nabla\cdot
\left(R^2\frac{\nabla S_R}{m}\right)=0.
$$

This equivalence verifies the Madelung reduction; it does not replace the derivation of the amplitude operator from the official action.

Indeed,

$$
\partial_t\Psi
=e^{iS_R/\hbar}
\left(
\partial_tR
+\frac{i}{\hbar}R\partial_tS_R
\right)
$$

and

$$
\Delta\Psi
=e^{iS_R/\hbar}
\left[
\Delta R
+\frac{2i}{\hbar}\nabla R\cdot\nabla S_R
+\frac{i}{\hbar}R\Delta S_R
-\frac{1}{\hbar^2}R|\nabla S_R|^2
\right].
$$

Equating the real parts yields Hamilton–Jacobi–Bohm. Equating the imaginary parts, multiplying by $2R$, and using $\rho=R^2$ yields continuity.

## 2. WKB expansion

Consider formally, taking real $a_0$ at leading order,

$$
\Psi
=e^{iS_0/\hbar}
\left(a_0+\hbar a_1+\hbar^2a_2+\cdots\right).
$$

Ordering the powers of $\hbar$, the leading order yields

$$
\partial_tS_0
+\frac{|\nabla S_0|^2}{2m}
+V=0.
$$

The next order yields

$$
\partial_t(a_0^2)
+\nabla\cdot
\left(a_0^2\frac{\nabla S_0}{m}\right)=0.
$$

Hamilton–Jacobi and continuity are the first two equations of the semiclassical hierarchy.

## 3. Stationary phase

In an oscillatory integral

$$
I(\hbar)=\int a(x)e^{iS(x)/\hbar}\,dx,
$$

regions where $\nabla_x S\neq0$ cancel out due to rapid oscillation. The primary contributions come from the stationary points, $\nabla_xS=0$. In a path integral, the corresponding condition is functional, $\delta S/\delta x(t)=0$, and selects the extremizing trajectories of the classical action. This is the integral version of the same correspondence principle.

## 4. Caustics and Maslov index

When the determinant of the map between initial data and final position vanishes, the WKB amplitudes diverge and a caustic is formed. The classical trajectory does not cease to exist; rather, the representation by a single smooth branch of $S_0$ fails.

It is necessary to decompose the solution into multiple branches, use uniform approximations, track the Maslov phases, or work with a measure in phase space. The Maslov index records the passage through caustics. It should not be confused, without additional construction, with a proof of intrinsic spin.

## 5. Not a reverse Wick rotation

The WKB limit occurs in the already reconstructed physical time. We do not need to impose $\tau\mapsto it$ to obtain Hamilton–Jacobi. The flow parameter $\tau$, the causal contour $\gamma$, and the physical time $t$ have distinct roles, established in Chapters 3 and 6.
