# Continuity, ensemble, and the Liouville equation

## 1. Density transport

In the classical limit, the velocity is $\mathbf v=\nabla S_0/m$ and

$$
\partial_t\rho_0+\nabla\cdot(\rho_0\mathbf v)=0.
$$

Expanding the divergence,

$$
\frac{D\rho_0}{Dt}
=-\rho_0\nabla\cdot\mathbf v,
\qquad
\frac{D}{Dt}=\partial_t+\mathbf v\cdot\nabla.
$$

Along a characteristic, the density changes due to the expansion or compression of the bundle of trajectories.

## 2. Conservation of normalization

Integrating over a region $\Omega$,

$$
\frac{d}{dt}\int_\Omega\rho_0\,d^dx
=-\int_{\partial\Omega}
\rho_0\mathbf v\cdot\mathbf n\,d\Sigma.
$$

If there is no boundary flux, the integral of $\rho_0$ is constant. The probabilistic normalization of the quantum sector becomes the conservation of mass or of the number of the classical ensemble, depending on the physical normalization adopted.

## 3. Lifting to phase space

Before a caustic, a single phase defines $p=\nabla S_0(x,t)$. Define the monokinetic measure

$$
F(x,p,t)
=\rho_0(x,t)
\delta\bigl(p-\nabla S_0(x,t)\bigr).
$$

Using simultaneously the continuity and Hamilton's equations, one verifies, in the sense of distributions,

$$
\boxed{
\partial_tF
+\frac{\partial H}{\partial p}\cdot\nabla_xF
-\frac{\partial H}{\partial x}\cdot\nabla_pF
=0.
}
$$

This is the Liouville equation.

To unpack the verification, take a smooth test function $\varphi(x,p,t)$. By the definition of $F$,

$$
\int F\varphi\,d^dx\,d^dp
=\int\rho_0(x,t)
\varphi(x,\nabla S_0(x,t),t)\,d^dx.
$$

Differentiating in time, using continuity to integrate the $\partial_t\rho_0$ term by parts, and using the characteristic equations for the derivative of $\nabla S_0$, we obtain

$$
\frac d{dt}\int F\varphi\,d^dx\,d^dp
=\int F
\left[
\partial_t\varphi
+\frac{\partial H}{\partial p}\cdot\nabla_x\varphi
-\frac{\partial H}{\partial x}\cdot\nabla_p\varphi
\right]d^dx\,d^dp.
$$

This is precisely the weak formulation of the Liouville equation displayed above. Thus, the transition does not depend on informally identifying the quantum density with the classical density: it follows from the joint transport of $\rho_0$ and $\nabla S_0$.

## 4. After caustics

After a caustic, multiple momenta can reach the same point $x$. The monokinetic description ceases to hold, but Liouville accommodates a multivalued distribution in momentum. The correct chain is

$$
\text{Regular Madelung}
\longrightarrow
\text{Hamilton–Jacobi}
\longrightarrow
\text{Monokinetic Liouville}
\longrightarrow
\text{General Liouville after branch mixing}.
$$

The classical limit does not destroy the statistical structure. It converts it into a statistics over classical trajectories.
