# Preservation map of Chapter 7

This file records how the content of the legacy chapter on the classical limit was incorporated into the restructured edition.

## 1. Three reduction operators

The legacy combined:

1. $\hbar\to0$;
2. macroscopic noise cancellation;
3. reverse Wick rotation.

In the current edition, these processes have been separated. The demonstrated limit is controlled by

$$
\varepsilon_{\rm cl}
=\frac{\hbar}{pL_\rho}.
$$

Macroscopic averaging and torsion reduction are additional operations. The reverse Wick rotation was removed from the proof because physical time is already obtained by the causal reconstruction and by the global--local bridge.

## 2. Hamilton–Jacobi and classical mechanics

**Preserved and strengthened.** The intuition of the relative vanishing of the Bohm term was converted into a dimensionless estimate, under hypotheses of regularity, a node-free domain, and an error of order $O(\varepsilon_{\rm cl}^2)$. The proofs of Hamilton's characteristics, Newton's second law, the transition to Liouville, and the WKB hierarchy were added.

## 3. Maxwell electromagnetism

**Preserved and incorporated in Section 7.11.** The identity $F=dA$, the Bianchi identity, the quadratic form of the $U(1)_Q$ mode, and its variation yield Maxwell's equations. The longitudinal/transverse interpretation of the legacy was maintained. The absolute normalization remains a separate calculation of $Z_Q$.

## 4. Einstein gravitation

**Preserved and incorporated in Section 7.12.** The isotropization of torsion, the identity of the Hessian of $f$, the hydrodynamic closure, and the dimensional analysis were maintained. The sign was fixed by the attractive limit, and the factor $8\pi$ was derived by comparing the $00$ component with Poisson's. The global normalization of $G$ and possible torsional residuals remain sectoral.

## 5. New content required for the proof

The restructured edition added:

- distinction between semiclassical limit, average, and depolarization;
- domain of validity and failures at nodes, boundaries, and caustics;
- difference between classical ensemble and single trajectory;
- weak derivation of the Liouville equation;
- relation between WKB and Madelung;
- global cotangent potential and its local Kepler limit;
- conservation of energy, momentum, and angular momentum by Noether;
- precise conditional status of the result.

No legacy idea was erased. The statements were redistributed according to the operator and sector of the official action required to demonstrate them.

## 6. Addendum on $g-2$ and mesons

The legacy note on anomalies and applications was checked and preserved, but it was not incorporated as a proof of the classical limit. It proposes phenomenological extensions for the muon magnetic moment and meson decays. Their coefficients require their own sectoral derivation and numerical audit; therefore, they belong to the subsequent phenomenological program, rather than to the classical correspondence demonstrated in this chapter.
