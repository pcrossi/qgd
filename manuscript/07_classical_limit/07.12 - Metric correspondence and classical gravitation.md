# Metric correspondence and classical gravitation

## 1. Structural elements of the correspondence

The gravitational correspondence is organized into three parts:

1. macroscopic average of torsion;
2. stabilization of the metric background;
3. identification of the hydrodynamic stress contained in $f$.

These elements are kept. The presentation below corrects only the logical order and the sign of the constitutive relation, which must be fixed by the attractive Newtonian limit.

## 2. From the Bismut connection to the macroscopic connection

For a totally antisymmetric torsion $H$, the Bismut connection can be written, depending on the adopted sign convention, as

$$
\Gamma^{B\,a}{}_{bc}
=\Gamma^{LC\,a}{}_{bc}
+\frac12H^a{}_{bc}.
$$

In an unpolarized macroscopic ensemble, suppose that the average of the torsion at resolution scale $L$ satisfies

$$
\langle H^a{}_{bc}\rangle_L
\longrightarrow0.
$$

Then

$$
\Gamma^B
\longrightarrow
\Gamma^{LC}
$$

in the average observable. The limit connection is the torsion-free connection compatible with the metric.

This transition is conditional on depolarization. In a polarized body, a rotating medium, or near the core of a defect, $\langle H\rangle_L$ may be non-zero and the torsional correction must be preserved.

## 3. The exact identity of the density Hessian

In the real projection,

$$
\rho=e^{-f_R},
\qquad
f_R=-\ln\rho.
$$

Hence,

$$
\nabla_\mu f_R
=-\frac{1}{\rho}\nabla_\mu\rho.
$$

Applying another covariant derivative,

$$
\begin{aligned}
\nabla_\mu\nabla_\nu f_R
&=\nabla_\mu
\left(-\frac1\rho\nabla_\nu\rho\right)
\\
&=\frac{1}{\rho^2}
(\nabla_\mu\rho)(\nabla_\nu\rho)
-\frac1\rho\nabla_\mu\nabla_\nu\rho.
\end{aligned}
$$

Therefore,

$$
\boxed{
\nabla_\mu\nabla_\nu f_R
= \nabla_\mu f_R\nabla_\nu f_R
-\frac1\rho\nabla_\mu\nabla_\nu\rho.
}
$$

This identity is exact. It separates a bilinear transport part and a stress part associated with the variation of the density.

## 4. Direct reduction of the official metric equation

Chapter 5 derived, prior to physical projection, the weighted metric equation

$$
\begin{aligned}
0={}&\tau\mathcal U
\left(R_{\mu\nu}+P_{\mu\nu}^{(f)}\right)
\\
&+\tau\left(
g_{\mu\nu}\Delta\mathcal U
-\nabla_\mu\nabla_\nu\mathcal U
\right)
\\
&-\frac12\mathcal U
\left(\mathcal L_0-\lambda\right)g_{\mu\nu}.
\end{aligned}
$$

Write, in the real chart,

$$
f=f_R+i\theta,
\qquad
\mathcal U\propto e^{-f_R}.
$$

Here $\theta=S_R/\hbar$, and the proportionality in the measure contains only the constant normalization factor in the internal directions of the slice under consideration.

Then

$$
P_{\mu\nu}^{(f)}
=\nabla_\mu f_R\nabla_\nu f_R
+\nabla_\mu\theta\nabla_\nu\theta
$$

and

$$
\frac{\nabla_\mu\nabla_\nu\mathcal U}{\mathcal U}
=-\nabla_\mu\nabla_\nu f_R
+\nabla_\mu f_R\nabla_\nu f_R.
$$

The quadratic products of $\nabla f_R$ cancel exactly between the field stress and the derivative of the weight. After dividing by $\tau\mathcal U$, there remains

$$
R_{\mu\nu}
+\nabla_\mu\nabla_\nu f_R
+\nabla_\mu\theta\nabla_\nu\theta
+\Xi g_{\mu\nu}=0,
$$

where

$$
\Xi
=-\Delta f_R+|\nabla f_R|^2
-\frac{\mathcal L_0-\lambda}{2\tau}
$$

gathers the trace sector. This transition directly links the intuition of the Hessian to the variation of the official action; it does not use Perelman's auxiliary functional as a substitute for the physical action.

## 5. Macroscopic hydrodynamic closure

For a perfect fluid, the classical tensor is

$$
T_{\mu\nu}
=\left(\epsilon+p\right)
\frac{u_\mu u_\nu}{c^2}
+p\,g_{\mu\nu}.
$$

The terms of phase, density Hessian, and trace response are interpreted as convective transport, pressure, and internal energy. After averaging on the macroscopic scale, this identification yields the trace-reversed constitutive relation

$$
R_{\mu\nu}
=\kappa_G
\left(
\text{T}_{\mu\nu}-\frac12g_{\mu\nu}T
\right)
+\Lambda g_{\mu\nu}.
$$

Here $T_{\mu\nu}$ is not inserted as a new fundamental source: it is the macroscopic name given to the conserved average of the phase stress, density, pressure, and torsion that appears in the official metric equation. The normalization $\kappa_G$ and the background $\Lambda$ must still be fixed by the global scale and the physical limit.

## 6. Einstein form

Contracting in four dimensions,

$$
R=-\kappa_GT+4\Lambda.
$$

Substituting this trace back,

$$
\boxed{
\text{R}_{\mu\nu}
-\frac12g_{\mu\nu}R
+\Lambda g_{\mu\nu}
=\kappa_GT_{\mu\nu}.
}
$$

Thus, once the hydrodynamic closure is established, the Einstein form is an algebraic consequence of the stationary metric equation. It is not necessary to postulate the field equations again.

## 7. Dimension of the coupling

Curvature has dimension

$$
[R_{\mu\nu}]=L^{-2},
$$

while stress has dimension

$$
[T_{\mu\nu}]=M L^{-1}T^{-2}.
$$

Therefore,

$$
[\kappa_G]
=M^{-1}L^{-1}T^2.
$$

Since

$$
[G]=M^{-1}L^3T^{-2},
\qquad
[c]=LT^{-1},
$$

it follows that

$$
\left[\frac{G}{c^4}\right]
=M^{-1}L^{-1}T^2.
$$

Dimensional analysis therefore dictates,

$$
\kappa_G=C_G\frac{G}{c^4},
$$

with dimensionless $C_G$.

## 8. Fixing the factor $8\pi$ by the Newtonian limit

In the weak-field and non-relativistic source regime,

$$
T_{00}\simeq\rho_m c^2
$$

and

$$
G_{00}\simeq\frac{2}{c^2}\nabla^2\Phi.
$$

The $00$ component of the metric equation yields

$$
\frac{2}{c^2}\nabla^2\Phi
=\kappa_G\rho_m c^2.
$$

Therefore,

$$
\nabla^2\Phi
=\frac{\kappa_Gc^4}{2}\rho_m.
$$

Comparing with Poisson's equation, whose spherical normalization is

$$
\nabla^2\Phi=4\pi G\rho_m,
$$

we obtain

$$
\boxed{
\kappa_G=\frac{8\pi G}{c^4}.
}
$$

The factor $4\pi$ comes from the three-dimensional angular flux; the additional factor of $2$ comes from the relation between $G_{00}$ and the Newtonian potential. This makes the geometric origin of the angular factor precise.

## 9. Cotangent potential and weak field

In the global space $S^3$, the radial solution is cotangent. In the local chart,

$$
-\frac{\kappa}{R_E}
\cot\left(\frac r{R_E}\right)
\longrightarrow
-\frac{\kappa}{r}.
$$

For gravitation, the Newtonian normalization identifies $\kappa=Gm_1m_2$ in the energy potential of two masses, after $G$ has been fixed by the metric response. Thus, Kepler's law in Section 7.8 and the weak limit of Einstein's equation are two descriptions of the same local regime.

## 10. Residual torsion

In a polarized environment, the torsional average may not vanish. The general parameterization is

$$
\langle H\rangle_L=\delta H_L.
$$

If $\delta H_L\neq0$, the macroscopic connection contains a contortion correction, and the gravitational response can depend on orientation, rotation, or polarization. This is a legitimate experimental hypothesis, but its magnitude is not determined by the correspondence argument. Proposals involving the Earth's rotation or variations in measurements of $G$ should be treated as future tests, not as already verified consequences.

The important technical point is that, for totally antisymmetric torsion, the purely geodesic correction proportional to $H^\mu{}_{\nu\rho}u^\nu u^\rho$ vanishes:

$$
H^\mu{}_{\nu\rho}u^\nu u^\rho
=0,
$$

because $H^\mu{}_{\nu\rho}$ is antisymmetric in $\nu,\rho$, while $u^\nu u^\rho$ is symmetric. Therefore, observable residuals require additional structure: spin/polarization, rotation, collective anisotropy, boundaries, or non-homogeneous gradients. This preserves the weak equivalence in the unpolarized macroscopic sector.

## 11. Cosmological constant

The term $\Lambda$ represents the homogeneous background allowed by the macroscopic equation and cosmological conditions. It should not be automatically called "entropy residue" without evaluating the global background of the official action. Its relationship to the vacuum energy density belongs to the corresponding cosmological sector.

## 12. Complete verdict of the chapter

The classical limit is organized into three correspondences:

$$
\begin{aligned}
&\text{scalar Madelung sector}
&&\longrightarrow
\text{Hamilton–Jacobi, Newton, and Liouville},
\\
&\text{vector sector }U(1)_Q
&&\longrightarrow
\text{Maxwell},
\\
&\text{stationary metric sector}
&&\longrightarrow
\text{Einstein and its Newtonian limit}.
\end{aligned}
$$

The reduced forms are consequences of the elements already constructed. What remains dependent on their own sectoral calculations are the absolute normalizations, the admissible backgrounds, and any torsional residuals.
