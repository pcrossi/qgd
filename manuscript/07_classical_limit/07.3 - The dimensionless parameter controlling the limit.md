# The dimensionless parameter controlling the limit

## 1. Local scales

Define $R=\sqrt\rho$ and $p=|\nabla S_R|$. In a node-free region, we introduce a scale $L_\rho$ for the variation of the amplitude by

$$
\left|\frac{\Delta R}{R}\right|
\leq\frac{C_R}{L_\rho^2},
$$

where $C_R$ is a controlled dimensionless constant. The local kinetic energy and the reduced de Broglie wavelength are

$$
T_{\rm cl}=\frac{p^2}{2m},
\qquad
\lambda_{\rm dB}^{\rm red}=\frac{\hbar}{p}.
$$

## 2. Estimate of the Bohm term

By the definition of $Q_B$,

$$
|Q_B|
\leq\frac{\hbar^2}{2m}\frac{C_R}{L_\rho^2}.
$$

Dividing by the kinetic energy and defining

$$
\boxed{
\varepsilon_{\rm cl}
=\frac{\hbar}{pL_\rho}
=\frac{\lambda_{\rm dB}^{\rm red}}{L_\rho},
}
$$

we obtain

$$
\boxed{
\frac{|Q_B|}{T_{\rm cl}}
\leq C_R\varepsilon_{\rm cl}^2.
}
$$

This is the estimate that controls the limit. The Bohm correction is of second order in the ratio between the quantum scale of the phase and the macroscopic scale of the amplitude.

## 3. Formulation by characteristic action

If $S_{\rm char}=pL_\rho$, then

$$
\varepsilon_{\rm cl}=\frac{\hbar}{S_{\rm char}}.
$$

Thus, the classical regime is $S_{\rm char}\gg\hbar$. This formulation explains why it is not necessary to change the value of $\hbar$. A macroscopic body has momentum and a spatial scale so large that its characteristic action contains many units of $\hbar$.

## 4. Bohm force

To compare trajectories, controlling the energy is not enough. The acceleration contains

$$
\mathbf F_B=-\nabla Q_B.
$$

If the amplitude has a controlled third derivative on the scale $L_\rho$, then

$$
|\nabla Q_B|
=O\left(\frac{\hbar^2}{mL_\rho^3}\right).
$$

Compared to a typical classical force $p^2/(mL_V)$,

$$
\frac{|\mathbf F_B|}{|\mathbf F_{\rm cl}|}
=O\left(\varepsilon_{\rm cl}^2\frac{L_V}{L_\rho}\right).
$$

## 5. Where the criterion fails

The parameter ceases to be small or well-defined at nodes, sharp boundaries, interference fringes, turning points, caustics, and topological defects whose core remains microscopic. An object can be macroscopic and still exhibit a quantum correction in a narrow layer or in a protected internal degree of freedom.
