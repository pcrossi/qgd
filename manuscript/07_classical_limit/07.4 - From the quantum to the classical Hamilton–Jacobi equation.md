# From the quantum to the classical Hamilton–Jacobi equation

## 1. Semiclassical family

Consider a family $(\rho_\varepsilon,S_\varepsilon)$ of solutions of the Madelung sector in a node-free domain $K$. Suppose that $\rho_\varepsilon\geq\rho_*>0$, that $R_\varepsilon=\sqrt{\rho_\varepsilon}$ has uniformly controlled second-order derivatives, and that $\varepsilon_{\rm cl}\to0$.

Then the estimate of the previous section implies

$$
Q_B[\rho_\varepsilon]\to0
$$

uniformly in $K$, on the scale of the adopted classical energy.

## 2. Taking the limit

The exact equation is

$$
\partial_tS_\varepsilon
+\frac{|\nabla S_\varepsilon|^2}{2m}
+V+Q_B[\rho_\varepsilon]=0.
$$

If $S_\varepsilon\to S_0$ in a topology that preserves the first derivative, taking the limit gives

$$
\boxed{
\partial_tS_0
+\frac{|\nabla S_0|^2}{2m}
+V=0.
}
$$

This is the classical Hamilton–Jacobi equation for

$$
H(x,p,t)=\frac{|p|^2}{2m}+V(x,t).
$$

The residual error locally satisfies

$$
\partial_tS_\varepsilon
+H(x,\nabla S_\varepsilon,t)
=O(\varepsilon_{\rm cl}^2T_{\rm cl}).
$$

## 3. What happened to the density

Continuity does not vanish:

$$
\partial_t\rho_0
+\nabla\cdot
\left(\rho_0\frac{\nabla S_0}{m}\right)=0.
$$

It proceeds to transport a classical distribution along the characteristics of $S_0$. The passage to the determinism of a single trajectory additionally requires that the initial distribution be concentrated in a small region of phase space. The semiclassical limit, on its own, produces a classical ensemble dynamics, not necessarily a single point particle.

## 4. Covariant form

On a spatial slice with metric $h_{ij}$, the Hamiltonian is

$$
H(x,p,t)=\frac{1}{2m}h^{ij}p_ip_j+V.
$$

The limit equation becomes

$$
\partial_tS_0
+\frac{1}{2m}h^{ij}\partial_iS_0\partial_jS_0
+V=0.
$$

The reasoning remains the same, replacing gradients and divergences with operators compatible with $h$. The Lorentzian reconstruction and the choice of physical time precede this reduction; they are not produced by the limit $\varepsilon_{\rm cl}\to0$.

## 5. Status of the theorem

The result is conditional on the selection of the Madelung sector, the kinetic normalization defining $m$, the uniform regularity of the amplitude, the absence of nodes in the domain, the convergence of the phase, and boundary control. Under these hypotheses, the reduction is an asymptotic proof.
