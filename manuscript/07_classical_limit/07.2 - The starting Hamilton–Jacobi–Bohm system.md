# The starting Hamilton–Jacobi–Bohm system

## 1. The physical sector used in this chapter

The official action has the extended Cauchy space

$$
(\rho,p_\rho,S_R,\Pi_{S_R}).
$$

The quantum hydrodynamic sector employed here is the polarization

$$
p_\rho=0,
\qquad
\Pi_{S_R}=\sqrt h\,\rho.
$$

This selection does not modify the official action. It restricts the dynamics to the sector in which $(\rho,S_R)$ are the canonical coordinates of the normalized space of states. Chapter 6 explained the meaning and scope of this reduction.

## 2. Reduced physical action

In a local non-relativistic chart, we write the reduced action of the sector as

$$
I_{\rm M}[\rho,S_R]
=\int dt\int_{\Sigma_t}
\left[
\rho\left(
\partial_tS_R
+\frac{|\nabla S_R|^2}{2m}
+V
\right)
+\frac{\hbar^2}{8m}
\frac{|\nabla\rho|^2}{\rho}
\right]d\Sigma.
$$

The global sign of this action is conventional and does not alter its equations. The Fisher term is the physical form of the amplitude contribution derived in Chapter 5. This formula is an **effective reduction** of the official action after selecting the chart, mass, and polarization; it is not a new fundamental action.

## 3. Variation of the phase

Keeping $\rho$ fixed,

$$
\delta_{S_R}I_{\rm M}
=\int dt\int_{\Sigma_t}
\left[
\rho\,\partial_t\delta S_R
+\frac{\rho}{m}
\nabla S_R\cdot\nabla\delta S_R
\right]d\Sigma.
$$

Integrating by parts and vanishing the boundary term,

$$
\delta_{S_R}I_{\rm M}
=-\int dt\int_{\Sigma_t}
\left[
\partial_t\rho
+\nabla\cdot
\left(\rho\frac{\nabla S_R}{m}\right)
\right]\delta S_R\,d\Sigma.
$$

Since $\delta S_R$ is arbitrary,

$$
\boxed{
\partial_t\rho+\nabla\cdot(\rho\mathbf v)=0,
\qquad
\mathbf v=\frac{\nabla S_R}{m}.
}
$$

## 4. Variation of the density

The term that requires care is

$$
F[\rho]
=\frac{\hbar^2}{8m}
\int\frac{|\nabla\rho|^2}{\rho}\,d\Sigma.
$$

For $\rho\mapsto\rho+\varepsilon\eta$,

$$
\delta F
=\frac{\hbar^2}{8m}
\int
\left[
\frac{2\nabla\rho\cdot\nabla\eta}{\rho}
-\frac{|\nabla\rho|^2}{\rho^2}\eta
\right]d\Sigma.
$$

After an integration by parts,

$$
\frac{\delta F}{\delta\rho}
=-\frac{\hbar^2}{2m}
\frac{\Delta\sqrt\rho}{\sqrt\rho}.
$$

Therefore, the Euler–Lagrange equation for $\rho$ is

$$
\boxed{
\partial_tS_R
+\frac{|\nabla S_R|^2}{2m}
+V+Q_B=0,
}
$$

where

$$
Q_B
=-\frac{\hbar^2}{2m}
\frac{\Delta\sqrt\rho}{\sqrt\rho}.
$$

## 5. The coupled system

The starting dynamics of the classical limit are, therefore,

$$
\begin{cases}
\displaystyle
\partial_tS_R+\dfrac{|\nabla S_R|^2}{2m}+V+Q_B=0,
\\
\displaystyle
\partial_t\rho+
\nabla\cdot\left(\rho\dfrac{\nabla S_R}{m}\right)=0.
\end{cases}
$$

The first equation determines the phase fronts; the second transports their density. The classical limit must show when $Q_B$ is small without vanishing the second equation.
