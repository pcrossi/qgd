# What it means to take the classical limit

## 1. The correspondence principle

The correspondence principle requires more than verbal similarity between two theories. One must exhibit:

1. the microscopic variables that survive;
2. the dimensionless parameter that becomes small;
3. the limit equation;
4. the estimate of the discarded error;
5. the regions where the approximation fails.

In QGD, the density $\rho$ and the phase $S_R$ are not added to construct the limit. They already come from the complex field:

$$
\rho=e^{-(f+\bar f)/2},
\qquad
S_R=\frac{\hbar}{2i}(f-\bar f).
$$

The Bohm operator is also not imported. Chapter 5 showed that the variation of the density of the official action produces the combination

$$
\frac{\Delta_g\sqrt\rho}{\sqrt\rho}.
$$

After physical reconstruction and kinetic normalization, this combination appears with the coefficient $-\hbar^2/(2m)$.

## 2. Three operations that must be separated

It is common to call mathematically different operations the "classical limit":

- Semiclassical limit, in which the typical action is large compared to $\hbar$;
- Macroscopic average, which eliminates unresolved fast fluctuations;
- Selection of a sector approximately free of net torsion or observable internal excitations.

These operations may occur together in an experiment, but none automatically implies the others. In this chapter, the basic proof uses only the first mechanism: the controlled smallness of the Bohm term.

## 3. Why writing $\hbar\to0$ is not enough

Consider

$$
Q_B
=-\frac{\hbar^2}{2m}
\frac{\Delta R}{R},
\qquad
R=\sqrt\rho.
$$

Even with a small coefficient $\hbar^2$, the ratio $\Delta R/R$ can grow as $1/\hbar^2$ near a node, a boundary layer, or a structure that shrinks with the quantum scale. Therefore, $\hbar\to0$ does not uniformly imply $Q_B\to0$ for all families of states. One must simultaneously control the regularity and scale of the amplitude.

Physically, $\hbar$ remains fixed. The classical regime is reached when

$$
\text{characteristic action}\gg\hbar,
$$

or, equivalently, when the reduced de Broglie wavelength is much smaller than the scale over which the amplitude and the potential vary.

## 4. Adopted domain

The theorem of this chapter will be formulated in a local physical chart $\Sigma_t$, after the global--local bridge, under the following conditions:

- selected Madelung sector;
- positive effective mass $m$;
- density $\rho>0$ in the region under study;
- amplitude at least twice differentiable;
- sufficiently regular real potential;
- absence of leakage through the boundary, or explicitly accounted boundary flux;
- time interval prior to the formation of a single-phase caustic.

Nodes and caustics do not refute the classical limit. They indicate that a single phase function ceases to be a sufficient chart and must be replaced by multiple branches, a phase measure, or a uniform description.
