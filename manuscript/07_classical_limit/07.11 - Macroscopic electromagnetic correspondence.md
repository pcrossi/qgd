# Macroscopic electromagnetic correspondence

## 1. The starting point already constructed

The classical limit of the electromagnetic sector does not need to be reconstructed from the scalar phase equation. The previous chapters have already provided the elements to be reduced:

- a primitive internal direction $U(1)_Q$;
- an effective connection $A$ associated with this direction;
- a connection curvature;
- a conserved current;
- a quadratic form obtained from the physical Hessian;
- the global--local bridge transporting the mode and its current.

In this section, we use these elements. The global normalization of the quadratic form, which determines the absolute value of the coupling, is a calculation distinct from the formal correspondence.

## 2. Connection curvature

In the physical chart, the reduced connection is a real 1-form

$$
A=A_a\,dx^a.
$$

Its curvature is

$$
F=dA,
$$

or, in components,

$$
F_{ab}=\partial_aA_b-\partial_bA_a.
$$

Since $d^2=0$,

$$
dF=0.
$$

In covariant notation,

$$
\nabla_{[a}F_{bc]}=0.
$$

This is a geometric identity, not an added phenomenological equation.

## 3. Quadratic form and source equation

After the physical projection of the $U(1)_Q$ mode, in the isotropic, even, and lowest-derivative-order sector, the gauge-invariant local quadratic part has the form

$$
I_Q[A]
=-\frac{Z_Q}{4}
\int F_{ab}F^{ab}\sqrt{-g}\,d^4x
+\int A_aJ_Q^a\sqrt{-g}\,d^4x.
$$

This is not a new fundamental action. It is the quadratic restriction of the official Hessian to the physical mode, after the other blocks have been eliminated by the Schur complement. The coefficient $Z_Q$ is the norm inherited from the mode.

Topological terms, medium anisotropies, and higher orders may survive in other regimes, but they do not alter the Maxwell correspondence in the declared macroscopic sector.

Variing $A_b$,

$$
\delta F_{ab}
=\nabla_a\delta A_b-\nabla_b\delta A_a.
$$

By the antisymmetry of $F^{ab}$,

$$
\delta I_Q
=-Z_Q\int F^{ab}\nabla_a\delta A_b\sqrt{-g}\,d^4x
+\int J_Q^b\delta A_b\sqrt{-g}\,d^4x.
$$

Integrating by parts and vanishing the variational flux at the boundary,

$$
\delta I_Q
=\int
\left(
Z_Q\nabla_aF^{ab}+J_Q^b
\right)
\delta A_b\sqrt{-g}\,d^4x.
$$

With the corresponding sign convention for the current, we obtain

$$
\boxed{
\nabla_aF^{ab}=j^b,
\qquad
j^b=-\frac{J_Q^b}{Z_Q}.
}
$$

An opposite choice for the sign of the $A\cdot J_Q$ term redefines $j^b$ without changing the physical content.

## 4. Decomposition into electric and magnetic fields

On an approximately inertial local slice, we define

$$
E_i=F_{i0},
\qquad
B^i=\frac12\epsilon^{ijk}F_{jk}.
$$

The identity $dF=0$ yields

$$
\boxed{
\nabla\cdot\mathbf B=0,
\qquad
\nabla\times\mathbf E
=-\frac{\partial\mathbf B}{\partial t}.
}
$$

The equation $\nabla_aF^{ab}=j^b$, after restoring the units of the sector, yields

$$
\boxed{
\nabla\cdot\mathbf E=\frac{\rho_Q}{\varepsilon_0},
\qquad
\nabla\times\mathbf B
=\mu_0\mathbf J_Q
+\mu_0\varepsilon_0
\frac{\partial\mathbf E}{\partial t}.
}
$$

Thus, the four Maxwell equations are the $3+1$ decomposition of the Bianchi identity and the variational equation of the $U(1)_Q$ mode.

## 5. Geometric interpretation of the connection

The two parts of $F$ admit a mechanical-geometric reading:

- $\mathbf E$ is the longitudinal response associated with the compression, expansion, and phase gradient of the connection;
- $\mathbf B$ is the transverse response associated with circulation, shear, and vorticity.

This reading is compatible with

$$
\mathbf E=-\nabla\phi-\partial_t\mathbf A,
\qquad
\mathbf B=\nabla\times\mathbf A.
$$

It provides an interpretation of the connection, but does not replace the variational derivation above.

## 6. Conservation of charge

Applying $\nabla_b$ to the source equation,

$$
\nabla_bj^b
=\nabla_b\nabla_aF^{ab}=0,
$$

where the last equality follows from antisymmetry and geometric identities. Thus,

$$
\partial_t\rho_Q+\nabla\cdot\mathbf J_Q=0
$$

in the flat chart. The conservation required by Noether and the Maxwell equation are, therefore, compatible.

## 7. What remains for the normalization

The Maxwell form is established once the $U(1)_Q$ mode and its quadratic Hessian are transported. The value of

$$
Z_Q,
\qquad
\varepsilon_0,
\qquad
\mu_0,
\qquad
\alpha
$$

depends on the global norm, the Schur complement, and the synchronization of units. This evaluation should not be confused with the proof of the form of the equations.
