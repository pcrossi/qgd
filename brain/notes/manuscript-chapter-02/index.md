# Manuscript chapter 02

## Scope

This entry indexes the second manuscript chapter:

`manuscrito/02_geometrization/`

The chapter introduces the constructive hypothesis of GDQ: matter is to be
sought as a localized configuration of a dynamic geometry, not as a point
object added to a rigid background.

## Main chain

1. The metric participates in gradients, volume, and Laplacians; a rigid
   background is therefore a physical restriction, not a neutral absence.
2. The local bulk is fixed structurally as `R^4 x T^4`, giving real dimension
   eight and complex dimension four.
3. The geometry is Hermitian and may carry Bismut torsion; it is not generally
   Kähler strict.
4. The complex field `f` decomposes exactly into density and phase.
5. The weighted measure `U` follows the heat-kernel power in real dimension
   eight and is not an independent variable.
6. Perelman supplies a geometric grammar, not the physical action.
7. The official GDQ action is the dynamic axiom of the current theory.
8. A material soliton requires stationarity, finite relative action,
   localization, conserved class, physical Hessian stability, and spectral
   response.
9. Circulation, torsion, and charge are related by topology and interface
   dynamics, but are not synonyms.
10. Lorentzian spacetime is reconstructed by a clock one-form selected from
    cosmological simultaneity and causal orientation, not by simply pulling
    back the positive bulk metric.
11. Existence and stability of each material background remain sector-by-sector
    problems.

## Current status

The chapter fixes the geometric and dynamic language of GDQ. It contains one
core axiom, several definitions and identities, several conditional results,
and an explicit open problem: existence and stability of concrete material
backgrounds.
