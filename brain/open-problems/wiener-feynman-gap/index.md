# Wiener-Feynman structural gap

## Problem

The first chapter isolates the structural gap between:

- Wiener: positive probability measure on continuous non-differentiable paths;
- Feynman: complex oscillatory amplitude over paths.

The problem is not merely the factor `i` in the exponent. It involves
positivity, phase, interference, domains, boundary data, differentiability,
and causal reconstruction.

## Requirements for a GDQ solution

A complete GDQ solution must show:

1. how a positive diffusive sector arises;
2. how phase information and interference are preserved;
3. which domains, boundaries, and causal prescriptions connect the sectors;
4. when analytic continuation is reversible;
5. how physical unitary evolution is reconstructed;
6. how continuity and Hamilton-Jacobi arise from one complex field;
7. how non-differentiable microscopic paths produce regular mean fields;
8. whether universal diffusion and inertia are derived or only parametrized;
9. how torsion modifies geometric flow without destroying positivity and
   stability.

## Status

Open as the motivating problem of the theory. Later chapters are expected to
answer these requirements progressively.
